MME-41

Galois Theory -II and Algebraic Topology
M.Sc. Mathematics · Semester IV2025
  1. 1 Define covariant derivative of a vector field $\mathbf Y$ in the direction of a vector field $\mathbf X$. Examine whether the map $\bar\nabla_{\mathbf X}\mathbf Y=\mathbf X[f^i]\frac\partial{\partial x^i}$\unreadable{superscripts of $f$ and $x$ blurred} follows conditions for connection. Find $\bar\nabla_{\mathbf X}\mathbf Y$ for $\mathbf X=y\frac\partial{\partial x}+xy\frac\partial{\partial y}$ and $\mathbf Y=y^2\frac\partial{\partial x}+x^2y\frac\partial{\partial y}$. Write down the covariant derivatives of a frame field $\{\mathbf e_1,\mathbf e_2,\mathbf e_3\}$ in the direction of a vector field $\mathbf X$. Interpret the coefficients involve there. Define Christoffel symbols and find $\bar\nabla_{\mathbf e_i}\mathbf X$ for $\mathbf X=v^k\mathbf e_k$. [[6]]
  2. 2 Define the first and the second fundamental form on a surface in $\mathbb R^3$. Describe the length of a vector field $\mathbf X$ and the angle between two vector fields $\mathbf X$ and $\mathbf Y$ involving the metric tensors appearing in the first fundamental form of a surface in $\mathbb R^3$. Find the expression for the first and the second fundamental forms on a surface of revolution $\mathbf x(u,v)=(u\cos v,u\sin\ v,f(u))$. [[6]]
  3. 3 Introduce the frame $\{\mathbf T,\mathbf g,\mathbf n\}$. Prove that derivative of tangent of a curve on some surface in $\mathbb R^3$ lies in the plane of $\mathbf g$ and $\mathbf n$. Define normal curvature, geodesic curvature and the geodesic torsion of a curve on a surface. Prove that normal curvature of a surface is the ratio of second and first fundamental form on the surface. Find coefficient of second fundamental form in terms first and second derivatives of the position vector $\mathbf r(u,v)$ of the surface. Find coefficients of second fundamental form and normal curvature to a cylindrical surface of radius $a$. [[6]]
  4. 4 For two orthonormal frames $\{e_i\}$ and $\{\bar e_i\}$ related by an invertible transformation $\bar e_i=e_jB^j_i$, prove that their connections $\omega^i_j$ and $\bar\omega^i_j$ respectively, are related by the relation $\bar\omega=B^{-1}\omega B+B^{-1}dB$. If $\{\theta^j\}$ be the dual coframe of frame $\{e_i\}$ establish formulae a) $\Theta^i=d\theta^i+\omega^i_j\wedge\theta^j=0$, b) $\Omega=d\omega+\omega\wedge\omega=0$. [[6]]
  5. 5 Derive expressions for second derivatives of $\mathbf r(u,v)$ with respect to the parameters $u,v$ in terms of Christoffel symbols of the second kind. Establish the formula $\Gamma_{ijk}=\mathbf r_{ij}\cdot\mathbf r_k=\frac12(g_{ik;j}+g_{jk;i}-g_{ij;k})$. Obtain $\Gamma^i_{jk}(u,v)$ in terms of coefficients $E,F,G$ of first fundamental form that in curvilinear coordinate system $(u,v)$ on a surface in $\mathbb R^3$. Hence, recover the formulae $\Gamma^1_{11}=\frac{E_u}{2E}$, $\Gamma^2_{22}=\frac{G_v}{2G}$, $\Gamma^1_{12}=\Gamma^1_{21}=\frac{E_v}{2E}$, $\Gamma^2_{21}=\Gamma^2_{12}=\frac{G_u}{2G}$, $\Gamma^1_{22}=-\frac{G_u}{2E}$ and $\Gamma^2_{11}=-\frac{E_v}{2G}$. [[6]]
  6. 6 Derive the equation for geodesic on a surface in $\mathbb R^3$. Find the equation for geodesic on the cylindrical surface of unit radius $\mathbf r(u,v)=(\cos u,\sin u,v)$ and the sphere $\mathbf r(u,v)=(\sin u\cos v,\sin u\sin v,\cos u)$. [[6]]
  7. 7 Define the Riemann-Christoffel curvature and the Ricci tensor. Establish that both tensors reflect the intrinsic property of a surface in $\mathbb R^3$. Prove that Riemann-Christoffel curvature is antisymmetric concerning some indices that you must determine. Prove the Gauss theorem Egregium, i.e., Gauss curvature of a surface in $\mathbb R^3$ is an intrinsic property. [[6]]
  8. 8 Show that at every point on a smooth surface in $\mathbb R^3$ there exists two curves having optimal normal curvature $\kappa_n$. Hence classify points on a smooth surface. Define Gauss and mean curvature on a surface. Derive the relation among Gauss and mean curvature and coefficients of first and second fundamental forms. [[6]]
