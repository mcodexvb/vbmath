MMC-16

Integral Transforms and Integral Equations
M.Sc. Mathematics · Semester I2017
  1. 1 Answer \textit{any five} questions: \begin{romanparts} \item Find the Laplace transform of $f(t)$, where $f(t)=e^{-5t}\sinh 2t+e^{9t}\cos t+t^3e^{-7t}$. \item Solve the Fredholm integro-differential equation $u'(x)=x^5+\displaystyle\int_0^1tu(t)dt$, $u(0)=1$. \item What is the value of $g(5)$, where $g(x)=\sin x+\displaystyle\int_0^x\cos(x-t)g(t)dt$. \item Find the finite Fourier sine transform of $\sin ax$ in $0<x<\pi$. \item Convert the boundary value problem $y''+\lambda y=0$, $y(0)=1$, $y(1)=0$ into an integral equation. \item Find the inverse Laplace transform of $F(s)$, where $F(s)=\dfrac{se^{-2\pi s/3}}{s^2+1}$. \item Find the condition for which the integral equation $y(t)=f(t)+5\displaystyle\int_0^1tsy(s)ds$ has infinitely many solutions. \item Convert the integral equation $v(x)=1-x-4\sin x+\displaystyle\int_0^x(3-2(x-t))v(t)dt$ into a differential equation. \end{romanparts} [$5\times2=10$]
  2. 2(a) Using the Laplace transform method, solve the differential equation, $\dfrac{d^2y}{dt^2}+9y=\cos 2t$, $y(0)=1$, $y(\frac\pi2)=-1$. [4]
  3. 2(b) Prove that \[ L^{-1}\left\{\frac{P(s)}{Q(s)}\right\}= \sum_{k=1}^{n}\left(\frac{P(\alpha_k)}{Q'(\alpha_k)}\right)e^{\alpha_k t}, \] where $P(s)$ and $Q(s)$ are two polynomials with $\deg(P(s))<\deg(Q(s))$ and $\alpha_k$, $k=1,2,\ldots,n$ are $n$ distinct zeros of $Q(s)$. Hence find the inverse Laplace transform of \[ \dfrac{2s^2+5s-4}{s^3+s^2-2s}. \] [5]
  4. 2(c) Give an example of a function which is not of exponential order but whose Laplace transform exists. [1]
  5. 3(a) Find the Fourier cosine transform of $\dfrac1{1+x^2}$. Hence find the Fourier sine transform of $\dfrac{x}{1+x^2}$. \hfill\textbf{P.T.O.} [4]
  6. 3(b) Find the Fourier transform of $f(x)$, where $f(x)=\begin{cases}1,&|x|<\alpha\\0,&|x|>\alpha.\end{cases}$ [2]
  7. 3(c) Using Parseval's identity, Prove that \[ \int_0^\infty\frac{\sin\alpha t}{t(\alpha^2+t^2)}dt =\frac\pi2\left(\frac{1-e^{-\alpha^2}}{\alpha^2}\right). \] [3]
  8. 3(d) If $f(t)=\begin{cases}e^{-xt}\varphi(t)&t>0\\0,&t<0\end{cases}$, then prove that $F\{f(t)\}=L\{\varphi(t)\}$. [1]
  9. 4(a) Using Fredholm alternative theorem, find the conditions for which the integral equation $y(x)=f(x)+\lambda\displaystyle\int_0^{\pi/2}\cos(x+t)y(t)dt$ has infinite number of solutions. [5]
  10. 4(b) With the help of resolvent kernel solve the Volterra integral equation $y(x)=1+x^2+\displaystyle\int_0^x\frac{1+x^2}{1+t^2}y(t)dt$. [5]
  11. 5(a) Solve the first kind Volterra integral equation $\displaystyle\int_0^x(2+x^2-t^2)y(t)dt=x^2$. [3]
  12. 5(b) With the help of Hilbert-Schmidt theorem, solve the integral equation \[ y(x)=(x+1)^2+\int_{-1}^1(xt+x^2t^2)y(t)dt. \] [5]
  13. 5(c) Solve the integral equation $u(x)=x\sin x-\frac12+\frac12\displaystyle\int_0^{\pi/2}u(t)dt$ by using modified Adomian decomposition method. [2]
  14. 6(a) If $f(k)$ and $g(k)$ are Fourier transforms of $F(x)$ and $G(x)$ respectively, then prove that \[ \int_{-\infty}^\infty f(x)\overline{g(k)}dk=\int_{-\infty}^\infty F(x)\overline{G(x)}dx. \] [2.5]
  15. 6(b) If $f(t)$ and $g(t)$ are piecewise continuous on $[0,\infty)$ and of exponential order, then prove that $L\{f*g\}=L\{f\}.L\{g\}$. [2.5]
  16. 6(c) Prove that the resolvent kernel $R(x,t;\lambda)$ of the integral equation \[ y(x)=f(x)+\lambda\displaystyle\int_a^b k(x,t)y(t)dt, \] satisfies the integral equation \[ R(x,t;\lambda)=k(x,t)+\lambda\int_a^b k(x,z)R(z,t;\lambda)dz. \] [3]
  17. 6(d) If $L\{F(t)\}=f(s)$, then prove that $L\{\frac{F(t)}{t}\}=\displaystyle\int_s^\infty f(x)dx$. [2]
