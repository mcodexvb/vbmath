MJMA-1

Algebra-I and Analysis I
B.Sc. Mathematics · Semester I2024
  1. 1(a) Find the inverse of an element $\begin{pmatrix}2&6\\3&5\end{pmatrix}$ in $GL(2,\mathbb Z_{11})$. [[3]]
  2. 1(b) For any integer $n\ge2$, show that there are at least two elements in $U_n$. [[3]]
  3. 1(c) Show that a group $G$ is Abelian if and only if $(ab)^{-1}=a^{-1}b^{-1}$ for every $a,b\in G$. [[2+2]]
  4. 2(a) Find a group that contains elements $a$ and $b$ such that $O(a)=O(b)=2$ and $O(ab)=2$. [[2]]
  5. 2(b) Let $G$ be a group and $a\in G$ is an element of order $n$. If $k$ divides $n$, show that $O(a^{\frac nk})=k$. [[4]]
  6. 2(c) Suppose $G$ is a group that has exactly eight elements of order 3. How many subgroups of order 3 are there in $G$? [[4]]
  7. 3(a) Let $a$ be the only element of order 2 in a group $G$. Then show that $a\in Z(G)$. [[3]]
  8. 3(b) Show that the set $H=\{A\in GL(2,\mathbb R)\mid\det A\text{ is a power of }2\}$ is a subgroup of $GL(2,\mathbb R)$. [[3]]
  9. 3(c) List all subgroups of $D_4$. [[4]]
  10. 4(a) Give an example of a non cyclic group, all of whose proper subgroups are cyclic. Justify your answer. [[3]]
  11. 4(b) Prove that a group $G$ of order $n$ is cyclic if and only of there is an element of order $n$ in $G$. [[2+2]]
  12. 4(c) Suppose that a cyclic group $G$ has exactly three subgroups: $G$ itself, $\{e\}$, and a subgroup of order 7. Find the order of $G$. [[3]]
  13. 5(a) Show that the number of all even permutations on a finite set is equal to the number of all odd permutations. [[4]]
  14. 5(b) Find $\beta\in S_7$ be such that $\beta^4=(2143567)$. [[3]]
  15. 5(c) How many odd permutations of order 4 are there in $S_6$? [[3]]
  16. 6(a) Show that every element in $A_n$ is either a 3-cycle or a product of 3-cycles. [[4]]
  17. 6(b) Find a conjugate element of $\begin{pmatrix}1&2&3&4&5&6&7&8\\2&1&4&5&4&7&8&6\end{pmatrix}$ in $S_8$ [[3]]
  18. 6(c) How many proper subgroups are there in a cyclic group of order 100? Justify your answer. [[3]]
  19. 7(a) Prove the following results :\\(i) If $z$ and $a$ are elements in $\mathbb R$ with $z+a=a$ then $z=0$.\\(ii) If $u$ and $b(\ne0)$ are elements in $\mathbb R$ with $u\cdot b=b$ then $u=1$.\\(iii) If $a\in\mathbb R$ then $a\cdot0=0$. [[5]]
  20. 7(b) Prove the following results:\\(i) If $a\in\mathbb R$ and $a\ne0$ then $a^2>0$.\\(ii) If $a\in\mathbb R$ and $a>0$ then $-a<0$. [[5]]
  21. 8(a) For $x\in\mathbb R$ and $x>0$, prove the following results :\\(i) There exists a natural number $n$ such that $0<\frac1n<x$.\\(ii) There exists a natural number $m$ such that $m-1\le x<m$. [[4]]
  22. 8(b) Show that countable union of countable sets is countable. [[6]]
  23. 9(a) Let $\{x_n\}$, $\{y_n\}$ and $\{z_n\}$ be three sequences such that $x_n\le y_n\le z_n$ $\forall n\in\mathbb N$. If $x_n\to l$ and $z_n\to l$ then prove that $\{y_n\}$ is convergent and converges to $l$. [[4]]
  24. 9(b) Show that $\lim_{n\to\infty}\left\{\frac1{(n+1)^2}+\frac1{(n+2)^2}+\dots+\frac1{(n+n)^2}\right\}=0$. [[4]]
  25. 9(c) Evaluate $\lim_{n\to\infty}(\sqrt{n+1}-\sqrt n)$. [[2]]
  26. 10(a) Define limit point of a set. Show that 0 is a limit point of the set $\{1,\frac12,\frac13,\dots\}$ [[3]]
  27. 10(b) Let $S\subset\mathbb R$ and $x$ be a limit point of $S$. Prove that every neighborhood of $x$ contains infinitely many points of $S$. [[3]]
  28. 10(c) Give the $(\epsilon-\delta)$ definition of limit of a function and hence evaluate $\lim_{x\to2}f(x)$ where $f(x)=x^3-4x^2+3x+2$, $x\in[1,4]$. [[4]]
  29. 11(a) When is a sequence said to be convergent ? Prove that every convergent sequence is bounded. Does the converse hold ? Justify your answer. [[5]]
  30. 11(b) Evaluate : $\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(1+\frac1n\right)^n$, $n\in\mathbb N$. [[5]]
  31. 12(a) State and prove Cauchy's First Limit Theorem. [[5]]
  32. 12(b) Evaluate : $\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac1n\{(2n+1)(2n+2)\dots(2n+n)\}^{\frac1n}$ \fbox{\parbox{3.4cm}{\small Use separate answer script for each unit}} [[5]]
