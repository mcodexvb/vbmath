MMO-41

Advanced Real Analysis-II
M.Sc. Mathematics · Semester IV2017
  1. 1(a) Define $\sigma$- algebra? Give an example of a nontrivial $\sigma$- algebra. [3]
  2. 1(b) Let $(X,\ \mathcal A,\ \mu)$ be a measure space. If $A,B\in\mathcal A,$ with $B\subset A,$ show that\par \hspace*{1em}i) $\mu(B)\le\mu(A)$\par \hspace*{1em}ii) if $\mu(B)<\infty$ then $\mu(A-B)=\mu(A)-\mu(B)$ [2]
  3. 1(c) When is a sequence of sets called convergent? Prove that a monotone increasing sequence of sets is convergent and converges to its supremum. [5]
  4. 1(d) Consider the measure space $(\mathbb N,\mathcal P(\mathbb N),\mu)$ where $\mu$ is counting measure. Let $A_n=\{n,\,n+1,\,n+2,\ldots\}$. Show that $\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\mu(A_n)\neq\mu\left(\bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty}A_n\right).$ [2]
  5. 2(a) Let $\mu^*$ be an outer measure on a set $X$. Let $\mathcal M$ be the class of all $\mu^*$- measurable sets. Show that $\mathcal M$ is an $\sigma$- algebra and $\mu^*$- restricted to $\mathcal M$ is a measure. [6]
  6. 2(b) When is an outer measure $\mu^*$ on $X$ said to be regular? Let $\mu^*$ be a regular outer measure on $X$ and let $\mu^*(X)<\infty$. Prove that a necessary and sufficient condition that a set $A\subset X$ be measurable is $\mu^*(X)=\mu^*(A)+\mu^*(X-A)$. [6]
  7. 3(a) Show that there is an outer measure on $\mathbb R^2$, constructed from premeasure such that under which open sets in $\mathbb R^2$ need not be measurable. [4]
  8. 3(b) When is a measure $(X,\ \mathcal M,\ \mu)$ said to be complete? Show that there is a complete measure space $(X,\ \bar{\mathcal M},\ \bar\mu)$ such that $\mathcal M\subset\bar{\mathcal M}$ and $\bar\mu$ is extension of $\mu$ on $\bar{\mathcal M}.$ [6]
  9. 3(c) Show that the Lebesgue measure is complete. [2]
  10. 4(a) What is simple function. If $f$ is nonnegative measurable function then show that there is a sequence of nondecreasing simple function which converges to $f$. [1+7]
  11. 4(b) Show that sum and product of two simple functions are simple. [4]
  12. 5(a) If $f$ is integrable on $E$ and $F\subset E,$ then show that $f$ is integrable on $F$. Moreover if $f$ is nonnegative on $E$ then show that $\displaystyle\int_F f\le\int_E f.$ [5]
  13. 5(b) If $f$ is integrable on $E$ and $c$ is constant, show that $cf$ is integrable on $E$ and $\displaystyle\int_E cf=c\int_E f.$ [5]
  14. 5(c) If $f$ is integrable on $E$ and $f=g$ a.e on $E$ then show that $\displaystyle\int_E f=\int_E g.$ \par\hfill P.T.O. \begin{center}( 2 )\end{center} [2]
  15. 6 Answer \textbf{\textit{any two}}:
  16. 6(a) If $f$ be an integrable function defined on a measure space $(X,\ \mathcal M,\ \mu)$ such that $\displaystyle\int_E f\,d\mu=0$ for every $E\in\mathcal M,$ then show that $f=0$ a.e in $X$.
  17. 6(b) Let $(X,\ \mathcal M,\ \mu)$ be a measure space and let $E\in\mathcal M.$ If $\{f_n\}$ and $\{g_n\}$ be sequences of finite a.e, measurable functions on $E$ such that $f_n\to f\,[\mathrm{meas}]$ and $g_n\to g\,[\mathrm{meas}]$ on $E$, show that $f_n+g_n\to f+g\,[\mathrm{meas}]$ on $E$.
  18. 6(c) If $\mu(\mathrm E)=0$ then show that for any measurable function $f,\ \displaystyle\int_E f=0.$
