MMC-35

Galois Theory-I and Multivariable Analysis
M.Sc. Mathematics · Semester III2025
  1. 1(a) Show that $\mathbb Q(\sqrt[5]3)=\mathbb Q(\sqrt[5]9)$. [3]
  2. 1(b) How many intermediate fields are there of a field extension of degree 97. [2]
  3. 2(a) Let $F$ be a field of characteristic $p$, where $p$ is a prime. Let $\alpha$ be a root of $x^p-x-a$ over $F$. Find the spliting field of $x^p-x-a$ over $F$. [2]
  4. 2(b) Let $F/K$ be a field extension and $R$ be a ring such that $K\subseteq R\subseteq F$. Show that if every element of $R$ is algebraic over $K$, then $R$ is a field. [3]
  5. 3(a) Let $F$ is field extension of field $K$. Show that $c\in F$ is transcendental over $K$ if and only if $K(x)\cong K(c)$. [3]
  6. 3(b) \textit{Printed, struck through:} \sout{Let $F/K$ be a field extension and $R$ be a ring such that $K\subseteq R\subseteq F$. Show that if every element of $R$ is algebraic over $K$, then $R$ is a field.}\par \textit{Handwritten replacement:} Find the splitting field of the polynomial of $x^2+1$ over $\mathbb Z_2$. [2]
  7. 4(a) Describe the elements of the field $\mathbb Q(\sqrt2,\sqrt3)$. Justify your answer. [2]
  8. 4(b) \textit{Printed, struck through:} \sout{Find a splitting field of the polynomial $x^2+x+1$ oveer $\mathbb Z_5$.}\par \textit{Handwritten replacement:} If $F$ is an algebraic extension of an algebraic closed field $K$, then show that $F=K$. [3]
  9. 5(a) Construct an example of a inseparable polynomial over a field with prime charateristic. Justify your answer. [2]
  10. 5(b) Prove or disprove that $\mathbb Q(\sqrt[4]2,i)$ is a normal extension of $\mathbb Q$. [3]
  11. 6(a) Let $F$ be a finite field with $p$. Show that the mapping $\phi:F\to F$ defined by $\phi(a)=a^p$ is a $\mathbb Z_p$-automorphism. [3]
  12. 6(b) For characteristic $p$, find a simple extension of $\mathbb Z_p$. Justify your answer. [2]
  13. 1(a) Show that $f:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^m$ is differentiable at $x_0\in\mathbb R^n$, if and only if the component functions are differentiable at $x_0$ [4]
  14. 1(b) Suppose $f:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^m$ is such that for some matrix $T$,\par \[\lim_{x\to a}\frac{\|f(x)-f(a)-T(x-a)\|}{\|x-a\|}=0.\] Show that $T$ is the matrix of partial derivatives of $f$ at $a$. [4]
  15. 1(c) Compute $f^\prime(0,0,0)$ for the function $f:\mathbb R^3\to\mathbb R^5$\par \[f(x,y,z)=(\sin zx,\ \cos zy,\ \sin y,\ \cos x,\ e^{xyz}).\] [2]
  16. 2(a) Suppose $f:A\to\mathbb R^m$ has $(k+1)$th order continuous partial derivatives at $x_0\in A$, where $A\subset\mathbb R^n$ is an open set and $R_k(x)$ is the $k$th degree remainder of $f$ at $x_0$. Show that\par \[\lim_{x\to x_0}\frac{\|R_k(x)\|}{\|x-x_0\|^k}=0.\] [3]
  17. 2(b) For $a\in\mathbb R$, consider $f_a(x,y)=x^4+y^4-ax^2y^2$. Find all values of $a$ for which $(0,0)$ is a local minimum of $f_a$. [2]
  18. 2(c) State and prove Mean value theorem for the function $f:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R$ [3]
  19. 2(d) Let $f(x,y)=xy$, using Taylor's theorem expand $f(x,y)$ in powers of $(x-1)$ and $(y-1)$ [2]
  20. 3(a) Let $T:\mathbb R^2\to\mathbb R^2$ be $T(x,y)=\left(x^2-y^2,2xy\right)$. Find all points $(x,y)$ at which the Inverse Function Theorem guarantees a $C^1$ local inverse of $T$. [3]
  21. 3(b) Let $f(x,y)=x^2\sin y-4x$, show that the point $(2,\pi)$ is neither a local maximum nor a local minimum of $f$. [2]
  22. 3(c) Suppose $f:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^m$ is $C^1$ and $\det(f^\prime(a))\ne0$ for all $a\in\mathbb R^n$ then show that $f$ is an open map. [5]
