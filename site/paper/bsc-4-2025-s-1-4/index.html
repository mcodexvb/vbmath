MJMA-10

B.Sc. Mathematics · Semester IV2025
  1. 1(a) State the axioms of mathematical probability. [3]
  2. 1(b) If $\{A_n\}$ is a monotonic non-decreasing sequence of events, then prove that \[P\left(\lim_{n\to\infty}A_n\right)=\lim_{n\to\infty}P(A_n).\] [7]
  3. 2(a) Prove the general addition rule of probability for any two events $A_1$ and $A_2$. [3]
  4. 2(b) A card is drawn at random from each of two well-shuffled packs of cards. What is the probability that at least one of them is queen of spades? [7]
  5. 3(a) State Bayes' theorem. [3]
  6. 3(b) Two urns contain respectively 2 white and 1 black balls, and 1 white and 5 black balls. One ball is transferred from the first to the second urn, and then a ball is drawn from the second urn. What is the probability that the ball drawn is white? [7]
  7. 4(a) Define the distribution function $F(x)$ of a random variable $X$. Prove that for any $a<b$, $F(a)\le F(b)$. [2+3]
  8. 4(b) Let the spectrum of the random variable $X$ be $\{1,2,\ldots,n\}$ and $P(X=i)\propto 1/i(i+1)$. Determine the distribution function of $X$ and compute $P(3<X\le n)$. [3+2]
  9. 5(a) If a random variable $X$ is uniformly distributed over the interval $(0,\pi/2)$, compute the expectation of the function $g(x)=\sin X$. [3]
  10. 5(b) A continuous random variable $X$ has probability density $f(x)=\beta e^{-\beta x}$ with $0<x<\infty$ and $\beta>0$. Calculate the moment generating function of $X$, and hence obtain the $k$-th order moment of $X$ about the origin $(\alpha_k)$. [2+5]
  11. 6(a) If $\rho(X,Y)$ denotes the correlation coefficient of two random variables $X$ and $Y$, prove that $-1\le\rho(X,Y)\le1$. [3]
  12. 6(b) State Tchebycheff's inequality. Using it show that in 2000 throws with a coin, the probability that the number of heads lies between 900 and 1100 is at least 19/20. [2+5]
  13. 7 Define a population and a random sample. What is meant by the distribution of a random sample? Show that if the size of the sample is large, the distribution of the sample is the statistical image of the distribution of the population. [2+2+6]
  14. 8 Define an estimate of a population parameter. When is an estimate said to be consistent and unbiased? For a normal $(m,\sigma)$ population, show that the sample variance $S^2$ is a consistent but biased estimate of $\sigma^2$. Obtain an estimate of $\sigma^2$ that is both consistent and unbiased.
  15. 9(a) Define a \emph{statistic}. Find the sampling distribution of the mean for the binomial $(N,p)$ population. [1+4]
  16. 9(b) Estimate the parameter $\alpha$ of a continuous population having the probability density function $f(x)=(1+\alpha)x^\alpha$ $(0<x<1)$ by the method of maximum likelihood. [5]
  17. 10(a) Define a confidence interval of a population parameter. What is confidence coefficient? [2+1]
  18. 10(b) The population of scores of 10-year old children in a psychological performance test is known to have a standard deviation 5.2. If a random sample of size 20 shows a mean of 16.9, find 95\% confidence limits for the mean score of the population, assuming that the population is normal. Given $P(U>1.96)=0.025$, where $U$ is a standard normal variate. [7]
  19. 11(a) Define (i) Simple hypothesis, (ii) Composite hypothesis, (iii) Null hypothesis, and (iv) Alternative hypothesis, and give examples. [2+2+2+2]
  20. 11(b) What are meant by the rejection and acceptance regions of testing a statistical hypothesis? [2]
  21. 12(a) Define two types of errors in testing a statistical hypothesis. [2+2]
  22. 12(b) Let $p$ be the probability that a coin will fall head in a single toss in order to test the null hypothesis $H_0:p=1/2$ against the alternative $H_1:p=3/4$. The coin is tossed 5 times and $H_0$ is rejected if more than 3 heads are obtained. Find the probabilities of two types of errors. [3+3]
