TIFR Graduate School · GS 2025 · Mathematics PhD at CAM

  1. 1 For $0 < a < 1$, let $\lambda = \frac{1}{a} + ai$ and \[ z_n = \int_{\mathbb{R}} e^{inx} e^{-\lambda x^2}\,dx. \] What is $\lim_{n \to \infty} z_n$? \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)] \item $a$ \item $\infty$ \item $0$ \item $1$ \end{enumerate}
  2. 2 Let $\mathbb{D} = \{(x, y) \in \mathbb{R}^2 : x^2 + y^2 < 1\}$. For $p \in \mathbb{R}$, define \[ C_p = \int_{\mathbb{D}} \frac{\sin^2(x^2 + y^2)}{(x^2 + y^2)^p}\,dx\,dy. \] Choose the correct option. \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)] \item $C_p < \infty$ for all $p > 0$. \item $C_p < \infty$ for all $p > 1$. \item $C_p < \infty$ for all $p < 3$. \item $C_p < \infty$ for all $p < 4$. \end{enumerate}
  3. 3 Let $p(x) = x^3 - x^2 - x + 1$. Consider the following statements. \begin{enumerate}[label=(\Roman*)] \item If $T: \mathbb{R}^3 \to \mathbb{R}^3$ is a linear transformation and $p(T) = 0$, then $T$ is invertible. \item If $T: \mathbb{R}^3 \to \mathbb{R}^3$ is a linear transformation and $p(T) = 0$, then $T$ is diagonalizable. \end{enumerate} Choose the correct option. \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)] \item (I) and (II) are both true. \item (I) and (II) are both false. \item (I) is true and (II) is false. \item (I) is false and (II) is true. \end{enumerate}
  4. 4 What is the cardinality of $\{A \subseteq \mathbb{R} : \mathbb{R} \setminus \overline{A} = \overline{\mathbb{R} \setminus A}\}$? \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)] \item $0$ \item $1$ \item $2$ \item infinite \end{enumerate}
  5. 5 Let $y \in C^2([0,1])$ be a non identically zero solution to \[ \frac{d^2 y}{dt^2}(t) + (e^{t^2} \sin t) y(t) = 0, \qquad t \in (0,1). \] Choose the correct option. \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)] \item $y$ can have infinitely many zeroes. \item Either $y$ has no zeroes or finitely many zeroes. \item The third derivative of $y$ does not exist at $1/2$. \item The equation has a unique non identically zero solution. \end{enumerate}
  6. 6 Let $f$ be an entire function which satisfies $|f(z)| \ge |z|^2$ for all $z \in \mathbb{C}$ and assume moreover that $f(i) = -i$. Then, \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)] \item $f(0) \ne 0$ \item $f(1) = -3$ \item $f(2)$ cannot be uniquely determined from the given information. \item $f(3) = 9i$ \end{enumerate}
  7. 7 Let $\mathbb{H} = \{x + iy : x \in \mathbb{R}, \, y > 0\}$ and $f: \mathbb{H} \to \mathbb{H}$ be a bijective holomorphic function such that $\lim_{|z| \to \infty} |f(z)| = \infty$. Choose the correct option. \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)] \item $f$ is a linear function. \item $f$ is a rational function that is not a polynomial. \item $f(\{it : t > 0\})$ is a subset of a circle of finite radius. \item $f(\{it : t > 0\})$ is a subset of a horizontal line. \end{enumerate}
  8. 8 Let $f: \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ be defined by $f(y) = y \sin(1/y)$ for $y \ne 0$, and $f(0) = 0$. Then, the initial value problem \[ \begin{cases} \frac{dy}{dt}(t) = f(y(t)), & t \in \mathbb{R}, \\ y(0) = 0, \end{cases} \] has \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)] \item at least one strictly monotonic solution. \item a unique solution; which is $y \equiv 0$. \item infinitely many solutions. \item at least one oscillatory solution (i.e., the solution is not identically zero and has infinitely many zeroes). \end{enumerate}
  9. 9 Choose the correct option. For any real Banach space $X$ and any linear operator $T: X \to X^*$, if $\langle Tx, y \rangle = \langle Ty, x \rangle$ for all $x, y \in X$, then \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)] \item $T$ is invertible. \item $T$ is surjective. \item $T$ is bounded. \item $T = 0$. \end{enumerate}
  10. 10 Choose the correct option. \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)] \item If $f: \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ is a bijective differentiable map then its inverse is also a bijective differentiable map. \item If $f: \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ is a bijective differentiable map then for all $x \in \mathbb{R}$ there exists an $\epsilon > 0$ and a differentiable map $g: (x - \epsilon, x + \epsilon) \to \mathbb{R}$ such that $f \circ g(t) = t$ for all $t \in (x - \epsilon, x + \epsilon)$. \item If $f: \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ is a bijective differentiable map then $f$ is either monotonically increasing or monotonically decreasing. \item If $f: \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ is a bijective differentiable map then $f'(x) > 0$ for all $x \in \mathbb{R}$ or $f'(x) < 0$ for all $x \in \mathbb{R}$. \end{enumerate}
  11. 11 Let $f: \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ be a continuous function satisfying the following condition: for each $x_0$ and for each $r > 0$, \[ f(x_0) = \frac{1}{r} \int_{x_0 - r}^{x_0 + r} f(y)\,dy. \] Choose the correct statement. \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)] \item There exists a non constant function $f \in L^2(\mathbb{R})$ satisfying the above property. \item There exists at most one function in $L^2(\mathbb{R})$ that satisfies the above property. \item The space of functions $f \in L^2(\mathbb{R})$ and satisfying the above property is one dimensional. \item The space of functions $f \in L^2(\mathbb{R})$ and satisfying the above property is infinite dimensional. \end{enumerate}
  12. 12 Consider the $11 \times 11$ matrix: \[ A = \begin{pmatrix} -2 & \frac{1}{1^2} & -\frac{1}{2^2} & \frac{1}{3^2} & \cdots & -\frac{1}{10^2} \\[4pt] \frac{1}{11^2} & -2 & -\frac{1}{12^2} & \frac{1}{13^2} & \cdots & -\frac{1}{20^2} \\[4pt] \frac{1}{21^2} & -\frac{1}{22^2} & -2 & \frac{1}{23^2} & \cdots & -\frac{1}{30^2} \\[4pt] \vdots & \vdots & \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\[4pt] \frac{1}{101^2} & \cdots & \cdots & \cdots & -\frac{1}{110^2} & -2 \end{pmatrix}. \] Consider the following statements. \begin{enumerate}[label=(\Roman*)] \item The eigenvalues of $A$ are all non-negative. \item $A$ is invertible. \end{enumerate} Choose the correct option. \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)] \item (I) and (II) are both true. \item (I) and (II) are both false. \item (I) is true and (II) is false. \item (I) is false and (II) is true. \end{enumerate}
  13. 13 For sets $A, B \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n$, define $A + B = \{a + b : a \in A \text{ and } b \in B\}$. Choose the correct option. \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)] \item If $A$ is compact and $B$ is closed, then $A + B$ is compact. \item If $A$ is closed and $B$ is closed, then $A + B$ is closed. \item If $A$ is open and $B$ is compact, then $A + B$ is open. \item If $A$ is open and $B$ is compact, then $A + B$ is compact. \end{enumerate}
  14. 14 Choose the correct option. If $g: \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ is a positive continuous function such that $\int_0^\infty e^{\alpha x} g(x)\,dx < \infty$ for some $\alpha > 0$, then \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)] \item $\int_0^\infty \frac{g(x)}{x^n}\,dx < \infty$ for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$. \item $\lim_{x \to \infty} g(x)$ exists. \item $\int_0^\infty \frac{1}{g^\beta(x)}\,dx < \infty$ for some $\beta > 0$. \item there exist $C > 0$ and $\beta > 0$ such that $\int_t^\infty g(x)\,dx \le C e^{-\beta t}$, for all $t > 0$. \end{enumerate}
  15. 15 Let $f_n: \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ be a sequence of differentiable functions such that \[ \int_0^1 f_n(x)\,dx = 0 \quad \text{and} \quad |f_n'(x)| \le x^{-1/2} \quad \text{for all } x \in (0,1]. \] Consider the following statements. \begin{enumerate}[label=(\Roman*)] \item $\{f_n\}$ is uniformly bounded on $[0,1]$. \item $\{f_n\}$ has a subsequence that converges uniformly on $[0,1]$. \end{enumerate} Choose the correct option. \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)] \item (I) and (II) are both true. \item (I) and (II) are both false. \item (I) is true and (II) is false. \item (I) is false and (II) is true. \end{enumerate}
  16. 16 Let $n \ge 2$, and $A$ be an $n \times n$ real symmetric and positive definite matrix satisfying $\operatorname{trace}(A) = \det(A)$. Choose the correct statement that holds for every such $A$. \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)] \item $\operatorname{trace}(A) < n^{\frac{n}{n-1}}$. \item $\operatorname{trace}(A) \le n$. \item $\det(A) \ge n^{\frac{n}{n-1}}$. \item all of the eigenvalues of $A$ are $\le \frac{1}{2}$. \end{enumerate}
  17. 17 A box contains $2024$ balls in which $200$ are blue and the rest are red. Balls are chosen from the box one by one at random, and discarded. Consider the following statements. \begin{enumerate}[label=(\Roman*)] \item The probability of picking a red ball at the $k$-th pick is the same for all $k = 1, \dots, 2024$. \item The probability of picking a blue ball at the $200$-th pick is the same as the probability of picking a red ball at the $1824$-th pick. \item The probability of picking all the blue balls in the first $200$ draws is the same as the probability of picking all the red balls in the first $1824$ draws. \end{enumerate} Choose the correct option. \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)] \item (I) and (II) are true, (III) is false. \item (I) and (III) are true, (II) is false. \item (II) and (III) are true, (I) is false. \item (I), (II), and (III) are all true. \end{enumerate}
  18. 18 Let $X$ denote the inner product space $(C([0,1]), \langle \cdot, \cdot \rangle)$ where $\langle f, g \rangle = \int_0^1 f(x) g(x)\,dx$. Define $T: X \to X$ by $(Tf)(x) = \int_x^1 f(t)\,dt$ for $f \in X$ and $x \in [0,1]$. Choose the correct statement. \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)] \item $T$ is surjective. \item There exists a bounded linear operator $U: X \to X$ such that $U \circ T$ is the identity map. \item $T(X)$ is dense in $X$. \item $T(X)$ is a closed subset of $X$. \end{enumerate}
  19. 19 Let $T: L^2(0,1) \to L^2(0,1)$ be defined by $(Tf)(x) = f(\sqrt{x})$. We denote the operator norm of $T$ by $\|T\|$. Choose the correct option. \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)] \item $\|T\| = \sqrt{2}$. \item $\|T\| = 2$. \item $\|T\| = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$. \item $\|T\| = \frac{1}{2}$. \end{enumerate}
  20. 20 Let $f: \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ be defined by \[ f(x) = \begin{cases} x^{-1/2}, & \text{if } 0 < x < 1, \text{ and} \\ 0, & \text{otherwise.} \end{cases} \] Let $r_n$ be an enumeration of rational numbers and correspondingly define \[ h(x) = \sum_{n=1}^\infty 2^{-n} f(x - r_n), \qquad \text{for } x \in \mathbb{R}. \] Choose the correct statement. \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)] \item $h \in L^1(\mathbb{R})$ and $h \in L^2(\mathbb{R})$. \item $h \in L^1(\mathbb{R})$ and $h \notin L^2(\mathbb{R})$. \item $h \notin L^1(\mathbb{R})$ and $h \in L^2(\mathbb{R})$. \item $h \notin L^1(\mathbb{R})$ and $h \notin L^2(\mathbb{R})$. \end{enumerate}
