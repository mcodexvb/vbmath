MJMA-7

Algebra-III
B.Sc. Mathematics · Semester IV2025
  1. 1 Answer \emph{any ten} questions. Provide a clear and complete justification for each answer whenever necessary. Each question carries 2 marks. $(10\times2=20)$
  2. 1(a) Give an example to show that a subfield $K$ in a field $F$ may not be a vector space over $F$.
  3. 1(b) Is $W=\{(a_1,a_2,0):a_1,a_2\in\mathbb R\}$ isomorphic to $\mathbb R^2$?
  4. 1(c) Determine whether the set $\{x^3-x,\,2x^2+4,\,-2x^3+3x^2+2x+6\}$ is linearly dependent or independent in $\mathcal P_3(\mathbb R)$:
  5. 1(d) Give an example of a vector space having 121 elements.
  6. 1(e) Is the vector space of real numbers $\mathbb R$ over the field of rational numbers $\mathbb Q$ finite-dimensional?
  7. 1(f) Find the row rank of the matrix $\begin{pmatrix}10&0&-8\\2&-4&3\\-5&7&6\end{pmatrix}$.
  8. 1(g) Prove or disprove: If a linear transformation $T:V\to W$ is one-to-one, then the only vector $x\in V$ such that $T(x)=\theta_W$ is $x=\theta_V$.
  9. 1(h) Check whether the function $T:\mathbb R^2\to\mathbb R^2$ defined by $T(a_1,a_2)=(|a_1|,a_2)$ is linear or not.
  10. 1(i) Find $T(a,b)$, where $T:\mathbb R^2\to\mathbb R^2$ represents the projection onto the $y$-axis along the $x$-axis.
  11. 1(j) Let $V$ be a vector space with the ordered basis $\beta=\{v_1,v_2,\ldots,v_n\}$. Define $v_0=\theta_V$, and let $T:V\to V$ be a linear transformation such that $T(v_j)=v_j+v_{j-1}$, for $j=1,2,\ldots,n$. Find the matrix representation $[T]_\beta$.
  12. 1(k) What is the necessary condition on $m$ and $n$ such that the vector spaces $\mathcal P_n(F)$ and $\mathcal P_m(F)$ are isomorphic, where $F$ is a field?
  13. 1(l) Does there exist a singular change-of-basis matrix from one ordered basis $B_1$ to another ordered basis $B_2$ in a vector space?
  14. 1(m) Can a finite-dimensional vector space have a dual space of a different dimension?
  15. 1(n) Let $\beta=\{(2,1),(3,1)\}$ be an ordered basis for $\mathbb R^2$. Find two linear functionals $f_1$ and $f_2$ that form a minimal generating set for the dual space $(\mathbb R^2)^*$.
  16. 1(o) Can the row vectors of a nonsingular matrix $A$ form a basis for the row space of $A$?
  17. 2(a) Let $V=\{(a_1,a_2):a_1,a_2\in\mathbb R\}$. For $(a_1,a_2),(b_1,b_2)\in V$ and $c\in\mathbb R$, define \[(a_1,a_2)+(b_1,b_2)=(a_1+2b_1,a_2+3b_2)\quad\text{and}\quad c(a_1,a_2)=(ca_1,ca_2).\] Is $V$ a vector space over $\mathbb R$ with these operations? Justify your answer. [3]
  18. 2(b) Prove that $W_1=\{(a_1,a_2,\ldots,a_n)\in\mathbb F^n:a_1+a_2+\cdots+a_n=0\}$ is a subspace and $W_1=\{(a_1,a_2,\ldots,a_n)\in\mathbb F^n:a_1+a_2+\cdots+a_n=1\}$ is not a subspace. [3+2]
  19. 2(c) Let $W$ be a subspace of a vector space $V$ over a field $\mathbb F$. Prove that the set $S=\{v+W:v\in V\}$ with respect to addition and scalar multiplication by scalars of $F$ given by $(v_1+W)+(v_2+W)=(v_1+v_2+W)$ and $a(v_1+W)=av_1+W$ for all $a\in\mathbb F$ is a vector space over $F$. [7]
  20. 3(a) Give an example of three linearly dependent vectors in $R^3$ such that none of the three is a multiple of another. [3]
  21. 3(b) Prove that $L(\{x\})=\{ax:a\in\mathbb F\}$ for any vector $x$ in a vector space over a field $F$. Interpret this result geometrically in $R^3$. [3+2]
  22. 3(c) Let $S=\{(1,1,0),(1,0,1),(0,1,1)\}$ be a subset of the vector space $\mathbb F^3$. \begin{enumerate}\renewcommand{\labelenumi}{\roman{enumi}.} \item Prove that if $\mathbb F=\mathbb R$, then $S$ is linearly independent. \item Prove that if $\mathbb F$ has characteristic 2, then $S$ is linearly dependent. \end{enumerate} [4]
  23. 4(a) Let $V$ be a vector space with dimension $n$. Show that every linearly independent subset of $V$ can be extended to a basis for $V$. [5]
  24. 4(b) Find a basis for the subspace $W$ of $M_{n\times n}(F)$ of all upper triangular $n\times n$ matrices. What is the dimension of $W$? [3+1]
  25. 4(c) Consider the subspaces $W_1=\left\{\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\c&a\end{bmatrix}\in V:a,b,c\in\mathbb F\right\}$, and $W_2=\left\{\begin{bmatrix}0&a\\-a&b\end{bmatrix}\in V:a,b\in\mathbb F\right\}$ of $V=M_{2\times2}(\mathbb F)$. Find the dimensions of $W_1$, $W_2$, $W_1+W_2$, and $W_1\cap W_2$ respectively. [2+2+1+1]
  26. 5(a) Prove that there exists a linear transformation $T:\mathbb R^2\to\mathbb R^3$ such that $T(1,1)=(1,0,2)$ and $T(2,3)=(1,-1,4)$. What is $T(8,11)$? [3+1]
  27. 5(b) Find the rank and nullity of the linear mapping $G:\mathbb R^5\to\mathbb R^3$ defined by $G(x,y,z,s,t)=(x+2y+2z+s+t,x+2y+3z+2s-t,3x+6y+8z+5s-t)$. Is the mapping nonsingular? Justify your answer. [3+3+1]
  28. 5(c) Suppose $V$ is a vector space of dimension $n$ and $T$ be a linear operator on $V$ with $\operatorname{rank}T^2=\operatorname{rank}T$. Then find $\ker T\cap\operatorname{Im}T$. Hence, show that $V$ is a direct sum of $\ker T$ and $\operatorname{Im}T$. [3+1]
  29. 6(a) Let $T$ be a linear operator on a finite-dimensional vector space $V$, and let $\beta$ and $\beta'$ be two ordered bases for $V$. Suppose that $Q$ is the change of coordinate matrix that changes $\beta$-coordinates into $\beta'$-coordinates. Then show that $[T]_{\beta'}=Q^{-1}[T]_\beta Q$. [5]
  30. 6(b) Let $V$ and $W$ be vector spaces, and let $T:V\to W$ be a linear and invertible transformation. Show that $T^{-1}:W\to V$ is linear. For the linear transformation $T:\mathcal P_1(\mathbb R)\to\mathbb R^2$ defined by $T(a+bx)=(a,a+b)$, find the formula for $T^{-1}$. [2+1]
  31. 6(c) Let $T:\mathcal P_3(\mathbb R)\to\mathcal P_2(\mathbb R)$ be the linear transformation defined by $T(f(x))=f'(x)$, and let $\beta$ and $\gamma$ be the standard ordered bases for $\mathcal P_3(\mathbb R)$ and $\mathcal P_2(\mathbb R)$, respectively. For the polynomial $p(x)=2-4x+x^2+3x^3\in\mathcal P_3(\mathbb R)$, verify that $[T(p(x))]_\gamma=[T]_\beta^\gamma[p(x)]_\beta$. [7]
  32. 7(a) For subspaces $W_1$ and $W_2$ in a vector space $V$ over a field $F$, show that $(W_1+W_2)^0=W_1^0\cap W_2^0$. [4]
  33. 7(b) Let $V=\mathbb R^3$, and consider the basis $\{f_1(x,y,z)=x-2y,\ f_2(x,y,z)=x+y+z,\ f_3(x,y,z)=y-3z\}$ of $V^*$. Then find a basis for $V$ for which $\{f_1,f_2,f_3\}$ is the dual basis. [4]
  34. 7(c) Consider the vector spaces $V=\mathcal P_1(\mathbb R)$ and $W=\mathbb R^2$ with respective standard ordered bases. Define $T:V\to W$ by $T(p(x))=(p(0)-2p(1),p(0)+p'(0))$, where $p'(x)$ is the derivative of $p(x)$. Compute $[T^t]_{\beta^*}^{\gamma^*}$. [7]
