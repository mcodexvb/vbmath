MMO-41

P11/A13
M.Sc. Mathematics · Semester IV2023
  1. 1(a) Let $R$ be a unitary ring and $M$ be an abelian group. Prove that $M$ is an $R$-module if and only if there is a 1-preserving ring homomorphism from $R$ into $\mathit{End}(M)$. [[3]]
  2. 1(b) Let $A$ and $B$ be two submodules of an $R$-module $M$. Show that the sequence $0\longrightarrow M/(A\cap B)\longrightarrow M/A\times M/B\longrightarrow M/(A+B)\longrightarrow0$ is exact. Deduce that $(A+B)/(A\cap B)\cong(A+B)/A\times(A+B)/B\cong B/(A\cap B)\times B/(A\cap B)$. [[4]]
  3. 1(c) Let $M$ be an $R$-module. Show that $M$ can be considered as an $\overline R=R/\mathit{Ann}_R(M)$-module such that $\mathit{Ann}_{\overline R}(M)=0$. [[3]]
  4. 2(a) Let $f:M\longrightarrow N$ be an $R$-morphism. For every submodule $A$ of $M$ show that $f^{-1}(f(A))=A+\ker f$. [[3]]
  5. 2(b) Give example to show that an one-to-one $R$-morphism in the category rMod may not have any left inverse. [[3]]
  6. 2(c) Show that a short exact sequence $0\longrightarrow A\longrightarrow B\longrightarrow C\longrightarrow0$ is split exact if and only if $B\cong A\oplus C$. [[4]]
  7. 3(a) Define product of a family of $R$-modules $\{M_i\}$. Show that any two products of $\{M_i\}$ are isomorphic. [[3]]
  8. 3(b) Show that $\mathit{Mor}_R(\bigoplus_{i\in I}N_i,M)\cong\prod_{i\in I}\mathit{Mor}_R(N_i,M)$. [[4]]
  9. 3(c) Let $f:M\longrightarrow M$ be an $R$-morphism. If $M$ is of finite height, show that there exists $n\in\mathbb N$ such that $M=\ker f^n\oplus\mathit{Im} f^n$. [[3]]
  10. 4(a) Show that coproduct exists in the category rMod. [[3]]
  11. 4(b) Prove that an $R$-module $M$ is a direct sum of $n$-submodules $N_1,N_2,\ldots,N_n$ if and only if there are $n$ non zero $R$-morphisms $P_1,P_2,\ldots,P_n:M\longrightarrow M$ such that\par (i) $\sum P_i=\mathit{Id}_M$,\par (ii) $P_i\circ P_j=0$ for all $i\ne j$. [[4]]
  12. 4(c) Let $f:M\longrightarrow M$ be an $R$-morphism. Show that $M=\mathit{Im} f\oplus\ker f$ if and only if $\mathit{Im} f=\mathit{Im} f^2$. [[3]]
  13. 5(a) Let $(F,f)$ be a free $R$-module on a nonempty set $X$. Show that $f$ is one-to-one and $\mathit{Im} f$ generates $F$. [[3]]
  14. 5(b) Show that an $R$-module $F$ is free if and only if it has a basis. [[4]]
  15. 5(c) Let $F$ be a free $R$-module, and $f:F\longrightarrow M_1$ and $g:M_2\longrightarrow M_1$ be two $R$-morphisms. If $g$ is an epimorphism, show that there is an $R$-morphism $h:F\longrightarrow M_2$ such that $g\circ h=f$. [[3]]
  16. 6(a) Let $R$ be a commutative unitary ring and $I$ be an ideal. Show that a linearly independent subset of the $R$-module $I$ can not contain more than one element. Hence or otherwise show that if every ideal of $R$ is a free $R$-module, then $R$ is a PID. [[3]]
  17. 6(b) Show that direct sum of any family of free $R$-modules is free. [[4]]
  18. 6(c) Show that the $\mathbb Z$-module $\mathbb Q_p/\mathbb Z$ is not free. [[3]]
