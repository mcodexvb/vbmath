TIFR Graduate School · GS 2014 · Mathematics

  1. 1 Let $A, B, C$ be three subsets of $\mathbb{R}$. The negation of the following statement: \begin{quote} For every $\epsilon > 1$, there exists $a \in A$ and $b \in B$ such that for all $c \in C$, $|a - c| < \epsilon$ and $|b - c| > \epsilon$ \end{quote} is \begin{enumerate}[label=\Alph*.] \item there exists $\epsilon \le 1$, such that for all $a \in A$ and $b \in B$ there exists $c \in C$ such that $|a - c| \ge \epsilon$ or $|b - c| \le \epsilon$. \item there exists $\epsilon \le 1$, such that for all $a \in A$ and $b \in B$ there exists $c \in C$ such that $|a - c| \ge \epsilon$ and $|b - c| \le \epsilon$. \item there exists $\epsilon > 1$, such that for all $a \in A$ and $b \in B$ there exists $c \in C$ such that $|a - c| \ge \epsilon$ and $|b - c| \le \epsilon$. \item there exists $\epsilon > 1$, such that for all $a \in A$ and $b \in B$ there exists $c \in C$ such that $|a - c| \ge \epsilon$ or $|b - c| \le \epsilon$. \end{enumerate}
  2. 2 Let $f: \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ be a continuous bounded function, then: \begin{enumerate}[label=\Alph*.] \item $f$ has to be uniformly continuous. \item there exists an $x \in \mathbb{R}$ such that $f(x) = x$. \item $f$ cannot be increasing. \item $\lim_{x \to \infty} f(x)$ exists. \end{enumerate}
  3. 3 Let $f: \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ be a differentiable function such that $\lim_{x \to +\infty} f'(x) = 1$; then \begin{enumerate}[label=\Alph*.] \item $f$ is bounded. \item $f$ is increasing. \item $f$ is unbounded. \item $f'$ is bounded. \end{enumerate}
  4. 4 Let $f$ be the real valued function on $[0, \infty)$ defined by \[ f(x) = \begin{cases} x^{2/3} \log x, & \text{for } x > 0, \\ 0, & \text{if } x = 0. \end{cases} \] Then \begin{enumerate}[label=\Alph*.] \item $f$ is discontinuous at $x = 0$. \item $f$ is continuous on $[0, \infty)$, but not uniformly continuous on $[0, \infty)$. \item $f$ is uniformly continuous on $[0, \infty)$. \item $f$ is not uniformly continuous on $[0, \infty)$, but uniformly continuous on $(0, \infty)$. \end{enumerate}
  5. 5 Let $a_n = (n + 1)^{100} e^{-\sqrt{n}}$ for $n \ge 1$. Then the sequence $(a_n)_n$ is \begin{enumerate}[label=\Alph*.] \item unbounded. \item bounded but does not converge. \item bounded and converges to $1$. \item bounded and converges to $0$. \end{enumerate}
  6. 6 Let $f: [0, 1] \to \mathbb{R}$ be a continuous function. Which of the following statements is always true? \begin{enumerate}[label=\Alph*.] \item $\int_0^1 f^2(x)\,dx = \left(\int_0^1 f(x)\,dx\right)^2$. \item $\int_0^1 f^2(x)\,dx \le \left(\int_0^1 |f(x)|\,dx\right)^2$. \item $\int_0^1 f^2(x)\,dx \ge \left(\int_0^1 |f(x)|\,dx\right)^2$. \item $\int_0^1 f^2(x)\,dx \le \left(\int_0^1 f(x)\,dx\right)^2$. \end{enumerate}
  7. 7 Let $f_n(x)$, for $n \ge 1$, be a sequence of continuous nonnegative functions on $[0, 1]$ such that $\lim_{n \to \infty} \int_0^1 f_n(x)\,dx = 0$. Which of the following statements is always correct? \begin{enumerate}[label=\Alph*.] \item $f_n \to 0$ uniformly on $[0, 1]$. \item $f_n$ may not converge uniformly but converges to $0$ point-wise. \item $f_n$ will converge point-wise and the limit may be non-zero. \item $f_n$ is not guaranteed to have a point-wise limit. \end{enumerate}
  8. 8 Let $f: \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ be a continuous function such that $|f(x) - f(y)| \ge \frac{1}{2}|x - y|$ for all $x, y \in \mathbb{R}$. Then \begin{enumerate}[label=\Alph*.] \item $f$ is both one-to-one and onto. \item $f$ is one-to-one but may not be onto. \item $f$ is onto but may not be one-to-one. \item $f$ is neither one-to-one nor onto. \end{enumerate}
  9. 9 Let $A(\theta) = \begin{pmatrix} \cos\theta & \sin\theta \\ -\sin\theta & \cos\theta \end{pmatrix}$, where $\theta \in (0, 2\pi)$. Mark the correct statement below. \begin{enumerate}[label=\Alph*.] \item $A(\theta)$ has eigenvectors in $\mathbb{R}^2$ for all $\theta \in (0, 2\pi)$. \item $A(\theta)$ does not have an eigenvector in $\mathbb{R}^2$ for any $\theta \in (0, 2\pi)$. \item $A(\theta)$ has eigenvectors in $\mathbb{R}^2$ for exactly one value of $\theta \in (0, 2\pi)$. \item $A(\theta)$ has eigenvectors in $\mathbb{R}^2$ for exactly $2$ values of $\theta \in (0, 2\pi)$. \end{enumerate}
  10. 10 Let $\mathcal{C} \subset \mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}$ be the set of integer pairs $(a, b)$ for which the three complex roots $r_1, r_2$ and $r_3$ of the polynomial $p(x) = x^3 - 2x^2 + ax - b$ satisfy $r_1^3 + r_2^3 + r_3^3 = 0$. Then the cardinality of $\mathcal{C}$ is \begin{enumerate}[label=\Alph*.] \item $|\mathcal{C}| = \infty$ \item $|\mathcal{C}| = 0$ \item $|\mathcal{C}| = 1$ \item $1 < |\mathcal{C}| < \infty$ \end{enumerate}
  11. 11 Let $A$ be an $n \times n$ matrix with real entries such that $A^k = 0$ ($0$-matrix) for some $k \in \mathbb{N}$. Then \begin{enumerate}[label=\Alph*.] \item $A$ has to be the $0$ matrix. \item $\operatorname{trace}(A)$ could be non-zero. \item $A$ is diagonalizable. \item $0$ is the only eigenvalue of $A$. \end{enumerate}
  12. 12 There exists a map $f: \mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Q}$ such that $f$ \begin{enumerate}[label=\Alph*.] \item is bijective and increasing. \item is onto and decreasing. \item is bijective and satisfies $f(n) \ge 0$ if $n \le 0$. \item has uncountable image. \end{enumerate}
  13. 13 Let $S$ be the set of all tuples $(x, y)$ with $x, y$ non-negative real numbers satisfying $x + y = 2n$ for a fixed $n \in \mathbb{N}$. Then the supremum value of $x^2 y^2 (x^2 + y^2)$ on the set $S$ is \begin{enumerate}[label=\Alph*.] \item $3n^6$ \item $2n^6$ \item $4n^6$ \item $n^6$. \end{enumerate}
  14. 14 Let $G$ be a group and let $H$ and $K$ be two subgroups of $G$. If both $H$ and $K$ have $12$ elements, which of the following numbers cannot be the cardinality of the set $HK = \{hk : h \in H, \, k \in K\}$? \begin{enumerate}[label=\Alph*.] \item $72$ \item $60$ \item $48$ \item $36$. \end{enumerate}
  15. 15 How many proper subgroups does the group $\mathbb{Z} \oplus \mathbb{Z}$ have? \begin{enumerate}[label=\Alph*.] \item $1$ \item $2$ \item $3$ \item infinitely many. \end{enumerate}
  16. 16 $X$ is a metric space. $Y$ is a closed subset of $X$ such that the distance between any two points in $Y$ is at most $1$. Then \begin{enumerate}[label=\Alph*.] \item $Y$ is compact. \item any continuous function from $Y \to \mathbb{R}$ is bounded. \item $Y$ is not an open subset of $X$. \item none of the above. \end{enumerate}
  17. 17 Let $f: \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ be a continuous function and let $S$ be a non-empty proper subset of $\mathbb{R}$. Which one of the following statements is always true? (Here $\overline{A}$ denotes the closure of $A$ and $A^\circ$ denotes the interior of $A$.) \begin{enumerate}[label=\Alph*.] \item $f(S)^\circ \subseteq f(S^\circ)$ \item $f(\overline{S}) \subseteq \overline{f(S)}$ \item $f(\overline{S}) \supseteq \overline{f(S)}$ \item $f(S)^\circ \supseteq f(S^\circ)$. \end{enumerate}
  18. 18 What is the last digit of $97^{2013}$? \begin{enumerate}[label=\Alph*.] \item $1$ \item $3$ \item $7$ \item $9$. \end{enumerate}
  19. 19 For $n \in \mathbb{N}$ we define $s_n = 1^3 + 2^3 + 3^3 + \dots + n^3$. Which of the following holds for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$? \begin{enumerate}[label=\Alph*.] \item $s_n$ is an odd integer. \item $s_n = n^2(n + 1)^2 / 4$. \item $s_n = n(n + 1)(2n + 1) / 6$. \item none of the above. \end{enumerate}
  20. 20 Let $C$ denote the cube $[-1, 1]^3 \subset \mathbb{R}^3$. How many rotations are there in $\mathbb{R}^3$ {[obscured in source]}? \begin{enumerate}[label=\Alph*.] \item $6$ \item {[obscured in source]} \item {[obscured in source]} \item $24$. \end{enumerate}
  21. 21 Let $f: [0, 1] \to [0, \infty)$ be continuous. Suppose $\int_0^x f(t)\,dt \ge f(x)$ for all $x \in [0, 1]$. Then \begin{enumerate}[label=\Alph*.] \item no such function exists. \item there are infinitely many such functions. \item there is only one such function. \item there are exactly two such functions. \end{enumerate}
  22. 22 Let $f: \mathbb{R}^2 \to \mathbb{R}$ be a continuous map such that $f(x) = 0$ for only finitely many values of $x$. Which of the following is true? \begin{enumerate}[label=\Alph*.] \item either $f(x) \le 0$ for all $x$, or $f(x) \ge 0$ for all $x$. \item the map $f$ is onto. \item the map $f$ is one-to-one. \item none of the above. \end{enumerate}
  23. 23 Let $S_n$ be the symmetric group of $n$ letters. There exists an onto group homomorphism \begin{enumerate}[label=\Alph*.] \item from $S_5$ to $S_4$ \item from $S_4$ to $S_2$ \item from $S_5$ to $\mathbb{Z}/5$ \item from $S_4$ to $\mathbb{Z}/4$. \end{enumerate}
  24. 24 Let $H_1, H_2$ be two distinct subgroups of a finite group $G$, each of order $2$. Let $H$ be the smallest subgroup containing $H_1$ and $H_2$. Then the order of $H$ is \begin{enumerate}[label=\Alph*.] \item always $2$ \item always $4$ \item always $8$ \item none of the above. \end{enumerate}
  25. 25 Which of the following groups are isomorphic? \begin{enumerate}[label=\Alph*.] \item $\mathbb{R}$ and $\mathbb{C}$ \item $\mathbb{R}^*$ and $\mathbb{C}^*$ \item $S_3 \times \mathbb{Z}/4$ and $S_4$ \item $\mathbb{Z}/2 \times \mathbb{Z}/2$ and $\mathbb{Z}/4$. \end{enumerate}
  26. 26 The number of irreducible polynomials of the form $x^2 + ax + b$ with $a, b$ in the field $\mathbb{F}_7$ of $7$ elements is: \begin{enumerate}[label=\Alph*.] \item $7$ \item $21$ \item $35$ \item $49$. \end{enumerate}
  27. 27 $X$ is a topological space of infinite cardinality which is homeomorphic to $X \times X$. Then \begin{enumerate}[label=\Alph*.] \item $X$ is not connected. \item $X$ is not compact. \item $X$ is not homeomorphic to a subset of $\mathbb{R}$. \item none of the above. \end{enumerate}
  28. 28 Let $X$ be a non-empty topological space such that every function $f: X \to \mathbb{R}$ is continuous. Then \begin{enumerate}[label=\Alph*.] \item $X$ has the discrete topology. \item $X$ has the indiscrete topology. \item $X$ is compact. \item $X$ is not connected. \end{enumerate}
  29. 29 Let $f: X \to Y$ be a continuous map between metric spaces. Then $f(X)$ is a complete subset of $Y$ if \begin{enumerate}[label=\Alph*.] \item the space $X$ is compact. \item the space $Y$ is compact. \item the space $X$ is complete. \item the space $Y$ is complete. \end{enumerate}
  30. 30 How many maps $\phi: \mathbb{N} \cup \{0\} \to \mathbb{N} \cup \{0\}$ are there with the property that $\phi(ab) = \phi(a) + \phi(b)$ for all $a, b \in \mathbb{N} \cup \{0\}$? \begin{enumerate}[label=\Alph*.] \item none \item {[obscured in source]} \item {[obscured in source]} \item {[obscured in source]} \end{enumerate}
