BMC-52

B.Sc. Mathematics · Semester V2017
  1. 1 Answer \textit{any five} questions. [$5\times2=10$]
  2. 1(a) Show that $\left\{\frac1x,\frac{\log_e(x)}x\right\}$ is a fundamental set of solutions to $\frac{d^2y}{dx^2}+\frac3x\frac{dy}{dx}+\frac1{x^2}y=0$ on $(0,\infty)$.
  3. 1(b) Show that the substitution $v=\log_e(x)$ $(x>0)$ transforms the ODE $ax^3\frac{d^3y}{dx^3}+bx^2\frac{d^2y}{dx^2}+cx\frac{dy}{dx}+dy=0$, where $a,b,c$ and $d$ are constants, into the another equation $a\frac{d^3y}{dv^3}+(b-3a)\frac{d^2y}{dv^2}+(c-b+2a)\frac{dy}{dv}+dy=0$.
  4. 1(c) Find an integrating factor in which the following ODE is exact: $x^5\frac{d^2y}{dx^2}+3x^3\frac{dy}{dx}+(3-6x)x^2y=x^4+2x-5$.
  5. 1(d) Give geometrical interpretation of the integrable equation $Pdx+Qdy+Rdz=0$; $P,Q,R$ are functions of $x,y$ and $z$.
  6. 1(e) Locate and classify the singular points of the ODE $x^4(x-2017)\frac{d^2y}{dx^2}-5(x-2017)\frac{dy}{dx}+7x^2y=0$.
  7. 1(f) Find the PDE of all spheres of radius $c$ having centres in the $xy$-plane.
  8. 1(g) Show that the PDE $p^2+q^2=z^2$ has solutions of the forms $z=c_1e^x$ and $z=c_2e^y$, where $c_1$ and $c_2$ are arbitrary constants, but their sum $c_1e^x+c_2e^y$ is not a solution of the equation. Justify your answer. $[p=\frac{\partial z}{\partial x},q=\frac{\partial z}{\partial y},z=z(x,y)]$
  9. 1(h) Show that the PDEs $p=x^2-ay$, $q=y^2-ax$ are compatible and find their common solution.
  10. 2(a) If the functions $y_1,y_2:\mathbf I\to\mathbb R$ are linearly dependent, then their Wronskian function vanishes identically on the interval $\mathbf I$ -- Prove it. Is the converse statement true? Justify your answer with an example.
  11. 2(b) Show that the general solution of a second order linear homogeneous ODE $\frac{d^2y}{dx^2}+p(x)\frac{dy}{dx}+Q(x)y=0$ can be written as $y=uv$, where $u$ and $v$ respectively satisfy the differential equation $P+\frac2u\frac{du}{dx}=0$ and $\frac{d^2v}{dx^2}+Iv=0$; $I=Q-\frac{P^2}4-\frac12\frac{dP}{dx}$. Hence, solve the ODE $\frac d{dx}\left(\cos^2x\frac{dy}{dx}\right)+y\cos^2x=0$.
  12. 3(a) State Picard's existence and uniqueness theorem on successive approximations for the solution of the IVP $\frac{dy}{dx}=f(x,y)$, $y(x=x_0)=y_0$. Using Picard's method, find the first three approximations of the solutions of the equation $\frac{dy}{dx}=1+xy$, $y(x=1)=2$.
  13. 3(b) Determine the unknown function $f$, so that the following ODE is integrable and then solve it: $(y^2+z^2-x^2)dx-2xydy+2xf(z)dz=0$.
  14. 4(a) Solve the following simultaneous equations: $\frac{dx}{dt}-3x+4y=e^{-2t}$, $\frac{dy}{dt}-x+2y=3e^{-2t}$, satisfying $x=12$, $y=7$ when $t=0$. [5]
  15. 4(b) Use power series to solve the ODE $\frac{d^2y}{dx^2}-x\frac{dy}{dx}-y=0$, $y(0)=1$, $\frac{dy}{dx}\Big]_{x=0}=0$. [5]
  16. 5(a) Find the complete integral of $z\frac{\partial z}{\partial x}+\frac1{2017}\frac{\partial z}{\partial y}=0$ and verify that it satisfies the PDE. Also determine the integral surface when $z(x,0)=-x$ and comment on the nature of the solution as $y\to2017$.
  17. 5(b) Solve: $\frac{\partial^2z}{\partial x\partial y}+\frac{\partial z}{\partial x}-\frac{\partial z}{\partial y}=z+xy$. [5]
  18. 6(a) Find the general equation of surfaces orthogonal to the family given by $x(x^2+y^2+z^2)=cy^2$, $c$ is an arbitrary constant. [5]
  19. 6(b) Show that $z=ax+by+a^2+b^2$ is a complete integral of the PDE $z-px-qy-p^2-q^2=0$. Hence, obtain singular integral, if any and general integral by assuming $b=a$.
