MMC-13

Linear Algebra
M.Sc. Mathematics · Semester I2021
  1. 1(a) Let $X,Y\in\mathbb R^n$ and let $A=XY^t$. Show that the characteristic polynomial of $A$ is $t^{n-1}(t-\lambda)$ where $\lambda=X^tY$. If $\lambda\ne0$, then prove that $E_0\perp Y$. [2+2]
  2. 1(b) Let $T:\mathbb R^2\to\mathbb R^2$ be the projection of $\mathbb R^2$ on the $x$-axis along the line $y=2x$. Find two nonzero proper $T$-invariant subspaces of $\mathbb R^2$. Is there any other nonzero proper $T$-invariant subspaces? [3]
  3. 1(c) State and prove the Cayley-Hamilton theorem for linear operators. [3]
  4. 2(a) Let $m(t)$ be the minimal polynomial of a linear operator $T:V\to V$. Show that $m(t)\mid f(t)$ for every annihilating polynomial $f(t)$ of $T$. [2]
  5. 2(b) Give an example to show that two matrices may not be similar still they can have the same minimal polynomial. Justify. [2]
  6. 2(c) Let $A$ be an $n\times n$ nilpotent matrix. Then show that $A^n=O$. If index of nilpotence of $A$ is $n$, then show that there can not be a matrix $B$ such that $B^2=A$. [3]
  7. 2(d) Let $M\in M_n(F)$ and let $T:M_n(F)\to M_n(F)$ be defined by $T(A)=MA$. Show that $T$ and $M$ have the same minimal polynomial. [3]
  8. 3(a) Let $A\in M_n(F)$. Prove that eigen vectors belonging to distinct eigen values of $A$ are linearly independent. [4]
  9. 3(b) Let $A$ be an $n\times n$ real matrix with each entry equal to 1. Find the eigen values of $A$ with multiplicities. Hence find a diagonal form of $A$. [3]
  10. 3(c) Let $T:M_n(\mathbb R)\to M_n(\mathbb R)$ be defined by $T(A)=A^t$. Show that 1 and $-1$ are the only eigen values of $T$. Hence or otherwise show that $T$ is diagonalizable. [3]
  11. 4(a) Show that a linear operator $T:V\to V$ is triangulizable if and only if the characteristic polynomial of $T$ splits into linear factors. [4]
  12. 4(b) If $\lambda$ and $\mu$ are two distinct eigen values of a linear operator $T$, then show that $G_\lambda\cap G_\mu=\{\theta\}$. [3]
  13. 4(c) Find the Jordan canonical form of the linear operator $T(p(x))=p'(x)$ on $P_2(\mathbb R)$. [3]
  14. 5(a) Let $T$ be a linear operator on an inner product space $V$. Show that there is a unique linear operator $T^*$ on $V$ such that $\langle T(u),v\rangle=\langle u,T^*(v)\rangle$ for all $u,v\in V$. [4]
  15. 5(b) Let $T$ be a linear operator on an inner product space $V$. If $T$ has an eigen value $\lambda$ then show that $\overline\lambda$ is an eigen value of $T^*$. [2]
  16. 5(c) If $f:\mathbb R^3\to\mathbb R$ be defined by $f(x,y,z)=2x-3y+4z$, find a vector $Y\in\mathbb R^3$ such that $f(X)=\langle X,Y\rangle$ for all $X\in\mathbb R^3$. [2]
  17. 5(d) Find the adjoint of the linear operator $T:\mathbb R^2\to\mathbb R^2$ defined by $T(x,y)=(x-y,2x+y)$. [2]
  18. 6(a) Prove that eigen vectors belonging to two distinct eigen values of a normal operator are orthogonal. [3]
  19. 6(b) Show that every eigen value of a self-adjoint linear operator is real. [2]
  20. 6(c) Prove that a linear operator $T$ on $V$ is unitary if and only if $\|T(v)\|=\|v\|$ for all $v\in V$. [3]
  21. 6(d) Let $Q$ be a real skew-symmetric matrix. Then show that $I+Q$ is nonsingular. \endgroup [2]
