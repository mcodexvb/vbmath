BMC-64

B.Sc. Mathematics · Semester VI2019
  1. 1(a) Define stress, thrust and shearing stress on a surface of a fluid. [1.5]
  2. 1(b) Show that in a homogeneous fluid at rest under gravity, the difference of the pressures between any two points in the same vertical line is proportional to the difference of their depths. [2]
  3. 1(c) A small uniform circular tube, whose plane is vertical, contains equal quantities of fluids whose densities are $\rho$ and $\sigma$, $(\rho>\sigma)$, and do not mix. If they together fill half the tube, show that the radius passing through the common surface makes with the vertical an angle $\tan^{-1}\frac{\rho-\sigma}{\rho+\sigma}$. [2.5]
  4. 2(a) Obtain a necessary condition for a fluid of mean density $\rho$ to be in equilibrium under the external force $\mathbf F=(X,Y,Z)$ per unit mass of the fluid. [3]
  5. 2(b) Show that if the field of force is conservative, the surface of equi-pressure, equi-density and equi-potential energy coincide. Hence find the differential equations of the equi-density and equi-pressure surfaces. [1.5+1.5]
  6. 3(a) A given volume $V$ of liquid is acted upon by forces $-\mu x/a^2,-\mu y/b^2$ and $-\mu z/c^2$ along the rectangular Cartesian coordinate axes, where $\mu$, $a$, $b$ and $c$ are constants. Find the equation of the free surface. [2]
  7. 3(b) Show that the pressure at a small depth $z$ below the surface of a sphere of water attracted to the centre of the sphere with a force producing an acceleration $\mu/r^2$ at a distance $r$ is approximately $\Pi+g\rho\left(z+z^2/a\right)$, where $a$ is the radius of the sphere, $\Pi$ the pressure at the free surface, and $g$ the attraction of a unit mass at the surface of the sphere. [4]
  8. 4(a) Explain the notion of thrust of a heavy homogeneous liquid on a plane surface. [2]
  9. 4(b) A hollow weightless cone of vertical angle $2\alpha$ is filled with fluid, and suspended freely from a point on the rim of its base. Show that the ratio of the thrust on the base and the weight of fluid, the cone would contain, is $12\sin^2\alpha:\cos\alpha\sqrt{1+15\sin^2\alpha}$. [4]
  10. 5(a) Define the centre fo pressure of a given area in a fluid. Find the depth of the centre of pressure of a plane area immersed in a fluid under the action of gravity. [1+2]
  11. 5(b) A lamina in the shape of a quadrilateral $ABCD$ has its side $CD$ in the surface of a liquid and the sides $AD$, $BC$ vertical and equal to $\alpha$, $\beta$ respectively. Show that the depth of the centre of pressure is $\frac12\frac{(\alpha^2+\beta^2)(\alpha+\beta)}{\alpha^2+\alpha\beta+\beta^2}$. [3]
  12. 6(a) State the conditions of equilibrium of a freely floating body immersed wholly or partially in a fluid. [2]
  13. 6(b) Define Metacentre of a freely floating body, and hence obtain the condition(s) for stable and unstable equilibria. What is surface of buoyancy? [2+1+1]
  14. 1(a) State the concept of mathematical modelling. Sketch a schematic reprsentation of the modelling process. Classify the models and indicate their differences with illustrations. [4]
  15. 1(b) Show that the energy is not conserved in the spring-mass system in the presence of friction unlike that in frictionless system. [4]
  16. 2(a) Consider $\dot x=f(x)$ as one-dimensional autonomous dynamical system with $f:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R$ which is strongly differentiable on $\mathbb R$. If $x_0\in\mathbb R$ is a fixed point with $f(x_0)=0$, then show that $x_0$ is asymptotically stable if $f'(x_0)<0$ and unstable if $f'(x_0)>0$. [4]
  17. 2(b) Find the fixed points of the following systems and examine their stability\\(i) $\frac{dx}{dt}=4x^2(x+1)^3(x-3)^2$\\(ii) $\frac{dx}{dt}=x^2-x^5$ [4]
  18. 3(a) Define Lyapunov stability of the solutions of the $n$-dimensional dynamical system $\dot x=X(x,t)$. Hence show that all solutions of the $n$-dimensional system $\dot x=-x$ are stable for $t\ge0$ in the Lyapunov sense. [4]
  19. 3(b) Evaluate the Jacobian for the following autonomous system\\$\dot x_1=x_1^2(x_1-x_2)^3$\\$\dot x_2=x_2^3(x_1-x_2)^2$\\and hence examine the stability of the system at $(0,0)$. [4]
