TIFR Graduate School · GS 2018 · Mathematics

  1. 1 Let $A$ be a countable subset of $\mathbb{R}$ which is well-ordered with respect to the usual ordering on $\mathbb{R}$ (where `well-ordered' means that every nonempty subset has a minimum element in it). Then $A$ has an order preserving bijection with a subset of $\mathbb{N}$.
  2. 2 $\displaystyle \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin x}{\log(1 + \tan x)} = 1$.
  3. 3 For any closed subset $A \subset \mathbb{R}$, there exists a continuous function $f$ on $\mathbb{R}$ which vanishes exactly on $A$.
  4. 4 Let $f$ be a nonnegative continuous function on $\mathbb{R}$ such that $\int_0^\infty f(t)\,dt$ is finite. Then $\lim_{x \to \infty} f(x) = 0$.
  5. 5 The function $f(x) = \cos(e^x)$ is not uniformly continuous on $\mathbb{R}$.
  6. 6 Let $A$ be a $3 \times 3$ real symmetric matrix such that $A^6 = I$. Then, $A^2 = I$.
  7. 7 In the vector space $\{f \mid f:[0,1] \to \mathbb{R}\}$ of real-valued functions on the closed interval $[0,1]$, the set $S = \{\sin(x), \cos(x), \tan(x)\}$ is linearly independent.
  8. 8 Let $f$ be a twice differentiable function on $\mathbb{R}$ such that both $f$ and $f''$ are strictly positive on $\mathbb{R}$. Then $\lim_{x \to \infty} f(x) = \infty$.
  9. 9 Let $G, H$ be finite groups. Then any subgroup of $G \times H$ is equal to $A \times B$ for some subgroups $A < G$ and $B < H$.
  10. 10 Let $g$ be a continuous function on $[0,1]$ such that $g(1) = 0$. Then the sequence of functions $f_n(x) = x^n g(x)$ converges uniformly on $[0,1]$.
  11. 11 Let $A, B, C \in M_3(\mathbb{R})$ be such that $A$ commutes with $B$, $B$ commutes with $C$ and $B$ is not a scalar matrix. Then $A$ commutes with $C$.
  12. 12 If $A \in M_n(\mathbb{R})$ (with $n \ge 2$) has rank $1$, then the minimal polynomial of $A$ has degree $2$.
  13. 13 Let $V$ be the vector space over $\mathbb{R}$ consisting of polynomials of degree less than or equal to $3$. Let $T: V \to V$ be the operator sending $f(t)$ to $f(t+1)$ and $D: V \to V$ the operator sending $f(t)$ to $df(t)/dt$. Then $T$ is a polynomial in $D$.
  14. 14 Let $V$ be the subspace of the real vector space of real valued functions on $\mathbb{R}$, spanned by $\cos t$ and $\sin t$. Let $D: V \to V$ be the linear map sending $f(t) \in V$ to $df(t)/dt$. Then $D$ has a real eigenvalue.
  15. 15 The set of nilpotent matrices in $M_3(\mathbb{R})$ spans $M_3(\mathbb{R})$ considered as an $\mathbb{R}$-vector space (a matrix $A$ is said to be nilpotent if there exists $n \in \mathbb{N}$ such that $A^n = 0$).
  16. 16 Let $G$ be a finite group with a normal subgroup $H$ such that $G/H$ has order $7$. Then $G \cong H \times G/H$.
  17. 17 The multiplicative group $\mathbb{F}_7^\times$ is isomorphic to a subgroup of the multiplicative group $\mathbb{F}_{31}^\times$.
  18. 18 Any linear transformation $A: \mathbb{R}^4 \to \mathbb{R}^4$ has a proper non-zero invariant subspace.
  19. 19 Let $A, B \in M_n(\mathbb{R})$ be such that $A + B = AB$. Then $AB = BA$.
  20. 20 Let $A \in M_n(\mathbb{R})$ be upper triangular with all diagonal entries $1$ such that $A \ne I$. Then $A$ is not diagonalizable.
  21. 21 A countable group can have only countably many distinct subgroups.
  22. 22 There exists a continuous surjection from $\mathbb{R}^3 - S^2$ to $\mathbb{R}^2 - \{(0,0)\}$ (here $S^2 \subset \mathbb{R}^3$ denotes the unit sphere defined by the equation $x^2 + y^2 + z^2 = 1$).
  23. 23 The permutation group $S_{10}$ has an element of order $30$.
  24. 24 Let $G$ be a finite group and $g \in G$ an element of even order. Then we can colour the elements of $G$ with two colours in such a way that $x$ and $gx$ have different colours for each $x \in G$.
  25. 25 Let $f(x)$ and $g(x)$ be uniformly continuous functions from $\mathbb{R}$ to $\mathbb{R}$. Then their pointwise product $f(x)g(x)$ is uniformly continuous.
  26. 1 The set of real numbers in the open interval $(0,1)$ which have more than one decimal expansion is \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)] \item empty. \item non-empty but finite. \item countably infinite. \item uncountable. \end{enumerate}
  27. 2 How many zeroes does the function $f(x) = e^x - 3x^2$ have in $\mathbb{R}$? \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)] \item $0$ \item $1$ \item $2$ \item $3$. \end{enumerate}
  28. 3 Let $f: \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ be defined as follows: \[ f(x) = \begin{cases} 1, & \text{if } x = 0, \\ 0, & \text{if } x \in \mathbb{R} \setminus \mathbb{Q}, \text{ and} \\ \frac{1}{n}, & \text{if } x = \frac{m}{n} \text{ with } m, n \in \mathbb{Z}, \, n > 0 \text{ and } \gcd(m,n) = 1. \end{cases} \] Which of the following statements is true? \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)] \item $f$ is continuous everywhere except at $0$. \item $f$ is continuous only at the irrationals. \item $f$ is continuous only at the non-zero rationals. \item $f$ is not continuous anywhere. \end{enumerate}
  29. 4 Suppose $p$ is a degree $3$ polynomial such that $p(0) = 1$, $p(1) = 2$ and $p(2) = 5$. Which of the following numbers cannot equal $p(3)$? \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)] \item $0$ \item $2$ \item $6$ \item $10$. \end{enumerate}
  30. 5 Let $A$ be the set of all functions $f: \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ that satisfy the following two properties: \begin{itemize} \item $f$ has derivatives of all orders, and \item for all $x, y \in \mathbb{R}$, $f(x+y) - f(y-x) = 2x f'(y)$. \end{itemize} Which of the following sentences is true? \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)] \item Any $f \in A$ is a polynomial of degree less than or equal to $1$. \item Any $f \in A$ is a polynomial of degree less than or equal to $2$. \item There exists $f \in A$ which is not a polynomial. \item There exists $f \in A$ which is a polynomial of degree $4$. \end{enumerate}
  31. 6 Denote by $\mathfrak{A}$ the set of all $n \times n$ complex matrices $A$ ($n \ge 2$ a natural number) having the property that $4$ is the only eigenvalue of $A$. Consider the following four statements: \begin{itemize} \item $(A - 4I)^n = 0$, \item $A^n = 4^n I$, \item $(A^2 - 5A + 4I)^n = 0$, \item $A^n = 4nI$. \end{itemize} How many of the above statements are true for all $A \in \mathfrak{A}$? \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)] \item $0$ \item $1$ \item $2$ \item $3$. \end{enumerate}
  32. 7 Let $A$ be the set of all continuous functions $f: [0,1] \to [0,\infty)$ satisfying the following condition: \[ \int_0^x f(t)\,dt \ge f(x), \quad \text{for all } x \in [0,1]. \] Then which of the following statements is true? \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)] \item $A$ has cardinality $1$. \item $A$ has cardinality $2$. \item $A$ is infinite. \item $A$ is empty. \end{enumerate}
  33. 8 Consider the following four sets of maps $f: \mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Q}$: \begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)] \item $\{f: \mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Q} \mid f \text{ is bijective and increasing}\}$, \item $\{f: \mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Q} \mid f \text{ is onto and increasing}\}$, \item $\{f: \mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Q} \mid f \text{ is bijective, and satisfies that } \forall n \le 0, \, f(n) \ge 0\}$, and \item $\{f: \mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Q} \mid f \text{ is onto and decreasing}\}$. \end{enumerate} How many of these sets are empty? \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)] \item $0$ \item $1$ \item $2$ \item $3$. \end{enumerate}
  34. 9 What are the last $3$ digits of $2^{2017}$? \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)] \item $072$ \item $472$ \item $512$ \item $912$. \end{enumerate}
  35. 10 The minimal polynomial of \[ \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 2 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 2 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 & 5 \end{pmatrix} \] is \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)] \item $(x - 2)(x - 5)$. \item $(x - 2)^2(x - 5)$. \item $(x - 2)^3(x - 5)$. \item none of the above. \end{enumerate}
  36. 11 Consider a cube $C$ centered at the origin in $\mathbb{R}^3$. The number of invertible linear transformations of $\mathbb{R}^3$ which map $C$ onto itself is \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)] \item $72$ \item $48$ \item $24$ \item $12$. \end{enumerate}
  37. 12 The number of rings of order $4$, up to isomorphism, is: \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)] \item $1$ \item $2$ \item $3$ \item $4$. \end{enumerate}
  38. 13 For a sequence $\{a_n\}$ of real numbers, which of the following is a negation of the statement `$\lim_{n \to \infty} a_n = 0$'? \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)] \item There exists $\epsilon > 0$ such that the set $\{n \in \mathbb{N} \mid |a_n| > \epsilon\}$ is infinite. \item For any $M > 0$ there exists $N \in \mathbb{N}$ such that $|a_n| > M$ for all $n \ge N$. \item There exists a nonzero real number $a$ such that for every $\epsilon > 0$, there exists $N \in \mathbb{N}$ with $|a_n - a| < \epsilon$ for all $n \ge N$. \item For any $a \in \mathbb{R}$ and every $\epsilon > 0$ there exist infinitely many $n$ such that $|a_n - a| > \epsilon$. \end{enumerate}
  39. 14 Let $f: \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ be continuous. Then which of the following statements implies that $f(0) = 0$? \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)] \item $\lim_{n \to \infty} \int_0^1 f(x)^n\,dx = 0$. \item $\lim_{n \to \infty} \int_0^1 f(x/n)\,dx = 0$. \item $\lim_{n \to \infty} \int_0^1 f(nx)\,dx = 0$. \item None of the above. \end{enumerate}
  40. 15 Consider the following maps from $\mathbb{R}^2$ to $\mathbb{R}^2$: \begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)] \item the map $(x, y) \mapsto (2x + 5y + 1, x + 3y)$, \item the map $(x, y) \mapsto (x + y^2, y + x^2)$, and \item the map given in polar coordinates as $(r, \theta) \mapsto (r, \theta + r^3)$ for $r \ne 0$, with the origin mapping to the origin. \end{enumerate} The number of maps in the above list that preserve areas is: \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)] \item $0$ \item $1$ \item $2$ \item $3$. \end{enumerate}
