CCMA-10

B.Sc. Mathematics · Semester IV2024
  1. 1 Answer \emph{any five} questions. [$5\times2=10$]
  2. 1(a) If $A$ and $B$ are two events such that $P(A)=P(B)=1$, then show that $P(A+B)=P(AB)=1$.
  3. 1(b) What is the probability of an odd sum when two dice are thrown?
  4. 1(c) If $F(x)$ denotes the distribution function of a random variable $X$, then prove that $F(\infty)=1$.
  5. 1(d) Define Poisson process and give an example.
  6. 1(e) If $X$ is a $\beta_2(l,m)$ variate, then show that $Y=1/X$ is a $\beta_2(m,l)$ variate.
  7. 1(f) Define the moment generating function of a random variable $X$ and find it for the binomial $(n,p)$ distribution.
  8. 1(g) Prove that $E(X^2)\ge[E(X)]^2$.
  9. 1(h) If $X$ is a non-negative random variable having mean $m$, prove by using Tchebycheff's inequality that, for any $\tau>0$, $P(X\ge\tau m)\le1/\tau$.
  10. 2(a) Using the axioms of mathematical probability, deduce the classical formula of probability. [3]
  11. 2(b) If $\{A_n\}$ is a monotonic non-decreasing sequence of events, then prove that \[P(\lim_{n\to\infty}A_n)=\lim_{n\to\infty}P(A_n).\] [3]
  12. 2(c) Two urns contain respectively 3 white, 7 red, 15 black balls, and 10 white, 6 red, 9 black balls. One ball is drawn from each urn. Find the probability that both the balls are of the same colour. [4]
  13. 3(a) Define stochastic independence of two events. Show that if two events $A$ and $B$ are independent, then $A$ and $\bar B$ are independent. [2]
  14. 3(b) State and prove Bayes' theorem. [2+3]
  15. 3(c) The chance that a doctor will diagnose a certain disease correctly is 60\%. The chance that a patient will die under his/her treatment after correct diagnosis is 40\% and the chance of death by wrong diagnosis is 70\%. A patient of the doctor who had the disease died. What is the chance that his/her disease was diagnosed correctly? [3]
  16. 4(a) If $F(x)$ denotes the distribution function of a random variable $X$, then show that (i) $P(a<X<b)=F(b-0)-F(a)$ and (ii) $P(a\le X\le b)=F(b)-F(a-0)$. [3]
  17. 4(b) Find the value of the constant $k$ such that \[f(x)=\begin{cases}kx(1-x)&0<x<1\\0&\text{elsewhere}\end{cases}\] is a probability density function corresponding to a random variable $X$. Find the distribution function of $X$ and compute $P(X>1/2)$. [1+2+1]
  18. 4(c) A point $P$ is taken at random on a line segment $AB$ of length $2a$. Find the probability that the area of the rectangle $AP.PB$ will exceed $a^2/2$. [3]
  19. 5(a) The joint probability density function of the random variables $X$ and $Y$ is given by \[f(x,y)=\begin{cases}k(3x+y)&1<x<3,\ 0<y<2,\\0&\text{elsewhere.}\end{cases}\] Find the value of the constant $k$ and calculate $P(X+Y<2)$. Examine if $X$ and $Y$ are independent. [1+2+2]
  20. 5(b) If $X_1,X_2,\ldots,X_n$ are mutually independent random variables each uniformly distributed over $(0,1)$, prove that the probability density function of $X=X_1X_2\cdots X_n$ is \[\frac1{(n-1)!}\left[\log\left(\frac1x\right)\right]^{n-1},\quad(0<x<1).\] Hence deduce the probability density function of the geometric mean $(X_1X_2\cdots X_n)^{1/n}$. [3+2]
  21. 6(a) If $X$ is a continuous random variable having spectrum $-\infty<x<\infty$ and distribution function $F(x)$, then show that \[E(X)=\int_0^\infty G(x)\,dx,\] provided $xG(x)\to0$ as $x\to\infty$, where $G(x)=1-F(x)-F(-x)$. [4]
  22. 6(b) Show that the characteristic function for the Pascal distribution, given by, \[f_i=(1-\lambda)\lambda^i,\quad i=0,1,2,\ldots,\] is $[1-\mu(e^{it}-1)]^{-1}$, where $\mu=\lambda/(1-\lambda)>0$. [3]
  23. 6(c) Find the mode of a binomial $(4,1/4)$ variate. [3]
  24. 7(a) Define correlation coefficient $\rho(X,Y)$ between two random variables $X$ and $Y$. If $X$ and $Y$ are uncorrelated, find the correlation coefficient between the linear combinations $a_1X+b_1Y$ and $a_2X+b_2Y$. [1+2]
  25. 7(b) The probability density function of a continuous bivariate distribution is given by \[f(x,y)=\begin{cases}x+y&0<x<1,\ 0<y<1,\\0&\text{elsewhere.}\end{cases}\] Find the regression curves for the means of $X$ and $Y$. [2+2]
  26. 7(c) If, for any pair of linearly dependent random variables $X$ and $Y$, we set $U=X\cos\alpha+Y\sin\alpha$ and $V=-X\sin\alpha+Y\cos\alpha$, then prove that $V$ will be constant (i.e., it has a one-point distribution) if $\tan\alpha=\rho\sigma_y/\sigma_x$. Here, $\alpha$ is a constant and $\sigma_x$ ($\sigma_y$) is the standard deviation of $X$ ($Y$). [3]
  27. 8(a) When is a sequence of random variables $\{X_n\}$ said to be asymptotically normal? State the Central limit theorem for the case of equal components and show that it implies the law of large numbers. [1+2+3]
  28. 8(b) State the limit theorem for characteristic functions and use it to show that the Poisson distribution is a limit of the Binomial distribution. [2+2]
  29. 9(a) Define convergence in probability for a sequence of random variables $\{X_n\}$ and state the law of large numbers for the case of equal components. [1+2]
  30. 9(b) If $f$ denotes the frequency ratio of successes in a Bernoullian sequence of $n$ trials with probability of success $p$, then prove that \[f\xrightarrow{\mathrm{in}\ p}p\quad\text{as }n\to\infty.\] [3]
  31. 9(c) Verify Tchebycheff's inequality for the probability distribution of a random variable $X$, given by, \[P(X=-1)=P(X=1)=\frac18,\quad P(X=0)=\frac34.\] \renewcommand{\paperfull}{25} \renewcommand{\meaningplural}{} [4]
