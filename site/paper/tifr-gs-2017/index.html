TIFR Graduate School · GS 2017 · Mathematics

  1. 1 Let $f:[0,1] \to \mathbb{R}$ be a continuous function such that $f(x) \ge x^3$ for all $x \in [0,1]$ with $\int_0^1 f(x)\,dx = \frac{1}{4}$. Then $f(x) = x^3$ for all $x \in \mathbb{R}$.
  2. 2 Suppose $a, b, c$ are positive real numbers such that \[ (1 + a + b + c)\left(1 + \frac{1}{a} + \frac{1}{b} + \frac{1}{c}\right) = 16. \] Then $a + b + c = 3$.
  3. 3 There exists a function $f:\mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ satisfying $f(-1) = -1$, $f(1) = 1$ and $|f(x) - f(y)| \le |x - y|^{3/2}$ for all $x, y \in \mathbb{R}$.
  4. 4 Over the real line, \[ \lim_{x \to \infty} \log\left(1 + \sqrt{4 + x} - \sqrt{1 + x}\right) = \log(2). \]
  5. 5 Suppose $f$ is a continuously differentiable function on $\mathbb{R}$ such that $f(x) \to 1$ and $f'(x) \to b$ as $x \to \infty$. Then $b = 1$.
  6. 6 If $f:\mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ is differentiable and bijective, then $f^{-1}$ is also differentiable.
  7. 7 Let $H_1, H_2, H_3, H_4$ be four hyperplanes in $\mathbb{R}^3$. The maximum possible number of connected components of $\mathbb{R}^3 - (H_1 \cup H_2 \cup H_3 \cup H_4)$ is $14$.
  8. 8 Let $n \ge 2$ be a natural number. Let $S$ be the set of all $n \times n$ real matrices whose entries are only $0$, $1$ or $2$. Then the average determinant of a matrix in $S$ is greater than or equal to $1$.
  9. 9 For any metric space $(X, d)$ with $X$ finite, there exists an isometric embedding $f: X \to \mathbb{R}^4$.
  10. 10 There exists a non-negative continuous function $f:[0,1] \to \mathbb{R}$ such that $\int_0^1 f^n\,dx \to 2$ as $n \to \infty$.
  11. 11 There exists a subset $A$ of $\mathbb{N}$ with exactly five elements such that the sum of any three elements of $A$ is a prime number.
  12. 12 There exists a finite abelian group $G$ containing exactly $60$ elements of order $2$.
  13. 13 Let $\alpha, \beta$ be complex numbers with non-positive real parts. Then $|e^\alpha - e^\beta| \le |\alpha - \beta|$.
  14. 14 Every $2 \times 2$-matrix over $\mathbb{C}$ is a square of some matrix.
  15. 15 Under the projection map $\mathbb{R}^2 \to \mathbb{R}$ sending $(x, y)$ to $x$, the image of any closed set is closed.
  16. 16 The number of ways a $2 \times 8$ rectangle can be tiled with rectangular tiles of size $2 \times 1$ is $34$.
  17. 17 Over the real line, \[ \lim_{x \to \infty} \left(\frac{x + \log 9}{x - \log 9}\right)^x = 81. \]
  18. 18 Let $f:[0,\infty) \to \mathbb{R}$ be a continuous function with $\lim_{x \to \infty} f(x) = 0$. Then $f$ has a maximum value in $[0,\infty)$.
  19. 19 Given a continuous function $f:\mathbb{Q} \to \mathbb{Q}$, there exists a continuous function $g:\mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ such that the restriction of $g$ to $\mathbb{Q}$ is $f$.
  20. 20 For all positive integers $m$ and $n$, if $A$ is an $m \times n$ real matrix, and $B$ is an $n \times m$ real matrix such that $AB = I$, then $BA = I$.
  21. 21 There is a continuous onto function $f: S^2 \to S^1$ from the unit sphere in $\mathbb{R}^3$ to the unit sphere in $\mathbb{R}^2$, where $S^n = \{v \in \mathbb{R}^{n+1} \mid \|v\| = 1\}$ denotes the unit sphere in $\mathbb{R}^{n+1}$.
  22. 22 Let $P$ be a monic, non-zero, polynomial of even degree, and $K > 0$. Then the function $P(x) - K e^x$ has a real zero.
  23. 23 A $p$-Sylow subgroup of the underlying additive group of a finite commutative ring $R$ is an ideal in $R$.
  24. 24 Suppose $A$ is an $n \times n$ real matrix, all whose eigenvalues have absolute value less than $1$. Then for any $v \in \mathbb{R}^n$, $\|Av\| \le \|v\|$.
  25. 25 For any $x \in \mathbb{R}$, the sequence $\{a_n\}$, where $a_1 = x$ and $a_{n+1} = \cos(a_n)$ for all $n$, is convergent.
  26. 26 Suppose $A_1, \dots, A_m$ are distinct $n \times n$ real matrices such that $A_i A_j = 0$ for all $i \ne j$. Then $m \le n$.
  27. 27 In the symmetric group $S_n$ any two elements of the same order are conjugate.
  28. 28 If a particle moving on the Euclidean line traverses distance $1$ in time $1$ starting and ending at rest, then at some time $t \in [0,1]$, the absolute value of its acceleration should be at least $4$.
  29. 29 Let $y(t)$ be a real valued function defined on the real line such that $y' = y(1 - y)$, with $y(0) \in [0,1]$. Then $\lim_{t \to \infty} y(t) = 1$.
  30. 30 The matrices \[ \begin{pmatrix} x & 0 \\ 0 & y \end{pmatrix} \quad \text{and} \quad \begin{pmatrix} x & 1 \\ 0 & y \end{pmatrix}, \qquad x \ne y, \] for any $x, y \in \mathbb{R}$ are conjugate in $M_2(\mathbb{R})$.
  31. 1 Show that the subset $GL_n(\mathbb{R})$ of $M_n(\mathbb{R})$ consisting of all invertible matrices is dense in $M_n(\mathbb{R})$.
  32. 2 Let $f$ be a continuous function on $\mathbb{R}$ satisfying the relation $f(f(f(x))) = x$ for all $x \in \mathbb{R}$. Prove or disprove that $f$ is the identity function.
  33. 3 Prove or disprove: the group of positive rationals under multiplication is isomorphic to its subgroup consisting of rationals which can be expressed as $p/q$ where both $p$ and $q$ are odd positive integers.
  34. 4 Show that the only elements in $M_n(\mathbb{R})$ commuting with every idempotent matrix are the scalar matrices. (A matrix $P$ in $M_n(\mathbb{R})$ is said to be idempotent if $P^2 = P$.)
  35. 5 Prove or disprove the following: let $f: X \to X$ be a continuous function from a complete metric space $(X, d)$ into itself such that $d(f(x), f(y)) < d(x, y)$ whenever $x \ne y$. Then $f$ has a fixed point.
  36. 6 How many isomorphism classes of associative rings (with identity) are there with $35$ elements? Prove your answer.
  37. 7 Prove or disprove: If $G$ is a finite group and $g, h \in G$, then $g, h$ have the same order if and only if there exists a group $H$ containing $G$ such that $g$ and $h$ are conjugate in $H$.
  38. 8 Prove or disprove: there exists $A \subset \mathbb{N}$ with exactly five elements, such that the sum of any three elements of $A$ is a prime number.
  39. 9 Show that there does not exist any continuous function $f: \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ that takes every value exactly twice.
  40. 10 For which positive integers $n$ does there exist an $\mathbb{R}$-linear ring homomorphism $f: \mathbb{C} \to M_n(\mathbb{R})$? Justify your answer.
