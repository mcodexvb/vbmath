BMC-61

B.Sc. Mathematics · Semester VI2017
  1. 1(a) Test the continuity at the origin of the function $f(z)$ defined by $f(z)=\frac{x^2y^2}{x^2y^2+(x-y)^2}$ for $z\ne0$; $=0$ for $z=0$. [2]
  2. 1(b) If $f(z)$ is differentiable at $\alpha\in\mathbb C$, then show that it is continuous at $\alpha$. Show by an example with proper justification that its converse may not be true. [2]
  3. 1(c) Verify whether $f(z)=|z|^2$ is differentiable at any point $z\in\mathbb C$. [2]
  4. 1(d) If a function $f(z)=u(x,y)+iv(x,y)$ is differentiable within a region $D$ show that $\frac{\partial u}{\partial x}=\frac{\partial v}{\partial y}$ and $\frac{\partial u}{\partial y}=-\frac{\partial v}{\partial x}$ at every point in $D$. [4]
  5. 2(a) Define a harmonic function. Find a harmonic conjugate $v(x,y)$ when $u(x,y)=\sinh x\sin y$. [1+2]
  6. 2(b) If $f(z)$ be regular in a region $D$ and $|f(z)|$ be constant in $D$, then show that $f(z)$ itself is constant in D. [2]
  7. 2(c) State and prove Cauchy's general principle of convergence of a sequence of complex numbers. [1+3]
  8. 2(d) Show by an example that there may be a convergent series of complex numbers which is not absolutely convergent. [1]
  9. 3(a) Find the Bilinear transformation which transforms the points $1,0,\infty$ in the $z$-plane to the points $-1,i,-i$ respectively in the $w$-plane. [2]
  10. 3(b) Show that the equation $\left|\frac{z-\alpha}{z-\beta}\right|=l\,(l\ne1)$ represents a circle with respect to which $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are inverse points. Further show that by a Möbius transformation a circle transforms into a circle or a straight line. [3+2]
  11. 3(c) State orientation principle. [1]
  12. 3(d) Find the stereographic projection of a circle in the complex plane whose equation is given as $x^2+y^2+2gx+2fy+c=0$. [2]
  13. 1(a) Prove that $C[a,b]$, the set of all real valued continuous functions on $[a,b]$, is a metric space with respect to a metric defined by you. [3]
  14. 1(b) Let $(X,d)$ be a metric space. Then show that the union of an arbitrary family of open sets in $X$ is open in $X$. [2]
  15. 2(a) If $x$ and $y$ are distinct points in a metric space $(X,d)$, then show that there is $r>0$ such that $S_r(x)\cap S_r(y)=\varphi$. [2]
  16. 2(b) Show that a set $F$ in a metric space $(X,d)$ is closed if and only if $F'\subset F$, where $F'$ is the derived set of $F$. [2]
  17. 2(c) Give an example to show that $(A\cap B)'$ may not be equal to $A'\cap B'$ in a metric space. [1]
  18. 3(a) Show that for any subset $A$ of a metric space $(X,d)$, $X-\overline A=(X-A)^\circ$. [$\overline A$ and $A^\circ$ respectively denote the closure and interior of $A$]. [1.5]
  19. 3(b) Show that for any subset $A$ of a metric space $(X,d)$, $Diam(A)=Diam(\overline A)$. [2.5]
  20. 3(c) Give an example to show that distance between two disjoint sets may be zero. [1]
  21. 4(a) Let $\{x_n\},\{y_n\}$ be sequences in a metric space $(X,d)$ such that $x_n\to x$ in $X$. Then show that $y_n\to x$ in $X$ if and only if $d(x_n,y_n)\to0$ in $\mathbb R$. [2]
  22. 4(b) Show that every Cauchy sequence in a metric space is bounded. [2]
  23. 4(c) Give an example of an incomplete metric space. [1]
  24. 5(a) State and prove Cantor's intersection theorem. [3]
  25. 5(b) Show that a set $A$ in a metric space $(X,d)$ is nowhere dense if and only if $X-\overline A$ is dense in $X$. [1]
  26. 5(c) Give an example with justification of a set of first category which is nowhere dense. [1]
  27. 6(a) Let $(X,d)$ be complete metric space and $f:X\to X$ be a map. If for some positive integer $n$, $f^n$ is a contraction map on $X$, then show that $f$ has a unique fixed point in $X$. [3]
  28. 6(b) Give an example with justifications of a weak contraction map which is not contraction. [2]
