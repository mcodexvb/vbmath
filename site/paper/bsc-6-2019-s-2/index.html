BMC-62

B.Sc. Mathematics · Semester VI2019
  1. 1 Answer \textit{any four} questions. [$4\times5=20$]
  2. 1(a) Let $H$ be a subgroup of a group $G$. If for each $a\in G$, there exists $b\in G$ such that $aH=Hb$, then prove that $H$ is a normal subgroup of $G$. Show that the quotient group $\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z$ is an infinite group but each of its elements is of finite order.
  3. 1(b) Let $f:G\to G'$ be a group homomorphism. If $N$ is a normal subgroup of $G'$ then show that $f^{-1}(N)$ is also normal in $G$.\\If moreover, $f$ is one-to-one then show that $0(a)=a(f(a))$ for every $a\in G$. [2+3]
  4. 1(c) Find all homomorphisms from $(\mathbb Z_6,+)$ into $(\mathbb Z_8,+)$. Also show that there does not exist any isomorphism from the group $(\mathbb R,+)$ to the group $(\mathbb R^*,\cdot)$. [3+2]
  5. 1(d) Define $f:\mathbb C^*\to\mathbb R^+$ by $f(z)=|z|$ for every $z\in\mathbb C^*$. Consider the equivalence relation $\rho$ on $\mathbb C^*$ by : $z\rho w$ if $f(z)=f(w)$. Find the $\rho$-equivalence class of $2+3i$. Also find the inverse of $(2+3i)\rho$ in the group $\mathbb C^*/\rho$ of all $\rho$-equivalence classes of $\mathbb C^*$. Find a commonly known group to which the group $\mathbb C^*/\rho$ is isomorphic. [2+1+2]
  6. 1(e) State and prove the second isomorphism theorem. If there exists an epimorphism from a finite group $G$ onto the group $\mathbb Z_{15}$, then show that $G$ has a normal subgroup of index 5. [3+2]
  7. 1(f) Show that every commutative group of order 6 is cyclic. Find all subgroups of $\mathbb Z/24\mathbb Z$. [3+2]
  8. 2 Answer \textit{any one} question. [$1\times5=5$]
  9. 2(a) Show that the set $I=\{a+b\sqrt3\in\mathbb Z[\sqrt3]\mid a-b\text{ is an even integer}\}$ is an ideal in the ring $\mathbb Z[\sqrt3]$. List all distinct cosets of $I$ in $\mathbb Z[\sqrt3]$. [2+3]
  10. 2(b) Find all ring homomorphisms from $\mathbb Z_{12}$ into $\mathbb Z_{30}$. Show that the fields $\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)$ and $\mathbb Q(\sqrt3)$ are not isomorphic. [3+2]
  11. 3 Answer \textit{any three} questions. [$3\times5=15$]
  12. 3(a) State and prove the rank-nullity theorem for linear transformations. Find the rank of a linear transformation $T:\mathbb R^3\to\mathbb R^3$, given that $\ker T$ is the $x$-axis in $\mathbb R^3$. [4+1]
  13. 3(b) Show that a linear transformation $T:U\to V$ is a projection if and only if $T^2=T$. Define the projection of $\mathbb R^2$ on $x$-axis along $y=2x$ line. [3+2]
  14. 3(c) Define $T:P_2(\mathbb R)\to M_{2\times2}(\mathbb R)$ by $T(p(x))=\begin{pmatrix}f'(0)&2f(1)\\0&f''(3)\end{pmatrix}$. Find $[T]_\alpha^\beta$ where $\alpha=\{1,x,x^2\}$ and $\beta=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix}\right\}$. Find $\ker T$ and nullity of $T$. [3+2]
  15. 3(d) Prove that a matrix $A\in M_{n\times n}(F)$ is diagonalizable if and only if $F^n$ has a basis of eigenvectors of $A$. Is $A=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-0&0\end{pmatrix}\in M_{2\times2}(\mathbb R)$ diagonalizable? [4+1]
  16. 3(e) Let $\alpha=\{(1,-1,1),(0,1,1),(0,1,-1)\}$. Given that $T:\mathbb R^3\to\mathbb R^3$ is a linear transformation such that $[T]_\alpha^\alpha=\begin{pmatrix}1&2&3\\0&-1&1\\1&3&-2\end{pmatrix}$. Find $[T(-2,1,0)]_\alpha$. Is $T$ invertible? Find $[T^{-1}]_\alpha^\alpha$. What is the nullity of $T$? \fbox{\parbox{3.4cm}{\small Use separate answer script for each unit}} [2+2+1]
