MJMA-10

B.Sc. Mathematics · Semester IV2026
  1. 1 Answer \textit{any five} questions (Choose the correct one). [$5\times2=10$]
  2. 1(a) The probability of occurrence of exactly one of the events $A$ and $B$ is equal to (a) $P(A)+P(B)$ (b) $P(A)+P(B)-P(AB)$ (c) $P(A)+P(B)-2P(AB)$ (d) $P(\bar A)+P(\bar B)$.
  3. 1(b) If $A$ and $B$ are two events such that $P(\bar A)=P(\bar B)=0$, then $P(A+B)$ is equal to (a) 1 (b) 0 (c) 1/2 (d) none of these.
  4. 1(c) Let $A_n=(a<X\le a+1/n)$, where $a$ is a constant and $n=1,2,\dots$. Then (a) the sequence $\{A_n\}$ is monotone non-increasing with $P\left(\lim_{n\to\infty}A_n\right)=0$ (b) the limit $\lim_{n\to\infty}A_n$ does not exist. (c) the sequence $\{A_n\}$ is monotone non-decreasing with $P\left(\lim_{n\to\infty}A_n\right)=1$ (d) the limit, $\lim_{n\to\infty}A_n$ exists and it is equal to $X=a$.
  5. 1(d) Let $X$ be a random variable with the probability density function, defined by, $f(x)=x+1/2$ in $0\le x\le1$ and $0$ elsewhere. Then $P(-1<X\le1/2)$ is equal to (a) 3/4 (b) 3/8 (c) 1 (d) 1/4.
  6. 1(e) If $F(x)$ is the distribution function of a random variable $X$, then $P(a<X\le b)$ is equal to (a) $F(b-0)-F(a)$ (b) $F(b-0)-F(a-0)$ (c) $F(b)-F(a)$ (d) $F(b)-F(a+0)$.
  7. 1(f) The value of the constant $k$ for which the function $f(x)$, defined by, $f(x)=kx(1-x)$ in $0<x<1$ and $0$ elsewhere, is a probability density function, is (a) $-1$ (b) 6 (c) 2 (d) 1/3.
  8. 1(g) Which of the following is not a measure of dispersion? (a) Mean deviation (b) Variance (c) Coefficient of skewness (d) Coefficient of kurtosis.
  9. 1(h) The mean of a binomial $(n,p)$ distribution is (a) $p$ (b) $np$ (c) $n(n-1)p$ (d) $(n-1)p$.
  10. 2(a) Define stochastic independence of two events $A$ and $B$. Deduce that $P(\bar A+B)=1-P(A)+P(AB)$ [2+3]
  11. 2(b) Two urns contain, respectively, 3 white, 7 red, 15 black balls and 10 white, 6 red, 9 black balls. One ball is drawn from each urn. Find the probability that both the balls are of the same color. [5]
  12. 3(a) Define Bernoulli's sequence of trials. Find the probability of $i$ successes in a Bernoulli's sequence of $n$ trials. [2+3]
  13. 3(b) The probability of hitting a target is $1/5$. If 10 shots are fired, find the probability of at least 2 hits. Find also the minimum number of shots to be fired in order that the probability of hitting the target at least once exceeds $1/2$. [5]
  14. 4(a) Let $F(x)$ be a distribution function. Prove that for any fixed $h\ne0$, the function $G(x)=\frac1{2h}\int_{x-h}^{x+h}F(t)dt$ is also a distribution function. [6]
  15. 4(b) A point $X$ is chosen at random on a line segment $AB$ whose middle point is $O$. Find the probability that $AX$, $BX$, and $AO$ can form the sides of a triangle. [4]
  16. 5(a) A point is chosen at random on a line segment $AB$ of length $2a$. Calculate the expected values of the rectangle $AP.PB$ and the difference $|AP-PB|$. [4]
  17. 5(b) For the binomial $(n,p)$ distribution, prove that $\mu_{k+1}=p(1-p)\left(nk\mu_{k-1}+\frac{d\mu_k}{dp}\right)$, and hence obtain $\gamma_1$. [6]
  18. 6(a) Define convergence in probability. [2]
  19. 6(b) State Tchebycheff's inequality. Verify it for the distribution of a random variable $X$, given by, $P(X=-1)=P(X=1)=1/8$, $P(X=0)=3/4$. [2+3]
  20. 6(c) If $f$ is the frequency ratio of successes in a Bernoulli's sequence of $n$ trials with probability of success $p$, then prove that $f\xrightarrow{\text{in }p}p$ as $n\to\infty$. [3]
  21. 1(a) Define populations and random samples. What are sample characteristics? Give two examples. [2+2+2]
  22. 1(b) Define a statistic and sampling distribution of a statistic. [1+3]
  23. 2(a) Define an estimate of a population parameter. When is an estimate said to be consistent and unbiased? [2+2]
  24. 2(b) Show that for a normal $(m,\sigma)$ population, the sample mean is a consistent and unbiased estimate of the population mean. [6]
  25. 3(a) Describe the method of maximum likelihood for estimating a continuous population parameter. [3]
  26. 3(b) A population is defined by the density function $f(x;\alpha)=\frac{x^{l-1}e^{-x/\alpha}}{\Gamma(l)\alpha^l}$, $(0<x<\infty)$, where $l$ is a constant. Estimate the parameter $\alpha$ by the method of maximum likelihood, and show that the estimate is consistent and unbiased. [4+3]
  27. 4(a) Define a confidence interval and a confidence coefficient of a population parameter. [2+1]
  28. 4(b) The marks obtained by 17 candidates in an examination have a mean 57 and variance 64. Find 99\% confidence limits for the mean of the population of marks, assuming it to be normal. Given $P(t>2.921)=0.005$. [7]
  29. 5 Define a statistical hypothesis. Also, define (i) Simple hypothesis, (ii) Composite hypothesis, (iii) Null hypothesis, and (iv) Alternative hypothesis, and give examples. [2+8]
  30. 6 State Neyman-Pearson theorem. Using it construct a test of the null hypothesis $H_0:m=m_0$ against an alternative $H_1:m=m_1$ for a normal $(m,\sigma)$ population, where $\sigma$ is known and $m_1>m_0$. [2+8]
