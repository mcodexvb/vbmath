CC-6

Algebra-III
B.Sc. Mathematics · Semester III2021
  1. 1 Answer \textbf{\textit{any two}} questions. \((10\times2=20)\)
  2. 1(a) \begin{enumerate} \renewcommand{\labelenumi}{\roman{enumi}.} \item Show that the elements in \(A_n\) are product of 3-cycles. Let \(\beta\in S_7\) and suppose \(\beta^4=(2143567)\). Find \(\beta\). \item Find group elements \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\) in \(S_5\) such that \(|\alpha|=3,\ |\beta|=3,\) and \(|\alpha\beta|=5\). \item Show that in \(S_n(n\ge4)\), the equation \(x^2=(1234)\) has no solutions. \end{enumerate}
  3. 1(b) \begin{enumerate} \renewcommand{\labelenumi}{\roman{enumi}.} \item Describe and discuss the natures of all subgroups of the noncommutative group \(Q_8\). Are there any similarities with the subgroups of an abelian group? Justify \item Let \(G\) be a group and \([G:Z(G)]=4\), then find the orders of the elements of the group \(G/Z(G)\). Can you find any similarities with the Kelin's 4-group? Justify \item Show, by example, that in a quotient group \(G/H\) it can happen that \(aH=bH\) but \(O(a)\ne O(b)\). \end{enumerate}
  4. 1(c) \begin{enumerate} \renewcommand{\labelenumi}{\roman{enumi}.} \item Let \(G\) be a finite group. Then prove that \(a^{|G|}=e\) for all \(a\in G\). Suppose that \(K\) is a proper subgroup of \(H\) and \(H\) is a proper subgroup of \(G\). If \(|K|=42\) and \(|G|=420\), what are the possible orders of \(H\)? \item Let \(H\) be a subgroup of \(R^*\), the group of nonzero real numbers under multiplication. If \(R^+\subseteq H\subseteq R^*\), prove that either \(H=R^+\) or \(H=R^*\). \item Prove that the order of the group \(U_n\) is even when \(n>2\). \end{enumerate} [2]
  5. 2 Answer \textbf{\textit{any two}} questions. \((10\times2=20)\)
  6. 2(a) Find the group of all units in \(\mathbb Z[i]\). [2]
  7. 2(b) Show that every Boolean ring is commutative. Give example of a nonzero Boolean ring other than \(\mathbb Z_2\) and products of \(\mathbb Z_2\). [3]
  8. 2(c) Let \(R\) be a commutative ring. If \(u\in R\) is a unit and \(a\in R\) is a nilpotent element, then show that \(u+a\) is a unit in \(R\). [3]
  9. 2(d) Let \(R\) be a ring such that \(x^3=x\) for all \(x\in R\). Show that the characteristic of \(R\) is finite and is a divisor of 6. [2]
  10. 3(a) Give an example with justification of a division ring which is not a field. [2]
  11. 3(b) Prove that the characteristic of a finite field is a prime integer. [3]
  12. 3(c) Show that there is no integral domains of six elements. [3]
  13. 3(d) Let \(R\) be a ring with 1 such that any two nonzero elements in \(R\) is nonzero. Show that for every \(a,b\in R,\ ab=1\) if and only if \(ba=1\). [2]
  14. 4(a) Find the smallest subring of \(\mathbb R\) containing \(\sqrt[3]{2}\). [2]
  15. 4(b) Let \(R\) be a commutative ring with 1. If \(R\) is simple then show that it is a field. Give an example of a simple noncommutative ring with 1. [3]
  16. 4(c) Show that \(12\mathbb Z+28\mathbb Z=4\mathbb Z\). [2]
  17. 4(d) Show that \(I=\{a+ib\in\mathbb Z[i]\mid a\text{ and }b\text{ are even}\}\) is an ideal of the ring \(\mathbb Z[i]\) of Gaussian integers. Find the number of elements of the quotient ring \(\mathbb Z[i]/I\). [3]
  18. 5 Answer \textbf{\textit{any two}} questions. \((2\times10=20)\)
  19. 5(a) \begin{enumerate} \renewcommand{\labelenumi}{\roman{enumi}.} \item Show that every field is a vector space over a subfield of it. Give an example with proper justification to show that a subfield may not be a vector space over its field. \item Show that the set \(W=\{(a_1,a_2,a_3)\in\mathbb R^3:2a_1-7a_2+a_3=0\}\) forms a subspace of \(\mathbb R^3\). Hence find a subspace \(W'\) of \(\mathbb R^3\) such that \(\mathbb R^3=W\oplus W'\). \end{enumerate}
  20. 5(b) \begin{enumerate} \renewcommand{\labelenumi}{\roman{enumi}.} \item Let \(W=\{A\in M_{2\times2}(\mathbb R):\operatorname{Trace}(A)=0\}\). Find a smallest generating set for \(W\). \item Give an example of a linearly independent set having more than one element for the vector space \(P(\{1,2,3\})\) over the field \(\mathbb Z_2\). \item Let \(S\) be a linearly independent subset of a vector space \(V\), and let \(v\) be a vector in \(V\) that is not in \(S\). Then show that \(S\cup\{v\}\) is linearly dependent if and only if \(v\in\operatorname{span}(S)\). \end{enumerate} [4]
  21. 5(c) \begin{enumerate} \renewcommand{\labelenumi}{\roman{enumi}.} \item Let \(V\) be finite dimensional vector space over \(F\). Then show that every linearly independent subset of \(V\) can be extended to a basis of \(V\). \item Find the dimension of the subspace \(W_1+W_2\) of \(\mathbb R^3\) where \(W_1=\{(a,b,c):a+b+c=0\}\) and \(W_2=\{(a,b,c):a=b=c\}\) are two subspaces of \(\mathbb R^3\). \end{enumerate} [6]
