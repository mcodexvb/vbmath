IIT JAM · Mathematics (MA), 2010

  1. Which of the following conditions does NOT ensure the convergence of a real sequence $\left\{a_{n}\right\}$ ? \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)] \item $\left|a_{n}-a_{n+1}\right| \rightarrow 0$ as $n \rightarrow \infty$ \item $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left|a_{n}-a_{n+1}\right|$ is convergent \item $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} n a_{n}$ is convergent \item The sequences $\left\{a_{2 n}\right\},\left\{a_{2 n+1}\right\}$ and $\left\{a_{3 n}\right\}$ are convergent \end{enumerate}
  2. The value of $\iint_{G} \frac{\ln \left(x^{2}+y^{2}\right)}{x^{2}+y^{2}} d x d y$, where $G=\left\{(x, y) \in \mathbf{R}^{2}: 1 \leq x^{2}+y^{2} \leq e^{2}\right\}$, is \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)] \item $\pi$ \item $ 2 \pi$ \item $3 \pi$ \item $4 \pi$ \end{enumerate}
  3. The number of elements of $S_{5}$ (the symmetric group on 5 letters) which are their own inverses equals \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)] \item 10 \item 11 \item $ 25$ \item 26 \end{enumerate}
  4. Let $S$ be an infinite subset of $\mathbf{R}$ such that $S \cap \mathbf{Q}=\phi$. Which of the following statements is true? \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)] \item $S$ must have a limit point which belongs 10 \item S must have a limit point which belongs $1 \mathbf{R} \backslash \mathbf{Q}$ \item $S$ cannot be a closed set in $\mathbf{R}$ \item $\mathbf{R} \backslash S$ must have a limit point which belongs to $S$ \end{enumerate}
  5. Let $f:(1,4) \rightarrow \mathbf{R}$ be a uniformly continuous function and let $\left\{a_{n}\right\}$ be a Cauchy sequence in $(1,2)$. Let $x_{n}=\alpha_{n}^{2} f\left(a_{n}^{2}\right)$ and $y_{n}=\frac{1}{1+a_{n}^{2}} f\left(a_{n}^{2}\right)$, for all $n \in \mathbf{N}$. Which of the following statements is true? \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)] \item Both $\left\{x_{n}\right\}$ and $\left\{y_{n}\right\}$ must be Cauchy sequences in $\mathbf{R}$ \item $\left\{x_{n}\right\}$ must be a Cauchy sequence in $\mathbf{R}$ but $\left\{y_{n}\right\}$ need not be a Cauchy sequence in $\mathbf{R}$ \item $\left\{y_{n}\right\}$ must be a Cauchy sequence in $R$ but $\left\{x_{n}\right\}$ need not be a Cauchy sequence in $R$ \item Neither $\left\{x_{n}\right\}$ nor $\left\{y_{n}\right\}$ needs to be a Cauchy sequence in $\mathbf{R}$ \end{enumerate}
  6. Let $\vec{F}=2 x y z e^{x^{2}} \hat{i}+z e^{x^{2}} \hat{j}+y e^{x^{2}} \hat{k}$ be the gradient of a scalar function. The value of $\int_{L} \vec{F} \cdot \overrightarrow{d r}$ along the oriented path $L$ from $(0,0,0)$ to $(1,0,2)$ and then to $(1,1,2)$ is \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)] \item 0 \item $2 e$ \item $e$ \item $e^{2}$ \end{enumerate}
  7. Let $\vec{F}=x y \hat{i}+y \hat{j}-y z \hat{k}$ denote the force field on a particle traversing the path $L$ from $(0,0,0)$ to $(1,1,1)$ along the curve of intersection of the cylinder $y=x^{2}$ and the plane $z=x$. The work done by $F^{\prime}$ is \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)] \item 0 \item $\frac{1}{4}$ \item $\frac{1}{2}$ \item 1 \end{enumerate}
  8. Let $\mathbf{R}[X]$ be the ring of real polynomials iu the variable $X$. The number of ideals in the quotient ring $\mathbf{R}[X] /\left(X^{2}-3 X+2\right)$ is \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)] \item 2 \item 3 \item 4 \item 6 \end{enumerate}
  9. Consider the differential equation $\frac{d y}{d x}=a y-b y^{2}$, where $a, b>0$ and $y(0)=y_{0}$. As $x \rightarrow+\infty$, the solution $y(x)$ tends to \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)] \item 0 \item $\frac{a}{b}$ \item $\frac{b}{a}$ \item $y_{0}$ \end{enumerate}
  10. Consider the differential equation $(x+y+1) d x+(2 x+2 y+1) d y=0$. Which of the following statements is true? \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)] \item The differential equation is linear \item The differential equation is exact \item $e^{x+y}$ is an integrating factor of the differential equation \item A suitable substitution transforms the differentiable equation to the variables separable form \end{enumerate}
  11. Let $T: \mathbf{R}^{2} \rightarrow \mathbf{R}^{2}$ be a linear transformation such that $T((1,2))=(2,3)$ and $T((0,1))=(1,4)$. Then $T((5,6))$ is \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)] \item $(6,-1)$ \item $(-6,1)$ \item $(-1,6)$ \item $(1,-6)$ \end{enumerate}
  12. The number of $2 \times 2$ matrices over $\mathbf{Z}_{3}$ (the field with three elements) with determinant 1 is \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)] \item 24 \item 60 \item 20 \item 30 \end{enumerate}
  13. The radius of convergence of the power series $\sum_{n=0}^{n} a_{n} 2^{n^{2}}$, where $a_{0}=1, a_{n}=3^{-n} a_{n-1}$ for $n \in \mathbf{N}$, is \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)] \item 0 \item $\sqrt{3}$ \item 3 \item $\quad \infty$ \end{enumerate}
  14. Let $T: \mathbf{R}^{3} \rightarrow \mathbf{R}^{3}$ be the linear transformation whose matrix with respect to the standard basis $\left\{e_{1}, e_{2}, e_{9}\right\}$ of $\mathbf{R}^{3}$ is $\left(\begin{array}{lll}0 & 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 1 & 0 & 0\end{array}\right)$. Then $T$ \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)] \item maps the subspace spanned by $e_{1}$ and $e_{2}$ into itself \item has distinct eigenvalues \item has eigenvectors that span $\mathbf{R}^{3}$ \item has a non-zero null space \end{enumerate}
  15. Let $T: \mathbf{R}^{3} \rightarrow \mathbf{R}^{9}$ be the linear transformation whose matrix with respect to the standard basis of $\mathbf{R}^{3}$ is $\left(\begin{array}{ccc}0 & a & b \\ -a & 0 & c \\ -b & -c & 0\end{array}\right)$, where $a, b, c$ are real numbers not all zero. Then $T$ \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)] \item is one-to-one \item is onto \item does not map any line through the origin onto itself \item has rank 1 \end{enumerate}
  16. \begin{enumerate} \item Obtain the general solution of the following system of differential equations: $$ \begin{aligned} &\frac{d x}{d t}=x+2 y \\ &\frac{d y}{d t}=4 x-y+e^{y t} \end{aligned} $$ \item Find the curve passing through $\left(\frac{1}{2}, 0\right)$ and having slope at $(x, y)$ given by the differential equatiou $2\left(1+y^{2}\right) d x+\left(2 x-\tan ^{-1} y\right) d y=0$ \end{enumerate}
  17. \begin{enumerate} \item Find the volume of the region in the first octant bounded by the surfaces $x=0, y=x, y=2-x^{2}, z=0$ and $z=x^{2}$. \item Suppose $f: \mathbf{R} \rightarrow \mathbf{R}$ is a non-constant continuous function satisfying $f(x+y)=f(x) f(y)$ for all $x, y \in \mathbf{R}$. \begin{enumerate} \item Show that $f(x) \neq 0$ for all $x \in \mathbf{R}$. \item Show that $f(x)>0$ for all $x \in \mathbf{R}$. \item Show that there exists $\beta \in \mathbf{R}$ such that $f(x)=\beta^{x}$ for all $x \in \mathbf{R}$. \end{enumerate} \end{enumerate}
  18. \begin{enumerate} \item Let $f(x)$ and $g(x)$ be real valued functions continuous in $\lfloor\alpha, b]$, differentiable in $(a, b)$ and let $\xi^{\prime}(x) \neq 0$ for all $x \in(a, b)$. Show that there exists $c \in(a, b)$ such that $\frac{f(c)-f(a)}{g(b)-g(c)}=\frac{f^{\prime}(c)}{g^{\prime}(c)}$ \item Let $0<\lambda<4$ and let $\left\{a_{n}\right\}$ be a sequence of positive real numbers satisfying $a_{n+1}=\lambda a_{n}^{2}\left(1-a_{n}\right)$ for $n \in \mathbf{N}$. Prove that $\lim _{n \rightarrow-\infty} a_{n}$ exists and determine this limit. \end{enumerate}
  19. Let $G$ be an open subset of $\mathbf{R}$. \begin{enumerate} \item If $0 \notin G$, then show that $H=\{x y: x, y \in G\}$ is an open subset of $\mathbf{R}$. \item If $0 \in G$ and if $x+y \in G$ for all $x, y \in G$, then show that $G=\mathbf{R}$. \end{enumerate}
  20. Let $p(x)$ be a non-constant polynomial with real coefficients such that $p(x) \neq 0$ for all $x \in \mathbf{R}$. Define $f(x)=\frac{1}{p(x)}$ for all $x \in \mathbf{R}$. Prove that \begin{enumerate} \item for each $\varepsilon>0$, there exists $\alpha>0$ such that $|f(x)|<\varepsilon$ for all $x \in \mathbf{R}$ satisfying $|x|>\alpha$, and \item $f: \mathbf{R} \rightarrow \mathbf{R}$ is a uniformly continuous function. \end{enumerate}
  21. \begin{enumerate} Let $M(k)$ and $m(k)$ denote respectively the absolute maximum and the absolute minimum values of $x^{3}+9 x^{2}-21 x+k$ in the closed interval $[-10,2]$. Find all the real values of $k$ for which $|M(k)|=|m(k)|$. \item Let $\alpha_{1}=0, \beta_{1}=1 ; \alpha_{2}=1, \beta_{2}=1$, and for $n \geq 3$, $\alpha_{n}=\alpha_{n-1}+2 \alpha_{n-2}$ $\beta_{n}=\beta_{n-1}+2 \beta_{n-2}$ Prove that, for $n \in \mathbf{N}$ \begin{enumerate} \item $\beta_{n}=2 a_{n}+(-1)^{n-1}$ \item $\alpha_{n}+\beta_{n}=2^{n-1}$ \end{enumerate} Deduce that $\lim _{n \rightarrow \infty} \frac{a_{\mathrm{n}} a+\beta_{n} b}{2^{n-1}}=\frac{a+2 b}{3}$ for any $a, b \in \mathbf{R}$. \end{enumerate}
  22. \begin{enumerate} \item Let $f(x, y)=\alpha x^{2}+x y+\beta y^{2}, \alpha \neq 0, \beta \neq 0,4 \alpha \beta \neq 1$. Find suflicient conditions on $\alpha, \rho$ such that $(0,0)$ is \begin{enumerate} \item a point of local maxima of $f(x, y)$ \item a point of local minima of $f(x, y)$ \item a saddle point of $f(x, y)$. \end{enumerate} \item Find the derivative of $f(x, y, z)=7 x^{3}-x^{2} z-z^{2}+28 y$ at the point $A=(1,-1,0)$ along the unit vector $\frac{1}{7}(6 \hat{i}-2 \hat{j}+3 \hat{k})$. What is the unit vector along which $f$ decreases most rapidly at $A$ ? Also, find the rate of this decrease. \end{enumerate}
  23. Using $x=e^{u}$, transform the differential equation $x^{2} \frac{d^{2} y}{d x^{2}}+4 x \frac{d y}{d x}+2 y=\cos x$ to a second order differential equation with constant coeflicients. Obtein the general solntion of the transformed diflerential equation.
  24. Let $G$ be a group and let $A(G)$ denote the set of all automorphisms of $G$, i.e., all one-to-one, onto, group homomorphisms from $G$ to $G$. An automorphism $f: G \rightarrow G$ of the form $f(x)=a x a^{-1}, x \in G$ (for some $a \in G$ ) is called an inner automorphism. Let $I(G$ ) denote the set of all inner automorphisms of $G$. \begin{enumerate} \item Show that $A(G)$ is a group under composition of functions and that $I(G)$ is a normal subgroup of $A(G)$. \item Show that $I(G)$ is isomorphic to $G / Z(G)$, where $Z(G)=\{g \in G: x g=g x$ for all $x \in G\}$ is the center of $G$. \end{enumerate}
  25. \begin{enumerate} \item Give an example of a linear transformation $T: \mathbf{R}^{2} \rightarrow \mathbf{R}^{2}$ such that $T^{2}(v)=-v$ for all $v \in \mathbf{R}^{2}$. \item Let $V$ be a real n-dimensional vector space and let $T: V \rightarrow V$ be a linear transformation satisfying $T^{2}(v)=-v$ for all $v \in V$. \begin{enumerate} \item Show that $n$ is even. \item Use $T$ to make $V$ into a complex vector space such that the multiplication by complex numbers extends the multiplication by real numbers. \item Show that, with respect to the complex vector space structure on $V$ obtained in (ii), $T: V \rightarrow V$ is a complex linear transformation. \end{enumerate} \end{enumerate}
  26. Let $W$ be the region bounded by the planes $x=0, y=0, y=3, z=0$ and $x+2 z=6$. Let $S$ be the boundary of this region. Using Gauss' divergence theorem, evaluate $\iint_{S} \vec{F} \cdot \hat{n} d S$, where $\vec{F}=2 x y \hat{i}+y z^{2} \hat{j}+x z \hat{k}$ and $\hat{n}$ is the outward unit normal vector to $S$.
  27. \begin{enumerate} \item Using Stokes' theorem evaluate the line integral $\int_{L}(y \hat{i}+z \hat{j}+x \hat{k}) \cdot \overrightarrow{d r}$, where $L$ is the intersection of $x^{2}+y^{2}+z^{2}=1$ and $x+y=0$ traversed in the clockwise direction when viewed from the point $(1,1,0)$. \item Change the order of integration in the integral $\int_{0}^{1} \int_{x-1}^{\sqrt{1-x^{2}}} f(x, y) d y d x$. \end{enumerate}
  28. In a group $G, x \in G$ is said ta be conjugate to $y \in G$, written $x \sim y$, if there exists $z \in G$ such that $x=z y z^{-1}$. \begin{enumerate} \item Show that $\sim$ is an equivalence relation on $G$. Show that a subgroup $N$ of $G$ is a normal subgroup of $G$ if and only if $N$ is a union of equivalence classes of $~$. \item Consider the group of all non-singular $3 \times 3$ real matrices under matrix multiplication. Show that $\left[\begin{array}{lll}1 & 0 & 0 \\ 1 & 3 & 0 \\ 1 & 2 & 1\end{array}\right] \sim\left[\begin{array}{lll}3 & 0 & 4 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1\end{array}\right]$ (i.e., the two matrices are conjugate). \end{enumerate}
  29. Let $S$ denote the commutative ring of all continuous real-valued functions on $[0,1]$, under pointwise addition and multiplication. For $a \in[0,1]$, let $\left.M_{a}=|f \in S| f(a)=0\right\}$. \begin{enumerate} \item Show that $M_{a}$ is an ideal in $S$. \item Show that $M_{\text {at }}$ is a maximal ideal in $S$. \end{enumerate}
