MMC-41

Differential Geometry
M.Sc. Mathematics · Semester IV2017
  1. 1(a) Define curvilinear coordinate system. Show that $(x^1,x^2,x^3)$ defined by the following transformations \[y^1=x^1\,\mathrm{Sin}x^2\,\mathrm{Cos}x^3\] \[y^2=x^1\,\mathrm{Sin}x^2\,\mathrm{Sin}x^3\] \[y^3=x^1\,\mathrm{Cos}x^2\] are curvilinear coordinates and also find the coordinate surfaces. [1+3+2]
  2. 1(b) Show that a necessary and sufficient condition for a given curvilinear coordinate system to be orthogonal is that $g_{ij}=0$ for $i\neq j$ and $g_{ii}=0$ for $i=1,2,3$ at every point of the region of consideration in $E^3$. [4]
  3. 2(a) Show that the intrinsic derivative of a tensor of type $(1,0)$ is again a tensor of type $(1,0)$. If A is a scalar then show that $\dfrac{\delta A}{\delta t}=\dfrac{dA}{dt}$. [2+1]
  4. 2(b) Prove that a curve with vanishing torsion and constant nonzero curvature is a circle. [3]
  5. 2(c) Prove that the magnitude of a parallel vector field along a curve is constant. Also show that the angle between two non-null parallel vector fields along a curve remains constant. [2+2]
  6. 3(a) Prove that in order that the principal normals of a curve be binormals of another, the relation $a(\kappa^2+\tau^2)=b\kappa$ must hold where a, b are constants. [4]
  7. 3(b) Show that $g_{ij}\dfrac{\delta x^i}{\delta s}\dfrac{\delta x^j}{\delta s}=\kappa^2+\tau^2$ [2]
  8. 3(c) Show that the ratio of the curvature to the torsion of a space curve is a nonzero constant iff the curve is a helix. [4]
  9. 4(a) A curve $C$ is defined in cylindrical coordinates $x^i$ as follows: $x^1=a,\,x^2=\theta,\,x^3=b\theta\,(b\neq0)$ where a, b are constants of which `$a$' is positive and $\theta$ is a function of $s$. Find the curvature and torsion of $C$. [5]
  10. 4(b) Prove that every plane curve is a Bertrand curve. [3]
  11. 4(c) Show that a typical example of Bertrand curve in $E^3$ is a circular helix. \par\hfill P.T.O. \begin{center}[ 2 ]\end{center} [2]
  12. 5(a) Show that the cosine of the angles $\theta$ between the coordinate curves on a surface S is given by \[\mathrm{Cos}\theta=\frac{a_{12}}{\sqrt{a_{11}}\sqrt{a_{22}}}.\] Show that for the surface $x^1=a\mathrm{Cos}u^1\sin u^2,\ x^2=a\mathrm{Sin}u^1\mathrm{Sin}u^2,\ x^3=c\mathrm{Cos}u^2$ where a, c are constants, the coordinate curves are orthogonal. [3+3]
  13. 5(b) Prove that \[\sigma^2=a_{\alpha\beta}\frac{\delta t^\alpha}{\delta s}\frac{\delta t^\beta}{\delta s}\quad\text{and}\quad\sigma=\varepsilon_{\alpha\beta}t^\alpha\frac{\delta t^\beta}{\delta s}\] [2+2]
  14. 6(a) Show that the geodesics in the Euclidean space $E^3$ with Cartesian coordinate system are straight lines. [2]
  15. 6(b) Compute the geodesic curvature of the curve \[C: u^1=\text{Constant}=c\neq0,\ u^2=u^2\] on the surface of the sphere \[S: y^1=a_1\mathrm{Cos}u^1\mathrm{Cos}u^2\] \[\phantom{S: }y^2=a_1\mathrm{Cos}u^1\mathrm{Sin}u^2\] \[\phantom{S: }y^3=a_1\mathrm{Sin}u^1\] $a_1$ being a constant, $u^1,u^2$ are curvilinear coordinates on the surface of the sphere, $y^i,\ i=1,2,3$ are orthogonal Cartesian coordinates. [4]
  16. 6(c) Show that the Gaussian curvature is invariant. [4]
