SECMA-3

Number Theory
B.Sc. Mathematics · Semester III2025
  1. 1(a) Prove that the sum of the reciprocals of the first $n$ triangular numbers is strictly smaller than 2.
  2. 1(b) Let $\{a_n\}_{n=1}^\infty$ be a sequence defined by $a_1=1$, $a_2=2$, $a_3=3$ and $a_n=a_{n-1}+a_{n-2}+a_{n-3}$ for all integer $n\ge4$. Prove that $a_n<2^n$ for every integer $n\ge1$.
  3. 1(c) Prove that 8 divides $5^{2n}+7$ for every integer $n\ge1$. [[4+3+3]]
  4. 2(a) Prove that if an integer is simultaneously a square and a cube, then it is either of the form $7k$ or of the form $7k+1$ for some integer $k\ge1$.
  5. 2(b) Prove that the fraction $\frac{2n+1}{9n+4}$ is in its lowest term for every integer $n\ge1$.
  6. 2(c) Find all positive integers $n$ for which $(n+5)\mid(n^2+5)$. [[3+3+4]]
  7. 3(a) Prove that $n^5+n^4+1$ is not a prime number for any integer $n\ge2$.
  8. 3(b) Prove that each positive integer of the form $3n+2$ has a prime factor of the same form.
  9. 3(c) If $p\ne5$ is an odd prime number, then prove that either $10\mid(p^2-1)$ or $10\mid(p^2+1)$. [[4+3+3]]
  10. 4(a) If $p$ is a prime number, then prove that $\sqrt p$ is an irrational number.
  11. 4(b) Prove that $111^{333}+333^{111}$ is divisible by 7.
  12. 4(c) Determine all positive integers $a,b$ and $c$ such that $\frac1a+\frac1b+\frac1c=1$. [[2+3+5]]
  13. 5(a) Prove that a positive integer is divisible by 8 if and only if the integer formed by its last three digits is divisible by 8.
  14. 5(b) Let $x_1,\ldots,x_5\in\mathbb Z$ be such that $x_1^3+\cdots+x_5^3=0$. Prove that at least one $x_i$ is a multiple of 7.
  15. 5(c) Let $abc$ be a three digit positive integer. Prove that it is divisible by 7 if and only if 7 divides $(2a+3b+c)$. [[4+3+3]]
  16. 6(a) Define the divisor function $\tau(n)$ and prove that if $n=p_1^{\alpha_1}\cdots p_k^{\alpha_k}$, where $p_i$'s are distinct prime numbers, then $\tau(n)=(1+\alpha_1)\cdots(1+\alpha_k)$.
  17. 6(b) Prove that $n$ is a perfect square if and only if $\tau(n)$ is odd.
  18. 6(c) If $f$ is a multiplicative arithmetic function, then prove that $g(n)=\sum_{d\mid n} f(d)$ is also a multiplicative arithmetic function.
  19. 6(d) Give an example to prove that $\sigma(n)$ is not completely multiplicative. [[3+3+3+1]]
  20. 7(a) State and prove the M\"obius inversion formula.
  21. 7(b) Define the von-Mangdolt function $\Lambda(n)$ and prove that $\sum_{d\mid n}\Lambda(d)=\log n$.
  22. 7(c) Define the Liouville's function $\lambda(n)$ and prove that it is multiplicative. [[4+3+3]]
  23. 8(a) Determine $\sum_{d\mid n}\mu(d)\lambda(d)$ as a function of $n$.
  24. 8(b) Determine the number of zeros at the end of $100!$.
  25. 8(c) Prove that $\phi(n)\tau(n)\ge n$ for all integers $n\ge1$. [[3+3+4]]
  26. 9(a) For any two integers $n\ge2$ and $k\ge1$, prove that the last digits of $n$ and $n^{4k+1}$ are same.
  27. 9(b) If $m\ge3$ and $n\ge3$ are two integers with $\gcd(m,n)=1$, then prove that $U_{mn}$ is not cyclic.
  28. 9(c) Prove that every odd prime divisor of $9^n+1$ is of the form $4k+1$ for some integer $k\ge1$. [[4+4+2]]
