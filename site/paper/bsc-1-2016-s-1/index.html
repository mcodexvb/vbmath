BMC-11

B.Sc. Mathematics · Semester I2016
  1. 1(a) If $f$ and $g$ be two real-valued functions defined in some deleted neighbourhood of `c' and if $\underset{x\to c}{Lt}f(x)$, $\underset{x\to c}{Lt}g(x)$ exist and are equal to $\ell$ and $m(\neq0)$ respectively then prove that $\underset{x\to c}{Lt}\frac{f(x)}{g(x)}$ exists and is equal to $\frac{\ell}{m}$. [5]
  2. 1(c) Using $(\varepsilon-\delta)$ definition, check whether $\underset{x\to0}{Lt}f(x)$ exists where $f(x)=$\\(i) $\cos x$; (ii) $\sin\frac1x$ [2+3]
  3. 2(a) Prove that every monotonic increasing sequence which is bounded above is convergent and converges to its supremum. [3]
  4. 2(b) Check whether the sequence $\{x_n\}$, where $x_n=1+\frac1{1!}+\frac1{2!}+\frac1{3!}+\ldots+\frac1{n!}$, $n=1,2,\ldots,$ is convergent or not. [3]
  5. 2(c) State Cauchy's $1^{st}$ limit theorem and use it to prove the following: if $\{x_n\}$ and $\{y_n\}$ converge to $x$ and $y$ respectively then $\{z_n\}$, where $z_n=\frac{x_1y_n+x_2y_{n-1}+\ldots+x_ny_1}{n}$, converges to $xy$. [1+3]
  6. 3(a) Let $f(x)=x$ if $x$ is a rational number\\\hspace*{3.5em}$=1-x$ if $x$ is an irrational number\\Prove that $f$ is discontinuous at each real number except at $x=\frac12$. [3]
  7. 3(b) State and prove Bolzano's theorem on continuous functions. Deduce that every continuous function $f:[a,b]\to[a,b]$ has a fixed point. [1+4+2]
  8. 4(a) Show that the Supremum and Infimum of a non empty set $S$ are uniquely determined whenever they exist and that $\inf S\le\sup S$. [2+1]
  9. 4(b) Prove that the greatest member of a set, if it exists, is the supremum of the set. [2]
  10. 4(c) If $P_n=D^n(x^n\log x)$, then show that $P_n=nP_{n-1}+(n-1)!$\\Hence show that $P_n=n!\left(\log x+1+\frac12+\frac13+\ldots+\frac1n\right)$. [1+2]
  11. 4(d) If $y=x^{n-1}\log x$, then show that $y_n=\frac{(n-1)!}{x}$ [2]
  12. 5(a) If $y=(x^2-1)^n$, then show that $(x^2-1)y_{n+2}+2xy_{n+1}-n(n+1)y_n=0$\\Hence show that if $z=D^n(x^2-1)$, then $z$ satisfies the following $2^{nd}$ order differential equation: $(1-x^2)\frac{d^2z}{dx^2}-2x\frac{dz}{dx}+n(n+1)z=0$.\\Write down the name of this type of the differential equation. [2+2+1]
  13. 5(b) Adjust the constants $a$ and $b$ in order that $\lim_{\theta\to0}\frac{\theta(1+a\cos\theta)-b\sin\theta}{\theta^3}=1$. [2]
  14. 5(c) Evaluate $\lim_{x\to\infty}\left[\sqrt[n]{\{(x+a_1)(x+a_2)\ldots(x+a_n)\}}-x\right]$. [3]
  15. 6(a) State and prove Cauchy's Mean Value Theorem of Differential Calculus. Deduce Lagrange's Mean Value Theorem from Cauchy's Mean Value Theorem. [1+3+1]
  16. 6(b) Show that $\frac{\sin\alpha-\sin\beta}{\cos\beta-\cos\alpha}=\cot\theta$, where $0<\alpha<\theta<\beta<\pi/2$. [3]
  17. 6(c) If $f$ is continuous in $a\le x\le b$ and $f'(x)>0$ in $a<x<b$, then show that $f$ is strictly increasing in $[a,b]$. [2]
