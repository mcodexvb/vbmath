MMC-41

Differential Geometry
M.Sc. Mathematics · Semester IV2018
  1. 1(a) Prove that $A_{i,s}=\dfrac{\partial A_i}{\partial x^s}-\left\{\begin{matrix}r\\t\ s\end{matrix}\right\}A_r.$ [4]
  2. 1(b) Prove that $A^i{}_{,i}=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt g}\dfrac{\partial}{\partial x^k}\left(\sqrt g A^k\right)$, where $g=|g_{ij}|$.\par Hence prove that $\nabla^2\varphi=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt g}\dfrac{\partial}{\partial x^k}\left(\sqrt g g^{kj}\dfrac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x^j}\right).$ [3+3]
  3. 2(a) Find the elementary arc length in cylindrical polar coordinate system. [3]
  4. 2(b) Show that $\dfrac{\partial A}{\partial x^j}=A^\alpha{}_{,j}\,a_\alpha$. [4]
  5. 2(c) Prove that $\dfrac{\delta}{\delta t}\left(\dfrac{dx^i}{dt}\right)=\dfrac{d^2x^i}{dt^2}+\left\{\begin{matrix}i\\j\ k\end{matrix}\right\}\dfrac{dx^j}{dt}\dfrac{dx^k}{dt}.$ [3]
  6. 3(a) Show that a necessary and sufficient condition that a vector field A with components $A^i$ is a parallel vector field along a curve $C$ given by $x^i=x^i(t),\ t_1\le t\le t_2,\ i=1,2,3$ is that $\dfrac{\delta A^i}{\delta t}=0$. [4]
  7. 3(b) Using cylindrical polar coordinates prove that the curvature and torsion of the circle $x^1=a,\ x^2=\theta(s),\ x^3=0$ are $\dfrac1a$ and 0 respectively. [6]
  8. 4(a) If the tangent and binormal to a space curve make angles $\theta$ and $\varphi$ with a fixed direction, show that $\dfrac{\kappa}{\tau}=-\dfrac{\sin\theta}{\sin\varphi}\dfrac{d\theta}{d\varphi}.$ [3]
  9. 4(b) Define helix and Bertrand curve. [1+1]
  10. 4(c) If a Bertrand curve possesses more than one Bertrand associates, then show that it is a circular helix and in that case it possesses infinitely many associates. [4]
  11. 4(d) Show that $g_{ij}\dfrac{\delta t^i}{\delta s}\dfrac{\delta b^j}{\delta s}=-\kappa\tau$. \par\hfill P.T.O. \begin{center}[ 2 ]\end{center} [1]
  12. 5(a) Show that a twisted curve is a Bertrand curve if and only if its curvature and torsion are connected by a linear equation of the form $u\kappa+v\tau=1$, where $u,\ v$ are constants. [5]
  13. 5(b) Show that parametric curves are geodesic if and only if $\left\{\begin{matrix}2\\11\end{matrix}\right\}=0=\left\{\begin{matrix}1\\22\end{matrix}\right\}.$ [5]
  14. 6(a) Show that geodesics are auto-parallel curves. [3]
  15. 6(b) Prove that the unit tangent vector $t^\alpha=\dfrac{du^\alpha}{ds}$ of a geodesic $G$ is a parallel vector field along the geodesic. [2]
  16. 6(c) Show that the catenoid \[S_1:\ x^1=v^1\mathit{cos}v^2,\quad x^2=v^1\sin v^2,\quad x^3=a\cosh^{-1}\left(\frac{v^1}{a}\right)\] obtained by revolving the catenary $x^2=\cosh\left(\frac{y^3}{a}\right)$ about the $x^3$-axis is isometric with the surface of the helicoid given by \[S_2:\ x^1=u^1\cos u^2,\quad x^2=u^1\sin u^2,\quad x^3=au^2\] where `$a$' is a constant and $(x^1,x^2,x^3)$ are orthogonal cartesian coordinates. [5]
