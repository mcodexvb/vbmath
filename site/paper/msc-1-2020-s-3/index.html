MMC-13

Linear Algebra
M.Sc. Mathematics · Semester I2020
  1. 1(a) Define $T$-cyclic subspace. Consider the linear operator $D:P_3(\mathbb R)\to P_3(\mathbb R)$ defined by $D(p(x))=p'(x)$. Find the $D$-cyclic subspace generated by $x^2+x+1$. [1+2]
  2. 1(b) Let $V$ be a two dimensional vector space and $T:V\to V$ be a linear operator. Show that either $V$ is $T$-cyclic or $T=cI$ for some scalar $c$. [3]
  3. 1(c) Let $T:V\to V$ be a linear operator such that the characteristic polynomial splits over $F$ and has the distinct eigen values $\lambda_1,\lambda_2,\ldots,\lambda_k$. Show that $T$ is diagonalizable if and only if $V=E_{\lambda_1}\oplus E_{\lambda_2}\oplus\cdots\oplus E_{\lambda_k}$. [4]
  4. 2(a) Let $T:V\to V$ be a linear operator. Then there is a unique monic nonconstant polynomial $m(t)$ of least degree such that $m(T)=T_0$. [3]
  5. 2(b) Let $T:V\to V$ be a linear operator and $m(t)$ is the minimal polynomial of $T$. Prove that $\lambda\in F$ is an eigen value of $T$ if and only if $m(\lambda)=0$. [2]
  6. 2(c) Show that the linear operator $T(x,y,z)=(x-3y+3z,3x-5y+3z,6x-6y+4z)$ is diagonalizable. Find a basis $\beta$ of $\mathbb R^3$ such that $[T]_\beta$ is a diagonal matrix. [3]
  7. 2(d) Let $A$ be an $n\times n$ complex matrix such that every nonzero vector in $\mathbb C^n$ is an eigen vector of $A$. Is $A$ a scalar matrix? [2]
  8. 3(a) Let $\lambda$ be an eigen value of a linear operator $T$ and $G_\lambda$ be the corresponding generalized eigen space. Show that the subspace $G_\lambda$ is $T$-invariant and $\lambda$ is the only eigen value of $T|_{G_\lambda}$. [3]
  9. 3(b) Let $T:V\to V$ be a linear operator. Prove that nonzero generalized eigen vectors corresponding to distinct eigenvalues of $T$ are linearly independent. [3]
  10. 3(c) Let $T$ be a linear operator on $V$ whose characteristic polynomial splits over $F$, and $\lambda$ be an eigenvalue of $T$. If a basis for $G_\lambda$ is the union of $q$ disjoint cycles of generalized eigenvectors, prove that $q\geq\dim E_\lambda$. When does the equality hold? Justify. [2+2]
  11. 4(a) Let $T:V\to V$ be a nilpotent linear operator. Prove that $k$ is the index of nilpotence of $T$ if and only if $t^k$ is the minimal polynomial of $T$. [3]
  12. 4(b) Let $T:V\to V$ be a nilpotent operator. Prove that the number of Jordan blocks in a Jordan canonical form is equal to the geometric multiplicity of the eigen value 0. [3]
  13. 4(c) Let $T$ be a linear operator which has the characteristic polynomial $t^2(t-1)^4$ and the minimal polynomial $t(t-1)^2$. Find all possible Jordan canonical forms of $T$. In each case find the rank and nullity of the matrix; and the dimension of the eigen spaces. [4]
  14. 5(a) Consider the inner product space $P_2(\mathbb R)$ where the inner product is defined by $\langle p(x),q(x)\rangle=\int_0^1p(x)q(x)dx$ and a linear functional $\phi$ on $P_2(\mathbb R)$ is defined by $\phi(a+bx+cx^2)=a+2b+3c$. Find an orthonormal basis of this inner product space and find $q(x)\in P_2(\mathbb R)$ such that $\phi(p(x))=\langle p(x),q(x)\rangle$. Also find the adjoint of the linear operator $T:P_2(\mathbb R)\to P_2(\mathbb R)$ defined by $\phi(p(x))=p(0)+xp'(x)$. [3+1+2]
  15. 5(b) Let $T:U\to V$ be a linear transformation. Show that $\ker T^*=(\mathit{Im}\,T)^\perp$ and $\ker TT^*=\ker T^*$. [2+2]
  16. 6(a) Let $T$ be a normal operator on an Euclidean space $V$. Show that the eigen vectors belonging to two distinct eigen values are orthogonal and the minimal polynomial of $T$ is a product of distinct irreducible factors over $\mathbb R$. [1+2]
  17. 6(b) Let $T$ be an unitary operator over a complex inner product space $V$. Prove that $T$ maps every orthonormal basis of $V$ into an orthonormal basis of $V$. [3]
  18. 6(c) Let $T$ be a linear operator over an Euclidean space $V$.
