GEC-3

Differential Equations and Its Applications
B.Sc. Mathematics · Semester III2022
  1. 1(a) Show that the equation $\frac{dy}{dx}=3xy^{1/3}$, $y(0)=0$ does not have a unique solution. [3]
  2. 1(b) Form the differential equation of the one parameter family of curves \[\frac{x^2}{a^2+\lambda}+\frac{y^2}{b^2+\lambda}=1,\] $\lambda$ being the parameter of the family. [3]
  3. 1(c) Solve: $(x^3+xy^2+m^2y)\,dx+(y^3+yx^2+m^2x)\,dy=0$. [4]
  4. 2(a) Describe the following equations by giving their order, degree and stating whether they are linear or non-linear: \begin{romanparts} \item $\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2}+3\dfrac{dy}{dx}+9\sin y=0$, \item $\left(\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2}\right)^7=\left(\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2}\right)^3$. \end{romanparts} [1+1]
  5. 2(b) Find the orthogonal trajectories of the family of curves \[x^{2/3}+y^{2/3}=r^{2/3},\] $r$ being the parameter of the family. [4]
  6. 2(c) Solve: $xy\,dx+(2x^2+3y^2-20)\,dy=0$. [4]
  7. 3(a) Reduce the equation \[\sin y\,\frac{dy}{dx}=\cos x\,(2\cos y-\sin^2 x)\] to a linear equation and hence solve it. [5]
  8. 3(b) Solve: $xy\left\{\left(\frac{dy}{dx}\right)^2-1\right\}=(x^2-y^2)\frac{dy}{dx}$. [5]
  9. 4(a) Solve: $(D-1)^3(D^2-4)(D+2)y=0$. [2]
  10. 4(b) Compute the Wronskian $W(y_1,y_2)$ of the functions $y_1=e^{2x}\sin(2x)$, and $y_2=e^{-2x}\cos(2x)$. [1]
  11. 4(c) Solve: $\frac{d^3y}{dx^3}+\frac{1}{x}\frac{d^2y}{dx^2}+2(\frac{d^2y}{dx^2})^2=0$. [4]
  12. 4(d) Solve the differential equation $\frac{d^2y}{dx^2}-2\frac{dy}{dx}+y=xe^x\sin x$. [3]
  13. 5(a) Solve: $\frac{d^2y}{dx^2}=\sec^2 y\tan y$. [3]
  14. 5(b) Solve the equations $\frac{dx}{mz-ny}=\frac{dy}{nx-lz}=\frac{dz}{ly-mx}$. [2]
  15. 5(c) By the method of variation of parameters, solve the differential equation $\frac{d^2y}{dx^2}+4y=4\tan(2x)$. [5]
  16. 6(a) Show that the differential equation \[(2x^2+3x)\frac{d^2y}{dx^2}+(6x+3)\frac{dy}{dx}+2y=(x+1)e^x\] is exact and hence solve it. [1+4]
  17. 6(b) Solve the simultaneous equations \[\frac{dx}{dt}+5x-2y=e^t,\qquad \frac{dy}{dt}-x+6y=e^{2t}.\] [5]
  18. 1(a) Form partial differential equation by eliminating arbitrary function $\phi$ from \[xz^2=e^{(2x+3y)}\phi\left(\frac{x}{y}+\frac{y}{z}-\frac{z}{x}\right).\] What kind of partial differential equation is this? [3+1]
  19. 1(b) Find the order and degree of the following partial differential equations: \begin{romanparts} \item $\left(\dfrac{\partial^2z}{\partial x^2}+\left(\dfrac{\partial z}{\partial x}\right)^{1/3}\right)^{1/6}=z^{1/25}+xz^2+y^3$. \item $\left(\dfrac{\partial^3z}{\partial y^3}\right)^{1/4}+xz\dfrac{\partial^2z}{\partial x^2}+\sin\left(\dfrac{\partial z}{\partial x}\right)=x^2e^z+y$. \end{romanparts} [1+1]
  20. 1(c) Solve the partial differential equation $x^3(y-z)p+y^3(z-x)q+z^3(y-x)=0$. [4]
  21. 2(a) Form partial differential equation by eliminating arbitrary constants from the equation $z^2(1+a^2)=(ax+y+b)^2$. [3]
  22. 2(b) Solve the partial differential equation $(z^2-2yz-y^2)p+x(y+z)q=x(y-z)$. [3]
  23. 2(c) Solve the partial differential equation $xq=2xy+\log(p)$ using Charpit's method. [4]
  24. 3(a) Find the equation of the integral surface given by the partial differential equation \[(x-y)y^2p+(y-x)x^2q=(x^2+y^2)z,\] which passes through the curve $y=0$, $xz=a^3$. [5]
  25. 3(b) Solve the partial differential equation $xzq^2-p=0$. [5]
