MMC-24

Classical Mechanics
M.Sc. Mathematics · Semester II2025
  1. 1 What do you mean by constraints for a mechanical system. Classify them with some examples. Define degrees of freedom and generalized coordinate of a mechanical system. Find the degrees of freedom of a rigid body. Explain the principle for derivation of forces of constraints. Derive Lagrange's equation of motion for a system of $N$-particles satisfying $k_1$-holonomic and $k_2$-nonholonomic $(k_1+k_2<3N)$ constraints. Hence, describe the constraints for mechanical system consists of a simple pendulum oscillating in a vertical plane. [3]
  2. 2 Stating the D'Alembert's principle, establish the Lagrange's equation of motion. Hence, obtain the Lagrange's equation in standard form stating the proper restriction. Consider a particle of mass $m$ moving in a plane under the attractive force $\mu m/r^2$ directed to the origin of polar co-ordinate $r,\theta$. Find the Lagrangian and hence determine the equation of motion. [3]
  3. 3 State and prove the theorem of Legendre dual transformation. Hence or otherwise, derive Hamilton's equation of motion. What is the conjugate momentum? Show that the generalised momentum conjugate to a cyclic co-ordinate is constant. [3]
  4. 4 Define the Hamiltonian of a system. Give physical interpretation of Hamiltonian for a scleronomic, holonomic potential system. Use Hamilton's equation to obtain the equation of motion of a particle of mass $M$ moving in $\mathbb R^3$ under the influence of a force directed towards a fixed point in the space and proportional to the distance of the particle from that point. \unreadable{two words in the middle of the line are smudged} [3]
  5. 5 What do you mean by phase space variables. Define Poisson bracket. Show that Poisson bracket is bilinear, anticommutative, follows Jacobi identity. Define constant of motion of mechanical system. Prove that Poisson bracket of two constant of the motion is also a constant of the motion. Find at least one constant of motion for the simple pendulum. [3]
  6. 6 Derive the expression for kinetic energy of a holonomic system having $n$ degrees of freedom. Show the kinetic energy of a scleronomic holonomic system is homogeneous function of generalized velocities of degree 2. Hence exhibit that the Lagrange equation of a scleronomic holonomic system of coupled ODEs. Determine their order. [3]
  7. 1 What do you mean by the true and apparent velocities, accelerations of a moving particle. Establish the relation between them. Hence, derive the equation of motion of a moving particle of mass $m$ under the action of a physical force $\vec F_{Phys}$ with reference to frame rotating with angular velocity $\vec\omega(t)$ and sliding with acceleration $\vec A$ with reference to frame fixed in space. If the position vector of a particle at some instant $t$ with reference to a moving coordinate system rotating with angular velocity $\vec\omega(t)=2t^2\hat i-t\hat j+\cosh t\hat k$ is $\vec r(t)=\sin t\hat i+\cos t\hat j+\sinh t\hat k$, find its true and apparent accelerations at $t=3$. [3]
  8. 2 Derive the equation of motion of a particle relative to a frame of reference fixed at a point of colatitude $\lambda$ on the earth's surface (state the assumptions invoked during your derivation). An object is dropped from a height $H$ above a point with colatitude $\lambda$ on the earth's surface. If the air resistance is neglected, verify whether the particle will hit the earth's surface just at the bottom of the point of release of the particle. [3]
  9. 3 Obtain the equation of motion of a particle suspended from a roof taking into account the earth's rotation about its axis. Hence show that the oscillation of the particle rotates in the horizontal plane. Find the sense (clockwise or anticlockwise) of rotation if the point of suspension is on the southern hemisphere of the earth. [3]
  10. 4 Derive equation of motion of a particle relative to a frame of reference fixed at a point of colatitude $\lambda$ on the earth surface (state clearly the assumptions invoked during your derivation). An object is dropped from height 1KM above a point with colatitude $60^0$ on the earth surface. If the air resistance is neglected, find the point on the earth surface where the particle will hit. [3]
  11. 5 Derive equation of motion of a particle relative to a frame of reference fixed at a point of colatitude $\lambda$ on the earth surface (state clearly the assumptions invoked during your derivation). A particle is thrown with a high velocity $v_0$ in the nearly horizontal direction towards east from a point on the earth surface. If the air resistance is neglected, prove that the particle will always deflected towards the equator. Derive the magnitude of deflection. [3]
  12. 6 Derive equation of motion of a particle relative to a frame of reference fixed at a point of colatitude $\lambda$ on the earth surface (state clearly the assumptions invoked during your derivation). A particle is thrown with a high velocity $v_0$ at an angle $\alpha$ with the horizontal plane in the southward direction from a point with colatitude $\lambda$ on the earth surface. If the air resistance is neglected, prove that the particle will always deflected towards the east. Derive the magnitude of deflection at some instant $t$. \begin{center}Tutorial Exam II(2025).   13. (1) When two norms on a linear space are said to be equivalent ? Prove that any two norms on a finite dimensional linear space are equivalent.
  14. (2) Show that a normed linear space $(X,\|\ \|)$ is a Banach space iff $S=\{x\in X:\|x\|=1\}$ is complete.
  15. (3) Define norm of a bounded linear operator. If $T:X\to Y$ be a bounded linear operator where $X$ and $Y$ are normed linear spaces then prove that\par \[\|T\|=\sup_{x(\ne\theta)\in X}\frac{\|Tx\|}{\|x\|}.\]
