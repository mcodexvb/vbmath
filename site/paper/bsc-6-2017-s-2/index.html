BMC-62

B.Sc. Mathematics · Semester VI2017
  1. 1 Answer \textit{any four} questions: [$4\times5=20$]
  2. 1(a) Let $f:G\to G$ be a group homomorphism. If $a\in G$ is of finite order, then show that $o(f(a))\mid o(a)$. Find all homomorphisms from $(\mathbb Z_6,+)$ into $(\mathbb Z_4,+)$. [2+3]
  3. 1(b) Show that $(\mathbb Q,+)$ is not isomorphic to $(\mathbb Q^+,\cdot)$. Prove that there can not be any onto homomorphism from a group of order 9 onto a group of order 6. [3+2]
  4. 1(c) Find all subgroups of $\mathbb Z/21\mathbb Z$. Prove that every abelian group of order 6 is cyclic. [2+3]
  5. 1(d) Show that $GL_n(\mathbb R)/SL_n(\mathbb R)\simeq\mathbb R^*$. Let $K$ be a normal subgroup of a finite group $G$. If $G/K$ has an element of order $n$, then show that $G$ has an element of order $n$. [3+2]
  6. 1(e) Let the identity permutation $e$ in $S_n$ be expressed as $e=\tau_1\tau_2\dots\tau_r$ where $\tau_i$ are transpositions. Prove that $r$ is an even integer. Express $e$ as a product of transpositions in two ways one of which contains both (12) and (23). [4+1]
  7. 1(f) Prove that $|A_n|=\frac{\lfloor n}{2}$ [assume that $|S_n|=\lfloor n$]. Let $\beta\in S_n$ be such that $\beta^4=(2\ 1\ 4\ 3\ 5\ 6\ 7)$. Find $\beta$. [3+2]
  8. 2 Answer \textit{any one} question: [$1\times5=5$]
  9. 2(a) State and prove the second isomorphism theorem for rings. Show that $\mathbb Z$ and $2\mathbb Z$ are not isomorphic as rings. [4+1]
  10. 2(b) Let $F$ be a field. If there is a ring homomorphism from $\mathbb Z$ onto $F$, then show that $F\simeq\mathbb Z_p$ for some prime $p$. Prove that the fields $\mathbb R$ and $\mathbb C$ are not isomorphic. [3+2]
  11. 3 Answer \textit{any three} questions: [$3\times5=15$]
  12. 3(a) Define eigen values and eigen vectors of a square matrix $A\in M_n(F)$. Show that similar matrices have the same eigen values. Is the converse true? Justify. [1+2+2]
  13. 3(b) Find eigen values of $A=\begin{pmatrix}1&-1&0\\1&2&1\\3&2&-2\end{pmatrix}$. Also find the eigen space $E_1$ and dimension of $E_1$.\\If $\lambda$ is an eigen value of an idempotent matrix then show that $\lambda$ is either 0 or 1. [1+2+2]
  14. 3(c) Give an example of a linear transformation $T:\mathbb R^3\to\mathbb R^3$ having kernel the straight line $x+y+z=0=x+2y+3z$. Is it possible to obtain such an onto linear transformation? Justify. Give an example of a linear transformation $T:\mathbb R^2\to\mathbb R^2$ such that $KerT=ImT$. [2+1+2]
  15. 3(d) State and prove the rank-nullity theorem. Find the linear transformation $T:\mathbb R^2\to\mathbb R^2$ that maps $(1,1)$ and $(1,0)$ into $(1,2)$ and $(3,4)$ respectively. [3+2]
  16. 3(e) Let $T:\mathbb R^3\to\mathbb R^3$ be given by $T(x,y,z)=(2y+z,-x+4y+5z,x+z)$. Find the matrix representation of $T$ relative to the basis $\beta=\{(1,1,1),(1,1,0),(0,1,1)\}$. Show that $R(T)=rank[T]_\beta$. \fbox{\parbox{3.4cm}{\small Use separate answer scripts for each unit}} [3+2]
