BMC-61

B.Sc. Mathematics · Semester VI2019
  1. 1(a) Show that $\lim_{z\to a}\frac{\overline{z-a}}{z-a}$ does not exist.
  2. 1(b) If $f(z)$ is continuous at $z=a$ and $f(a)\neq0$ then show that $\frac1{f(z)}$ is also continuou at $z=a$.
  3. 1(c) Show that the function $f(z)$, defined by $f(z)=\frac{x^3y}{x^6+y^2}$, $z\ne0$; $=0$, $z=0$ is not continuous at the origin.
  4. 1(d) If $f(z)$ is differentiable at $a$, then prove that it must be continuous at $a$. Give an example to show that the converse is not necessarily true in general. [2+3+2+3]
  5. 2(a) Deduce Cauchy-Reimann equations for a function to be differentiable at a point in $\mathbb C$.
  6. 2(b) Show that the function $e^x\cos y$ is harmonic and find its conjugate harmonic function.
  7. 2(c) State Cauchy's general principle of convergence of a sequence of complex numbers.
  8. 2(d) Prove that every absolutely convergent series is convergent. Is the converse true? Justify your answer. [4+2+1+3]
  9. 3(a) Prove that a series $\sum_{n=1}^\infty z_n$ of complex terms is absolutely convergent if $\limsup_{n\to\infty}\left|\frac{z_{n+1}}{z_n}\right|<1$.
  10. 3(b) If $\alpha$ and $\beta$ be two fixed points of a bilinear transformation $w=\frac{a+bz}{c+dz}$, then show that the transformation can be written as $\frac{w-\alpha}{w-\beta}=k\frac{z-\alpha}{z-\beta}$, where $\left(\sqrt k+\frac1{\sqrt k}\right)^2=\frac{(b+c)^2}{bc-ad}$.
  11. 3(c) State Orientation Principle.
  12. 3(d) Find the stereographic projection of a circle in the complex plane whose equation is given as $x^2+y^2+2gx+2fy+c=0$. [3+3+1+3]
  13. 1(a) Define a `Discrete Metric Space'.
  14. 1(b) If $(X,d)$ is a metric space and $d_1:X\times X\to\mathbb R$ be such that $d_1(x,y)=\frac{d(x,y)}{1+d(x,y)}$, then show that $(X,d_1)$ is also a metric space.
  15. 1(c) Show that the set of all sequences of real numbers is a metric space with respect to a metric to be defined by you.
  16. 1(d) If $A$ and $B$ are two non-empty subsets of a metric space $X$, then show that $\overline{A\cup B}=\overline A\cup\overline B$.
  17. 1(e) Show that the closure of a set $A$ consisting of a single point in a metric space $X$ is the set $A$ itself. [1+2+3+2+2]
  18. 2(a) Define a closed sphere in a metric space $(X,\rho)$. Show that a closed sphere in a metric space $X$ is also a closed set.
  19. 2(b) Let $A\subset X$, where $X$ is a metric space. Show that $A$ is open if and only if for every point $x\in A$, there exists a neighbourhood $S$ of $x$ such that $S\subset A$.
  20. 2(c) Let $G$ be a non-empty subset in a metric space $(X,d)$. Then, show that $G$ is open if and only if it is a union of open spheres in $X$.
  21. 2(d) Let $X$ be a metric space. Show that a set $A\subset X$ is open if and only if $A=Int(A)$.
  22. 2(e) Let $A\subset X$, where $X$ is a metric space. Show that $A$ is closed iff $Br(A)\subset A$, where $Br(A)$ denotes the boundary of $A$. [3+2+2+2+1]
  23. 3(a) Let $X$ be the set of all polynomials $P(t)$, $0\le t\le1$ with real coefficients is in the metric $d$ defined by $d(P,Q)=\mathop{Sup}_{0\le t\le1}|P(t)-Q(t)|$, where $P(t),Q(t)\in X$. Examine if $(X,d)$ is an incomplete metric space.
  24. 3(b) Let $(X,\rho)$ be a metric space. Show that $\delta(\overline S)=\delta(S)$, where $S\subset X$ and $\delta(S)$ denotes the diameter of $S$.
  25. 3(c) State and prove Cantor's intersection theorem.
  26. 3(d) Show that any contraction mapping in a metric space $(X,\rho)$ is uniformly continuous. [2+2+5+1]
