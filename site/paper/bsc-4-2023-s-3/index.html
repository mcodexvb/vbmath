CCMA-10

B.Sc. Mathematics · Semester IV2023
  1. 1 Answer \emph{any five} questions. [$5\times2=10$]
  2. 1(a) For any two events $A$ and $B$ connected with a random experiment $E$, prove that (i) $P(\bar A+B)=1-P(A)+P(AB)$ and $P(\bar AB)=P(B)-P(AB)$.
  3. 1(b) Prove or disprove the statement: If any three events $A$, $B$, and $C$ are mutually independent, then the events $A$ and $B+C$ are also independent.
  4. 1(c) Let the probability of an event $A$ vanish, i.e., $P(A)=0$. Does it imply that $A$ is an impossible event? Justify your answer with an example.
  5. 1(d) If $F(x)$ denotes the distribution function of a random variable $X$ and $\alpha$ is any fixed point in the spectrum of $X$, then prove that $F(\alpha+0)=F(\alpha)$.
  6. 1(e) Define variance of a random variable $X$. Does it exist for the Cauchy distribution? Give reasons.
  7. 1(f) Prove the Schwartz's inequality for the expectations of two random variables $X$ and $Y$: $[E(XY)]^2\le E(X^2)E(Y^2)$.
  8. 1(g) If $\rho$ denotes the correlation coefficient between two random variables $X$ and $Y$, then prove that $-1\le\rho(X,Y)\le1$.
  9. 1(h) Define the characteristic function $\chi(t)$ of a random variable $X$ and obtain its expression for the Poisson-$\mu$ variate.
  10. 2(a) State the axioms of mathematical probability and hence deduce that for any two events $A$ and $B$, $P(A+B)=P(A)+P(B)-P(AB)$. [3+2]
  11. 2(b) Define conditional probability of an event. Show that the conditional probability satisfies the axioms of probability. [3]
  12. 2(c) Two cards are drawn from a well-shuffled pack. Find the probability that at least one of them is a spade. [2]
  13. 3(a) Define Bernoulli's sequence of trials. Show that the probability of $i$ successes in a Bernoullian sequence of $n$ trails is given by the binomial law. Hence show that if $p$ is the probability of success in each trail, the most probable number of successes $i_m$ satisfies the inequality: $(n+1)p-1\le i_m\le(n+1)p$. [1+3+4]
  14. 3(b) The probability of hitting a target is $1/5$. If 10 shots are fired, find the probability of at least two hits. [2]
  15. 4(a) If $F(x)$ is a distribution function, then prove that for any $h\ne0$, the function \[G(x)=\frac{1}{2h}\int_{x-h}^{x+h}F(t)\,dt\] is also a distribution function. [4]
  16. 4(b) Show that a function $f(x)$, defined corresponding to a random variable $X$ by, \[f(x)=\begin{cases}x & 0<x<1\\ k-x & 1<x<2\\0&\text{elsewhere}\end{cases}\] is a probability density function for a suitable value of the constant $k$. Calculate $P(1/2<X<3/2)$. [1+2]
  17. 4(c) A point $X$ is chosen at random on a line segment $AB$ whose middle point is $O$. Find the probability that the segments $AX$, $BX$, and $AO$ can form the sides of a triangle. [3]
  18. 5(a) The joint probability density function of the random variables $X$ and $Y$ is given by \[f(x)=\begin{cases}2 & 0<x<1,\ 0<y<x\\0&\text{elsewhere}\end{cases}.\] Find the marginal and conditional density functions. Compute $P(\frac14<X<\frac34\,/\,Y=\frac12)$. [2+2+2]
  19. 5(b) If $X_1$ and $X_2$ are independent random variables, each having the density function $2x\exp(-x^2)$, $0<x<\infty$, find the density function for the random variable $\sqrt{X_1^2+X_2^2}$. [4]
  20. 6(a) If $X$ is a Poisson variate with parameter $\mu$ and $n$ is any positive integer, then prove that \[P(X\le n)=\frac1{n!}\int_\mu^\infty e^{-x}x^n.\] \textit{[Differential absent in print.]} [3]
  21. 6(b) If $f(x,y)=x+y$ ($0<x<1$, $0<y<1$) is the joint density function of the variates $X$ and $Y$, find the distribution of $X+Y$. [3]
  22. 6(c) The numbers $X_1,X_2,\ldots,X_n$ are independently choasen at random in the interval $(a,b)$. Prove that the probability density function of the random variable $X=\min\{X_1,X_2,\ldots,X_n\}$ is given by \[f(x)=\frac{n(b-x)^{n-1}}{(b-a)^n},\quad(a<x<b).\] [4]
  23. 7(a) If $\mu_k$ denotes the $k$-th central moment and $p$ the probability of success in a binomial $(n,p)$ distribution, prove that \[\mu_{k+1}=p(1-p)\left(nk\mu_{k-1}+\frac{d\mu_k}{dp}\right).\] Hence obtain the coefficient of skewness $\gamma_1$. [3+1]
  24. 7(b) Define mode of a distribution. Show that the mode $M$ of a Poisson distribution with parameter $\mu$ is the integer(s) determined by the inequalities: $\mu-1\le M\le\mu$. [1+3]
  25. 7(c) Prove that the moment generating function of a uniform distribution over the interval $(-a,a)$ is $\sinh at/at$. [2]
  26. 8(a) Define correlation coefficient $\rho(X,Y)$ of two random variables $X$ and $Y$. Write down the equations for the least square regression lines of \lq\lq$X$ on $Y$\rq\rq{} and \lq\lq$Y$ on $X$\rq\rq. Show that the angle $\theta$ between these regression lines is given by \[\tan\theta=\frac{1-\rho^2}{\rho}\frac{\sigma_x\sigma_y}{\sigma_x^2+\sigma_y^2},\] where $\sigma_x$ ($\sigma_y$) denotes the standard deviation of $X$ ($Y$). Discuss the cases for $\rho=0$ and $\rho=1$. [1+2+2+2]
  27. 8(b) If the least square regression lines corresponding to $X$ and $Y$ are $x+6y=6$ and $3x+2y=10$, find the means and the correlation coefficient. [2+1]
  28. 9(a) State and prove the Tchebycheff's inequality for a discrete random variable $X$. [1+3]
  29. 9(b) Use Tchebycheff's inequality to show that in 2000 throws with an unbiased coin, the probability that the number of heads lies between 900 and 1100 is at least $19/20$. [4]
  30. 9(c) Define convergence in probability and state the weak law of large numbers (WLLN) for a sequence of random variables $\{X_n\}$. \renewcommand{\paperfull}{25} \renewcommand{\meaningplural}{} [1+1]
