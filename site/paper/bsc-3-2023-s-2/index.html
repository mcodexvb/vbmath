CC-6

Algebra-III
B.Sc. Mathematics · Semester III2023
  1. 1 Answer any two questions. $(10\times2=20)$
  2. 1(a) \begin{enumerate}\renewcommand{\labelenumi}{\roman{enumi}.} \item For $n>1$, show that $A_n$ has order $n!/2$. Give a reason why the set of odd permutations in $S_n$ is not a subgroup. Justify your answer \item Find all odd permutations of order 5 in $S_5$. \item Let $\beta=(123)(145)$. Write $\beta^{99}$ in disjoint cycle form. \end{enumerate} [2]
  3. 1(b) \begin{enumerate}\renewcommand{\labelenumi}{\roman{enumi}.} \item Let $H$ be a subgroup of $R^*$, the group of nonzero real numbers under multiplication. If $R^+\subseteq H\subseteq R^*$, prove that either $H=R^+$ or $H=R^*$. \item Suppose that $K$ is a proper subgroup of $H$ and $H$ is a proper subgroup of $G$. If $|K|=42$ and $|G|=420$, what are the possible orders of $H$? \item Prove that a group of order 63 must have an element of order 3. \item Give an example to show that the converse of Lagrange's Theorem does not hold in general. \end{enumerate} [2]
  4. 1(c) \begin{enumerate}\renewcommand{\labelenumi}{\roman{enumi}.} \item Prove that a quotient group of a cyclic group is cyclic. \item What is the order of the element $[14]+\langle8\rangle$ in the quotient group $\mathbb{Z}_{24}/\langle8\rangle$? \item Consider the subgroup $H=\{(1),(12)\}$ of $S_3$. Does there exist a quotient group of $S_3$ modulo $H$? Justify your answer \item Give an example with proper justification of a non commutative group of which every proper subgroup is normal. Find all normal subgroups (if exists) in the group $\mathbb Z_{47}$? \end{enumerate} [2+1]
  5. 2 Answer any two questions. $(10\times2=20)$
  6. 2(a) \begin{enumerate}\renewcommand{\labelenumi}{\roman{enumi}.} \item Show that a ring that is cyclic under addition is commutative. \item Explain why every subgroup of $\mathbb Z_n$ under addition is also a subring of $\mathbb Z_n$. \item Prove that an element $A\in M_2(\mathbb{Z})$ is a unit if and only if $\det A=\pm1$. \end{enumerate} [2+1]
  7. 2(b) \begin{enumerate}\renewcommand{\labelenumi}{\roman{enumi}.} \item Prove that characteristic of a finite ring is a divisor of the additive order of that ring. Hence show that characteristic of a finite ring is always finite. \item Show that the set $S=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}a&a\\a&a\end{pmatrix}:a\in\mathbb R\right\}$ is a field with respect to usual matrix addition and multiplication. \item Give an example of a commutative ring without zero-divisors that is not an integral domain. justify your answer \end{enumerate} [2]
  8. 2(c) \begin{enumerate}\renewcommand{\labelenumi}{\roman{enumi}.} \item Show that in a commutative ring with unity every maximal ideal is a prime ideal. Give an example of a ring in which there exists maximal ideal that is not prime. \item Check whether the ideal $\langle2+2i\rangle$ of the ring $\mathbb Z[i]$ is prime or not? \item If an ideal $I$ of a ring $R$ contains a unit, show that $I=R$. \end{enumerate} [2]
  9. 3 Answer any two questions. $(10\times2=20)$
  10. 3(a) \begin{enumerate}\renewcommand{\labelenumi}{\roman{enumi}.} \item Find conditions on $a,b,c\in\mathbb R$ so that $v=(a,b,c)\in\mathbb R^3$ belongs to\[W=\mathit{span}((1,2,0),(-1,1,2),(3,0,-4)).\] \item Let $U$ and $W$ be two subspaces of a vector space $V$. Show that $U+W$ is the smallest subspace containing $U$ and $W$. Hence show that $U+W=L(U\cup W)$. \item Determine whether the vectors $(1,2,-3,1),(3,7,1,-2),(1,3,7,-4)$ in $\mathbb R^4$ are linearly dependent or independent. \end{enumerate} [3]
  11. 3(b) \begin{enumerate}\renewcommand{\labelenumi}{\roman{enumi}.} \item Show that every vector space has a basis. \item The set of all $3\times3$ matrices having trace equal to zero is a subspace $W$ of $M_{n\times n}(\mathbb R)$. Find the dimension of $W$? \item Let $V$ be a finite dimensional vector space, and let $S$ be a subset of $V$. Prove that there is a subset of $S$ that is a basis for $L(S)$. \end{enumerate} [4]
  12. 3(c) \begin{enumerate}\renewcommand{\labelenumi}{\roman{enumi}.} \item Let $V$ be an inner product space over $\mathbb R$. Then show that for all $x,y\in V$, $\langle x,y\rangle\le\|x\|\cdot\|y\|$. \item Show that every orthogonal set of an inner product space is linear independent. \item Find the orthogonal projection of the vector $4+3t-2t^2$ on the given subspace $P_1(R)$ of the inner product space $P(R)$ with the inner product $\langle f,g\rangle=\int_0^1 f(t)g(t)\,dt$. \end{enumerate} [4]
