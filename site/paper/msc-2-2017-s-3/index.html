MMC-23

Algebra-II
M.Sc. Mathematics · Semester II2017
  1. 1(a) Let $R$ be an integral domain. Then show that the units of $R[x]$ are contained in $R$. [3]
  2. 1(b) Show that $[4]x^2+[2]x+[4]$ is a zero divisor and $[4]x+[3]$ unit in $\mathbb Z_8[x]$. [2]
  3. 1(c) Let $(E,+,\cdot,v)$ be a Euclidean domain. How many Euclidean valuation can you construct from the Euclidean valuation $v$? Justify you answer. [2]
  4. 1(d) Let $R$ be a commutative ring with unity 1. If $R[x]$ is a principal ideal domain, show that $R[x]$ is a Euclidean domain. [3]
  5. 2(a) In the ring $\mathbb Z[i\sqrt5]$, show that $\gcd\left(6(1-i\sqrt5)\right),\,3(3+i\sqrt5)(1-i\sqrt5)$ does not exist. [3]
  6. 2(b) Let $R$ be a principal ideal ring. Show that every irreducible element of $R$ is prime. [3]
  7. 2(c) Let $R$ be a field. Then show that $R[x]/\langle p(x)\rangle$ is an integral domain if and only if $\langle p(x)\rangle$ is maximal in $R[x]$. [4]
  8. 3(a) Show that every Euclidean domain is a factorization domain. [3]
  9. 3(b) Show that every irreducible polynomial in $\mathbb Z[x]$ is primitive. [2]
  10. 3(c) Let $F$ be a field and $f(x)\in F[x]$ be a polynomial of degree 2 or 3. If $f(x)$ has no root in $F$, show that $f$ is irreducible over $F$. [3]
  11. 3(d) Give an example of a polynomial having no roots in a field $\mathbb F$ reducible in $\mathbb F[x]$. [2]
  12. 4(a) Let $T$ be a linear operator on $P_2(\mathbb R)$ defined by \[ T(f(x))=f(1)+f'(0)x+(f''(0)+f''(0))x^2. \] Test the diagonalizability of $T$. [4]
  13. 4(b) Find the characteristic and minimal polynomial of the matrix. \[ \begin{pmatrix}2&5&0&0&0\\0&2&0&0&0\\0&0&4&2&0\\0&0&3&5&0\\0&0&0&0&7\end{pmatrix} \] [3]
  14. 4(c) Show that a linear operator $T$ on a finite dimensional vector space $V$ is diagonalizable if and only if $V$ is the direct sum of the eigenspaces of $T$. [3]
  15. 5(a) Let $T$ be a linear operator on a finite dimensional vectorspace $V$ such that the characteristic polynomial of $T$ splits. Let $\lambda$ be an eigen value of $T$ having geometric multiplicity $m$. Then prove that $\dim G_\lambda=m$. \hfill\textbf{P.T.O.} [4]
  16. 5(b) For the linear operator $T$ on $M_{2\times2}(\mathbb R)$, defined by $T(A)=2A+A^t$ for all $A\in M_{2\times2}(\mathbb R)$, find a basis $\beta$ of $M_{2\times2}(\mathbb R)$ which is union of disjoint cycles of generalized eigen vectors of $T$. [4]
  17. 5(c) Let $T$ be a linear operator on a finite dimensional vector space $V$ whose characteristic polynomial splits. Then show that $\beta\cap G_\lambda$ is a basis of $G_\lambda$ for each Jordan cannonical basis $\beta$ of $V$. [2]
  18. 6(a) Let $V$ be a finite dimensional inner product space over $F$ and let $g:V\to F$ be a linear transformation. Then show that there is a unique vector $y\in V$ s.t $g(x)=\langle x,y\rangle$ for all $x\in V$. [5]
  19. 6(b) Let $T$ be a linear operator on an inner product space $V$. Show that $\|T(x)\|=\|x\|$ for all $x\in V$ if and only if $\langle T(x),T(y)\rangle=\langle x,y\rangle$ for all $x,y\in V$. \begingroup\renewcommand{\exam}[5]{\begin{center}\textbf{#1}\\#2\\\textbf{#3}\\#4\end{center}\textbf{Time: #5}\hfill\textbf{Full Marks: 40}\par Questions are of value as indicated in the margin.\par (Notations have their usual meaning unless otherwise stated)} [5]
