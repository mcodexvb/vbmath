BMC-53

B.Sc. Mathematics · Semester V2017
  1. 1 Attempt \textit{any five} questions. [$5\times2=10$]
  2. 1(a) If two events $A$ and $B$ are independent, then show that $\bar A$ and $\bar B$ are also independent. [2]
  3. 1(b) Define Poisson process for a family of random variables $X(t)$. [2]
  4. 1(c) If $X$ is uniformly distributed over the interval $(-2,2)$, then show that $P(Y=1)=\frac14$, where $Y=\min\{X,1\}$. [2]
  5. 1(d) Define moment generating function of a random variable $X$. Hence find it for the Poisson distribution with parameter $\mu$. [1+1]
  6. 1(e) Define median of a probability mass distribution. Find the median of a binomial $(4,\frac14)$ variate. [1+1]
  7. 1(f) For a continuous bivariate distribution, write down the equations for the least square regression lines and the regression curves for the means. [1+1]
  8. 1(g) The distribution of a random variable $X$ is given by $P(X=-1)=P(X=1)=\frac18$, $P(X=0)=\frac34$. Verify Tchebycheff's inequality for this distribution. [2]
  9. 1(h) If $\rho$ denotes the correlation coefficient between two random variables $X$ and $Y$, then prove that $-1\le\rho(X,Y)\le1$. [2]
  10. 2(a) Define conditional probability. Show that the conditional probability satisfies the axioms of probability. [1+3]
  11. 2(b) Show that the probability of occurrence of only one of the events $A$ and $B$ is $P(A)+P(B)-2P(AB)$. [3]
  12. 2(c) From the numbers $1,2,\dots,2n+1$, three are chosen at random. Prove that the probability that these are in arithmetical progression is $3n/(4n^2-1)$. [3]
  13. 3(a) Define Bernoulli's sequence of trials in a random experiment. Show that the probability of $i$ successes in a Bernoulli's sequence of $n$ trials is given by the binomial law. Hence show that the most probable number of successes $i_m$ satisfies the inequality: $(n+1)p-1\le i_m\le(n+1)p$, where $p$ denotes the probability of success in each trial. [1+2+3]
  14. 3(b) If $X$ is a normal $(m,\sigma)$ variate, prove that $P(a<X<b)=\Phi\left(\frac{b-m}\sigma\right)-\Phi\left(\frac{a-m}\sigma\right)$ and $P(|X-m|>a\sigma)=2[1-\Phi(a)]$, where $\Phi(x)$ denotes the standard normal distribution function. [2+2]
  15. 4(a) The joint probability density function of two variates $X$ and $Y$ is given by $f(x,y)=(6-x-y)/8$, $0<x<2$, $2<y<4$ $=0$ elsewhere. Calculate the probabilities $(i)P(X+Y<3)$ $(ii)P(X<1/Y=3)$ $(iii)P(X<1/Y<3)$. [2+2+2]
  16. 4(b) If $X_1$ and $X_2$ are independent random variables each having the density function of the form $2xe^{-x^2}$, $0<x<\infty$, find the probability density function for the random variable $\sqrt{X_1^2+X_2^2}$. [4]
  17. 5(a) For the binomial $(n,p)$ distribution, prove that $\mu_{k+1}=p(1-p)\left(nk\mu_{k-1}+\frac{d\mu_k}{dp}\right)$ and hence obtain the coefficient of skewness $\gamma_1$. [3+1]
  18. 5(b) Show that the mode $M$ of the Poisson distribution with mean $\mu$ is the integer$(s)$ determined by inequalities: $\mu-1\le M\le\mu$. [3]
  19. 5(c) If the least square regression lines corresponding to two random variables $X$ and $Y$ are given by $x+6y=6$ and $3x+2y=10$, find the correlation coefficient of $X$ and $Y$. [3]
  20. 6(a) If $F$ is an $F(m,n)$ variate, then prove that $X=1/F$ is an $F(n,m)$ variate. [3]
  21. 6(b) Use Tchebycheff's inequality to show that in 2000 throws with a coin, the probability that the number of heads lies between 900 and 1100 is at least 19/20. [3]
  22. 6(c) State the central limit theorem for the case of equal components, and show that this theorem implies the law of large numbers. [1+3]
