SECMA-3

Number Theory
B.Sc. Mathematics · Semester III2024
  1. 1(a) Let $a,b$ and $n$ be positive integers with $n$ odd. Prove that $(a+b)\mid(a^n+b^n)$.
  2. 1(b) Prove that the $n^{\mathrm{th}}$ prime number is strictly bigger than $2n-1$ for all integer $n\ge5$.
  3. 1(c) Prove that $\gcd(21n+4,14n+3)=1$ for all integer $n\ge1$.
  4. 1(d) Let $m$ and $n$ be positive integers such that $\frac1m+\frac1n\in\mathbb Z$. Prove that $m=n$.
  5. 1(e) State the law of quadratic reciprocity. [$5\times2=10$]
  6. 2(a) Determine all prime numbers $p$ such that $3p+1$ is a perfect square.
  7. 2(b) If both $p$ and $p^2+8$ are prime numbers, then prove that $p^3+4$ is also a prime number.
  8. 2(c) Let $p_1,\ldots,p_k$ be prime numbers, all bigger than $5$, such that $6$ divides $(p_1^2+\cdots+p_k^2)$. Prove that $6$ divides $k$. [3+3+4]
  9. 3(a) For any integer $n\ge1$, prove that the unit's digit in the decimal expansion of $n^2-n+7$ is either $3$ or $7$ or $9$.
  10. 3(b) Let $n\ge1$ be an integer such that both $2n+1$ and $3n+1$ are perfect squares. Prove that $40$ divides $n$.
  11. 3(c) If $p\ge5$ is a prime number, then prove that $13$ divides $(10^{2p}-10^p+1)$. [3+4+3]
  12. 4(a) Let $n\ge1$ be an integer such that $\gcd(n,35)=1$. Prove that $n^{12}\equiv1\pmod{35}$.
  13. 4(b) Determine, with proof, if there exist positive integers $m$ and $n$ such that $n^2+n+1=m^2$.
  14. 4(c) For any integer $n\ge1$, prove that there exist $n$ consecutive positive integers, none of which is a prime or a prime power. [4+3+3]
  15. 5(a) Prove that an integer $n\ge1$ is a perfect square if and only if $\tau(n)$ is odd.
  16. 5(b) For an integer $n\ge3$, prove that $\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mu(k!)=1$.
  17. 5(c) Determine, with proof, all prime numbers $p$ such that $\tau(p^2+11)=6$. [3+3+4]
  18. 6(a) Prove that there exist infinitely many integers $n\ge1$ for which $\phi(n)$ is a perfect square.
  19. 6(b) If $p$ and $2p+1$ are both odd prime numbers, then prove that $\phi(4p+2)=\phi(4p)+2$.
  20. 6(c) Let $d$ and $n$ be positive integers such that $d\mid n$. Prove that $\phi(d)\mid\phi(n)$.
  21. 6(d) For a square-free integer $n\ge1$, prove that $\tau(n^2)=n$ if and only if $n=3$. [2+2+3+3]
  22. 7(a) Let $p\equiv1\pmod4$ a prime number and let $g$ be a primitive root of $p$. Prove that $-g$ is also a primitive root of $p$.
  23. 7(b) Let $p$ be an odd prime number. Solve the congruence $X^{p-2}+\cdots+X+1\equiv0\pmod p$.
  24. 7(c) Prove that the congruence $X^3\equiv3\pmod{19}$ has no solutions. [4+3+3]
  25. 8(a) Determine, with justification, if there exist integers $x$ and $y$ satisfying $y^2=43x^3+42$.
  26. 8(b) Let $p$ be a prime number. Prove that $p\mid(n^2-2)(n^2-3)(n^2-6)$ for some integer $n\ge1$.
  27. 8(c) Let $p=2^k+1$ be a prime number. If $a$ is an integer with $1\le a\le p-1$, then prove that $\left(\frac{a}{p}\right)=-1$ if and only if $a$ is a a primitive root of $p$. [3+3+4]
  28. 9(a) Determine all $x,y\in\mathbb N$ such that $x^2-y!=2001$.
  29. 9(b) Let $a,b$ and $c$ be positive integers such that $a^2+b^2=c^2$. Determine, with justification, if $60$ divides $abc$.
  30. 9(c) Let $n\ge2$ be an integer. Prove that $\displaystyle\sum_{d\mid n}\mu(d)=0$. [3+3+4]
