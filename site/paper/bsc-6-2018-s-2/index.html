BMC-62

B.Sc. Mathematics · Semester VI2018
  1. 1 Answer \textit{any four} questions: [$4\times5=20$]
  2. 1(a) Let $f:G\to G_1$ be a group homomorphism. Show that $f(e)=e_1$ and $f(a^{-1})=f(a)^{-1}$ for all $a\in G$. Also prove that $f$ is one-to-one if and only if $\ker f=\{e\}$. [2+3]
  3. 1(b) Show that every cyclic group of order $n$ is isomorphic to $\mathbb Z_n$. Let $G=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}-1&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}-1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix}\right\}$. Find to which of the groups $\mathbb Z_4$ and $K_4$, the group $G$ is isomorphic. [Assume that $G$ is a group with respect to the usual product of matrices.] [3+2]
  4. 1(c) State and prove the first isomorphism theorem for groups. Let $f:GL_2(\mathbb R)\to\mathbb R^*$ be defined by $f(A)=|A|$ for all $A\in GL_2(\mathbb R)$. Express $f^{-1}(2)$ as a coset of $\ker f$. [3+2]
  5. 1(d) Prove that, upto isomorphism, there are only two groups of order four. Also show that groups $(\mathbb Q,+)$ and $(\mathbb Q^*,\cdot)$ are not isomorphic. [3+2]
  6. 1(e) Let $N$ be a normal subgroup of a group $G$. If $T$ is a subgroup of $G/N$, then show that there exists a subgroup $H$ of $G$ such that $N\subseteq H$ and $T=H/N$. Prove that $4\mathbb Z/12\mathbb Z\cong\mathbb Z_3$. [3+2]
  7. 1(f) Find all epimorphisms from $(\mathbb Z,+)$ onto $(\mathbb Z,+)$. Suppose that a group $G$ has a homomorphic image $\mathbb Z_{35}$. Then prove that $G$ has normal subgroups of index 7 and 5. [2+3]
  8. 2 Answer \textit{any one} question: [$1\times5=5$]
  9. 2(a) Define the sum and the product of two ideals. For any two $m,n\in\mathbb N$, show that $\langle m\rangle+\langle n\rangle=\langle d\rangle$ where $d=\gcd(m,n)$. [2+3]
  10. 2(b) Let $R$ be a ring and $f,g:\mathbb Q\to R$ be two ring homomorphisms. If $f(n)=g(n)$ for all $n\in\mathbb Z$, then show that $f=g$. Prove that $2\mathbb Z$ and $3\mathbb Z$ are not isomorphic as rings. [3+2]
  11. 3 Answer \textit{any three} questions: [$3\times5=15$]
  12. 3(a) Let $A$ be a square matrix. Is it possible for a nonzero vector $v$ to be an eigen vector belonging to two distinct eigen values of $A$? Also prove that eigen vectors belonging to distinct eigen values of $A$ are linearly independent. [1+4]
  13. 3(b) State and prove a necessary and sufficient condition for a square matrix $A$ to be diagonalizable. [1+4]
  14. 3(c) Let $A=\begin{pmatrix}2&2&-2\\2&5&-4\\-2&-4&5\end{pmatrix}$. Find a matrix $P$ such that $P^{-1}AP$ is diagonal. Also find another two such matrices $P_1$ and $P_2$. Is the diagonal form of $A$ unique? [3+1+1]
  15. 3(d) Let $A\in M_n(F)$ and $\lambda\in F$. Prove that $\lambda$ is an eigen value of $A$ if and only if it is a root of the characteristic equation of $A$. Hence or otherwise show that the similar matrices have the same eigen values. Show by example that the converse does not hold in general. [2+2+1]
  16. 3(e) Find the linear transformation $T:\mathbb R^3\to\mathbb R^3$ defined by $T(1,1,1)=(0,2,2)$, $T(1,1,0)=(3,3,-1)$ and $T(1,0,0)=(0,-2,2)$. Also find the matrix representation of $T$ relative to the basis $\{(1,1,0),(1,0,1),(0,1,1)\}$. \fbox{\parbox{3.4cm}{\small Use separate answer scripts for each unit}} [2+3]
