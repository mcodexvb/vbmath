MJMA-3

Algebra III
B.Sc. Mathematics · Semester II2025
  1. 1(a) Let $H$ be subgroup of a group $G$, and $a,b\in G$. Show that $aH=bH$ if $a^{-1}b\in H$. [3]
  2. 1(b) Let $H$ and $K$ be two subgroups of a group $G$, and $a,b\in G$. If $aH=bK$, then show that $H=K$. [3]
  3. 1(c) Let $H$ and $K$ be two finite subgroups of a group $G$. Prove that $|HK|=\frac{|H||K|}{|H\cap K|}$ [4]
  4. 2(a) State and prove the Lagrange's theorem on finite groups. [4]
  5. 2(b) Let $G$ be a group of order 91. Show that every proper subgroup of $G$ is cyclic. [3]
  6. 2(c) Show that every finite subgroup of the group $\mathbb C^*$ of all nonzero complex numbers is cyclic. [3]
  7. 3(a) Let $G$ and $H$ be two finite cyclic groups. Prove that $G\times H$ is cyclic if and only if $\gcd(|G|,|H|)=1$. [4]
  8. 3(b) Prove that $\mathbb Z\times\mathbb Z$ is not cyclic. [3]
  9. 3(c) Find all subgroups of order 5 of the group $\mathbb Z_5\times\mathbb Z_5$ [3]
  10. 4(a) Let $H$ be a subgroup of a group $G$. Show that $H$ is normal in $G$ if and only if $aHa^{-1}=H$ for all $a\in G$. [4]
  11. 4(b) Let $G$ be a non-abelian group, and $Z(G)$ be its center. Show that the order of the quotient group $G/Z(G)$ can not be 17. [3]
  12. 4(c) Give an example of a group $G$ that contains an element of order $n$ for each $n\in\mathbb N$. [3]
  13. 5(a) Let $f:G\longrightarrow G'$ be a group homomorphism. If $K$ is a normal subgroup of $G'$, then show that $f^{-1}(K)$ is a normal subgroup of $G$. Give an example to show that homomorphic image of a normal subgroup may not be normal. [4]
  14. 5(b) Show that $\mathbb R\simeq\mathbb R^+$ but $\mathbb R\not\simeq\mathbb R^*$. [3]
  15. 5(c) Show that the group $G=\{\begin{pmatrix}1&a\\0&1\end{pmatrix}\mid a\in\mathbb R\}$ is isomorphic to $\mathbb R$. [3]
  16. 6(a) State and prove that first isomorphism theorem for groups. [4]
  17. 6(b) Show that $GL_2(\mathbb R)/SL_2(\mathbb R)\simeq\mathbb R^*$. [3]
  18. 6(c) Let $f:G\longrightarrow G'$ be a group homomorphism. If $|\ker f|=6$, then show that $f$ is a 6-to-1 mapping. [3]
  19. 7(a) Let $R$ be a ring with 1 and $U(R)$ be the set of all units in $R$. Prove that $(U(R),\cdot)$ is a group. [3]
  20. 7(b) Determine $U(\mathbb Z[i])$. [3]
  21. 7(c) Let $S$ be a nonempty subset of a ring $R$. State and prove a necessary and sufficient condition for $S$ to be a subring of $R$. [4]
  22. 8(a) Let $R$ be a finite commutative ring with unity. Show that every nonzero element of $R$ is a unit or a zero-divisor. [4]
  23. 8(b) Let $a$ be a unit and $b$ be a nilpotent element of a commutative ring $R$ with 1. Show that $a+b$ is a unit in $R$. [3]
  24. 8(c) Show that $S$ is a subring of $\mathbb Z$ if and only if $(S,+)$ is a subgroup of $(\mathbb Z,+)$. [3]
  25. 9(a) Let $X$ be a nonempty set. Show that the characteristic of the ring $P(X)$ is 2. Find the unity, and characterize all the units in $P(X)$. [4]
  26. 9(b) Give an example with justification of a ring $R$ of characteristic 2, that is not a Boolean ring. [3]
  27. 9(c) Find all units and idempotents in $\mathbb Z_3\times\mathbb Z_6$. [3]
  28. 10(a) Show that $4x^2+6x+3$ is a unit in $\mathbb Z_8[x]$. [3]
  29. 10(b) Show that every finite integral domain is a field. [4]
  30. 10(c) Show that the ring $C[0,1]$ is not an integral domain. Characterize all units in $C[0,1]$. [3]
  31. 11(a) Characterize those integral domains for which 1 is the only self inverse element. [3]
  32. 11(b) Give examples of rings $R$ and\par (i) two zero divisors $a,b\in R$ such that $a+b$ is a unit;\par (ii) two units $a,b\in R$ such that $a+b$ is a zero divisor;\par (iii) two idempotents $a,b\in R$ such that $a+b$ is not an idempotent. [3]
  33. 11(c) Let $m$ and $n$ be two positive integers and $d=\gcd(m,n)$. Show that $m\mathbb Z+n\mathbb Z=d\mathbb Z$. [4]
  34. 12(a) Let $R$ be a finite commutative ring without zero divisors. Show that the characteristic of $R$ is a prime number. [4]
  35. 12(b) Let $F$ be a field of characteristic $p$. Show that $K=\{x\in F\mid x^p=x\}$ is a subfield of $F$. [3]
  36. 12(c) Find all solutions of the equation $x^2-5x+6=0$ in $\mathbb Z_{12}$. Explain the reason for existence of more than two solutions in $\mathbb Z_{12}$. [3]
