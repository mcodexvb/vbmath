MMC-32

Solid Mechanics
M.Sc. Mathematics · Semester III2018
  1. 1(a) Show that $dl^2-dL^2=2E_{ij}dx_idx_j$ where\par \[E_{ij}=\frac12\left[\frac{\partial u_i}{\partial x_j}+\frac{\partial u_j}{\partial x_i}+\frac{\partial u_k}{\partial x_i}\frac{\partial u_k}{\partial x_j}\right]\] is the Lagrangian finite strain tensor and $dl,dL$ are the lengths of a small line vector element in the deformed and undeformed states respectively in a continuous medium while the coordinates refer to the undeformed state, and $u_i$ is the displacement components. [5]
  2. 1(b) A displacement field is given by\par \hspace*{2em}$x_1=X_1+2X_3$\par \hspace*{2em}$x_2=X_2-2X_3$\par \hspace*{2em}$x_3=X_3-2X_1+2X_2$\par Determine Eulerian infinitesimal strain tensor, where $(X_i)$ be the coordinates of a point in the undeformed state and $(x_i)$ be the coordinates of the same point in the deformed state. [5]
  3. 2(a) Prove that the maximum shearing strain at any point of the continuum is equal to one-half the difference between algebraically the largest and smallest principal strains at that point. [5]
  4. 2(b) The strain tensor at a point is given by\par \[e_{ij}=\begin{pmatrix}1&-3&\sqrt2\\-3&1&-\sqrt2\\\sqrt2&-\sqrt2&4\end{pmatrix}\] Calculate the principal strains and determine the shear between the directions $\left(\dfrac12,-\dfrac12,\dfrac1{\sqrt2}\right)$ and $\left(-\dfrac12,\dfrac12,\dfrac1{\sqrt2}\right)$. \par\hfill P.T.O. [5]
  5. 3(a) Prove that the stress tensor is symmetric. [5]
  6. 3(b) The state of the stress at a point is given by\par \[\tau_{ij}=\begin{pmatrix}T&aT&bT\\aT&T&cT\\bT&cT&T\end{pmatrix},\ \text{where }a,b,c\text{ are constants and }T\text{ is some stress value.}\] Determine the constants $a,b,c$ so that the stress vector on a plane normal to $\left(\dfrac1{\sqrt3},\dfrac1{\sqrt3},\dfrac1{\sqrt3}\right)$ vanishes. [5]
  7. 4(a) Assuming symmetry of stress tensor, establish the relation\par \hspace*{2em}$T_i^{\gamma}=\tau_{ij}\gamma_j\ (i,j=1,2,3)$ [5]
  8. 4(b) Show that the following stress components satisfy the equations of equilibrium with zero body forces but are not solutions of a problem in elasticity\par \[T_{11}=\alpha\left[x_2^2+\sigma\left(x_1^2-x_2^2\right)\right],\ T_{22}=\alpha\left[x_1^2+\sigma\left(x_2^2-x_1^2\right)\right],\] \[T_{33}=\alpha\sigma\left(x_1^2+x_2^2\right),\ T_{12}=-2\alpha\sigma x_1x_2,\ T_{23}=0=T_{31}.\] [5]
  9. 5(a) State the principle of balance of linear momentum and hence obtain Cauchy's first equation of motion. [1+4]
  10. 5(b) Prove that the strain energy density function is a positive definite quadratic form in the strain components. [5]
  11. 6(a) State the second \textit{BVP} of elastostatics and obtain the Navier's equation of equilibrium. [5]
  12. 6(b) Show that the Navier's equation of motion in absence of body forces $(\lambda+\mu)\Delta_{,i}+\mu\nabla^2u_i=\rho\ddot u_i$ is satisfied by $\vec u=\vec\nabla\varphi+\mathrm{curl}\,\vec\psi$ provided $\varphi$ and $\vec\psi$ satisfy three-dimensional wave equation. [5]
