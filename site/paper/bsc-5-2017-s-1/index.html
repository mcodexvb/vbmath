BMC-51

B.Sc. Mathematics · Semester V2017
  1. 1(a) Let $\{f_n\}$ be a sequence of Reimann integrable functions defined on the closed interval $[a,b]$. If $\{f_n\}$ converges to $f$ uniformly on $[a,b]$, then show that $f$ is Reimann integrable on $[a,b]$, and $\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_a^bf_ndx=\int_a^bfdx$. Give an example to show that the conclusion of the theorem is not necessary. [3]
  2. 1(b) Show that a sequence of functions $\{f_n\}$ on an interval $I$ converges uniformly to $f$ on $I$ if and only if the sequence of real numbers $\{M_n\}$ converges to 0 as $n\to\infty$, where $M_n=\sup_{x\in I}|f_n(x)-f(x)|$. [3]
  3. 2(a) Show that $\frac1{1+x}+\frac{2x}{1+x^2}+\frac{4x^3}{1+x^4}+\frac{8x^7}{1+x^8}+\dots=\frac1{1-x}$ for $x\in(-1,1)$. [5]
  4. 2(b) State Cauchy criterion for uniform convergence of series. [2]
  5. 2(c) Let a sequence $\{f_k\}$ of functions be defined in an interval $I$. If (i) $|f_k(x)|\le M_k$ $\forall x\in I$, $k=1,2,\dots$ and (ii) $\sum_{k=1}^\infty M_k$ is convergent, then show that $\sum_{k=1}^\infty f_k$ converges uniformly on $I$. [3]
  6. 3(a) If a power series $\sum_{x=0}^\infty a_nx^n$ has a nonzero radius of convergence $\rho$, then show that, it converges uniformly and absolutely in every subinterval $[-\sigma,\sigma]$ of $(-\rho,\rho)$. [3]
  7. 3(b) If the power series $\sum_{n=0}^\infty a_nx^n$ has radius of convergence $\rho$ and if $\sum_{n=0}^\infty a_nx^n=f(x)$ for $x\in(-\rho,\rho)$, then show that Maclauren's series $\sum_{n=0}^\infty\frac{x^n}{n!}f^{(n)}(0)$ coincides with $\sum_{n=0}^\infty a_nx^n$ for $x\in(-\rho,\rho)$. [3]
  8. 3(c) Determine the radius of convergence of the following power series: (i) $x+\frac{2^2x^2}{2!}+\frac{3^3x^3}{3!}+\dots$ (ii) $\frac13-x+\frac{x^2}{3^2}-x^3+\frac{x^4}{3^4}-x^5+\dots$ [2+2]
  9. 4(a) If $u_1,u_2,\dots,u_n$ are functions of $y_1,y_2,\dots,y_n$ and $y_1,y_2,\dots,y_n$ are functions of $x_1,x_2,\dots,x_n$ possessing first order partial derivatives, then show that $\frac{\partial(u_1,u_2,\dots,u_n)}{\partial(y_1,y_2,\dots,y_n)}\cdot\frac{\partial(y_1,y_2,\dots,y_n)}{\partial(x_1,x_2,\dots,x_n)}=\frac{\partial(u_1,u_2,\dots,u_n)}{\partial(x_1,x_2,\dots,x_n)}$ [3]
  10. 4(b) State and prove Schwazz's theorem. [4]
  11. 4(c) Let $z$ be a differentiable function of $x$ and $y$ (rectangular Cartesian coordinates) and let $x=r\cos\theta$, $y=r\sin\theta$ prove that $\frac{\partial^2z}{\partial r^2}+\frac1r\frac{\partial z}{\partial r}+\frac1{r^2}\frac{\partial^2z}{\partial\theta^2}=\frac{\partial^2z}{\partial x^2}+\frac{\partial^2z}{\partial y^2}$. [3]
  12. 5(a) If $(a,b)$ be a point in the domain of definition of a function $f$ of two independent variables $x$ and $y$ such that one of the partial derivatives $f_x$ or $f_y$ exist and the other is continuous at $(a,b)$, then show that $f$ is differentiable at $(a,b)$. Give an example to show that the conditions are not necessary. [3+2]
  13. 5(b) Show that the functions $u=x+y-z$, $v=x-y+z$, $w=x^2+y^2+z^2-2yz$ are not independent. Find the relation between them. [2+1]
  14. 5(c) State Implicit function theorem. [2]
  15. 6(a) Find the Fourier series of $f$, where $f(x)=x^2$ in $(0,2\pi]$ and $f(x+2\pi)=f(x)\forall x$. [4]
  16. 6(b) Find the Fourier series of $f(x)=x\sin x$ in $[-\pi,\pi]$ and deduce that $\frac\pi4=\frac12+\frac1{1.3}-\frac1{3.5}+\frac1{5.7}-\dots$ [3+1]
  17. 6(c) Is $\sum_{n=1}^\infty\cos nx$ a Fourier series of a function? Give reason. [1]
  18. 6(d) If $\xi$ is a point of continuity of $f$, then show that the Fourier series corresponding to $f$ is $f(\xi)$. [1]
