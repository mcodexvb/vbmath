BMC-33

B.Sc. Mathematics · Semester III2016
  1. 1 Answer any five questions.
  2. 1(a) A particle, moving in a straight line, has the following relation between its velocity `$v$' and the distance `$x$' from the origin: $v=\sqrt{\frac{\mu-x}x}$, $\mu>0$. Is the motion simple harmonic? Justify your answer.
  3. 1(b) A particle describes a curve $r=ae^\theta$ with constant angular velocity. Show that its transverse acceleration varies as the distance from the pole.
  4. 1(c) Prove that $pv=h$ in a central orbit.
  5. 1(d) If a central force be given by $F\propto\frac1{r^2}$, state the nature of the corresponding central orbit.
  6. 1(e) A planet describes a circle of radius `$a$' with uniform speed $v$. Show that at any instant the tangential acceleration is zero and the normal acceleration is $\frac{v^2}a$.
  7. 1(f) State Kepler's laws of planetary motion.
  8. 1(g) The points at maximum and minimum distances of a satellite from the Earth are apsidal points -- Establish it.
  9. 1(h) A particle falls under gravity, supposed constant, in a resisting medium whose resistance varies as the square of the velocity. Find the terminal velocity of the motion of the particle if it starts from rest.
  10. 2(a) A particle moves in a straight line under a force to a point in it varying as $(\mathrm{distance})^{-4/3}$. Show that the velocity acquired in falling from rest at infinity to a distance `$a$' is equal to that in falling from rest at a distance `$a$' to a distance $\frac a8$. [4]
  11. 2(b) One end of a light elastic string of natural length `$\ell$' and modulus of elasticity $2mg$, is attached to a fixed point `$O$' and the other end to a particle of mass `$m$'. The particle, initially held at rest at `$O$', is allowed to fall. Find the greatest extension of the string during the motion and show that the particle will reach `$O$' again after a time $(\pi+2-\tan^{-1}2)\sqrt{\frac{2\ell}g}$. [6]
  12. 3(a) Deduce the expressions for the radial and transverse components of acceleration of a particle moving along a plane curve. [5]
  13. 3(b) A particle describes a path with an acceleration $\frac\mu{y^3}$ which is always parallel to the axis of $y$ and directed towards the $x$-axis. If the particle be projected from a point $(0,a)$ with the velocity $\frac{\sqrt\mu}a$ parallel to $x$-axis, then show that the path described is a circle. [5]
  14. 4(a) Establish the differential equation of the path of a particle moving in a central orbit under an attractive force `$F$' per unit mass in the form $F=\frac{h^2}{p^3}\frac{dp}{dr}$. [4]
  15. 4(b) A thin straight smooth tube is made to revolve upwards with a constant angular velocity `$\omega$' in a vertical plane about the extremity `$O$'. When it is in a horizontal position, a particle is at rest in it at a distance `$a$' from the fixed end `$O$'. If `$\omega$' be small then show that it will reach `$O$' in a time $\left(\frac{6a}{g\omega}\right)^{1/3}$ approximately. [6]
  16. 5(a) A particle moves with a central acceleration $\mu\left(r+\frac{a^4}{r^3}\right)$ being projected from an apse at a distance `$a$' with a velocity $2\sqrt\mu\,a$. Prove that it describes the curve $r^2(2+\cos\sqrt3\theta)=3a^2$. [5]
  17. 5(b) A planet is describing an ellipse about the Sun as a focus. Show that the velocity away from the Sun is greatest when the radius vector drawn to the planet is at right angles to the major axis of the path and that it is $\frac{2\pi ae}{T\sqrt{1-e^2}}$ where `$2a$' is the major axis, `$e$' is the eccentricity and `$T$' is the periodic time. [5]
  18. 6(a) A particle describes a circular path about a centre of force $(=\mu/r^n)$ at its centre. Discuss the stability of the motion. [5]
  19. 6(b) A particle of mass `$m$' is projected vertically under gravity; the resistance of the air being $mk$ times the velocity. Show that the greatest height attained by the particle is $\frac{V^2}g[\lambda-\log_e(1+\lambda)]$, where `$V$' is the terminal velocity of the particle and `$\lambda V$' is the initial velocity. [5]
