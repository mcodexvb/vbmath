GATE Mathematics · Mathematics (MA), 2015

  1. 1 Choose the appropriate word/phrase, out of the four options given below, to complete the following sentence: Apparent lifelessness \underline{\hspace{2.5cm}} dormant life. \begin{enumerate}[label=(\Alph*)] \item harbours \item leads to \item supports \item affects \end{enumerate}
  2. 2 Fill in the blank with the correct idiom/phrase. That boy from the town was a \underline{\hspace{2.5cm}} in the sleepy village. \begin{enumerate}[label=(\Alph*)] \item dog out of herd \item sheep from the heap \item fish out of water \item bird from the flock \end{enumerate}
  3. 3 Choose the statement where underlined word is used correctly. \begin{enumerate}[label=(\Alph*)] \item When the teacher eludes to different authors, he is being \underline{elusive}. \item When the thief keeps eluding the police, he is being \underline{elusive}. \item Matters that are difficult to understand, identify or remember are \underline{allusive}. \item Mirages can be \underline{allusive}, but a better way to express them is illusory. \end{enumerate}
  4. 4 Tanya is older than Eric.\\ Cliff is older than Tanya.\\ Eric is older than Cliff. If the first two statements are true, then the third statement is: \begin{enumerate}[label=(\Alph*)] \item True \item False \item Uncertain \item Data insufficient \end{enumerate}
  5. 5 Five teams have to compete in a league, with every team playing every other team exactly once, before going to the next round. How many matches will have to be held to complete the league round of matches? \begin{enumerate}[label=(\Alph*)] \item 20 \item 10 \item 8 \item 5 \end{enumerate}
  6. 6 Select the appropriate option in place of underlined part of the sentence. \underline{Increased productivity necessary} reflects greater efforts made by the employees. \begin{enumerate}[label=(\Alph*)] \item Increase in productivity necessary \item Increase productivity is necessary \item Increase in productivity necessarily \item No improvement required \end{enumerate}
  7. 7 Given below are two statements followed by two conclusions. Assuming these statements to be true, decide which one logically follows. Statements: \begin{itemize} \item[I.] No manager is a leader. \item[II.] All leaders are executives. \end{itemize} Conclusions: \begin{itemize} \item[I.] No manager is an executive. \item[II.] No executive is a manager. \end{itemize} \begin{enumerate}[label=(\Alph*)] \item Only conclusion I follows. \item Only conclusion II follows. \item Neither conclusion I nor II follows. \item Both conclusions I and II follow. \end{enumerate}
  8. 8 In the given figure angle $Q$ is a right angle, $PS:QS = 3:1$, $RT:QT = 5:2$ and $PU:UR = 1:1$. (In right triangle $PQR$ right-angled at $Q$, $S$ lies on $PQ$, $T$ lies on $QR$, and $U$ lies on hypotenuse $PR$.) If area of triangle $QTS$ is $20\text{ cm}^2$, then the area of triangle $PQR$ in $\text{cm}^2$ is \underline{\hspace{2cm}}.
  9. 9 Right triangle $PQR$ is to be constructed in the $xy$-plane so that the right angle is at $P$ and line $PR$ is parallel to the $x$-axis. The $x$ and $y$ coordinates of $P$, $Q$, and $R$ are to be integers that satisfy the inequalities: $-4 \le x \le 5$ and $6 \le y \le 16$. How many different triangles could be constructed with these properties? \begin{enumerate}[label=(\Alph*)] \item 110 \item 1,100 \item 9,900 \item 10,000 \end{enumerate}
  10. 10 A coin is tossed thrice. Let $X$ be the event that head occurs in each of the first two tosses. Let $Y$ be the event that a tail occurs on the third toss. Let $Z$ be the event that two tails occur in three tosses. Based on the above information, which one of the following statements is TRUE? \begin{enumerate}[label=(\Alph*)] \item $X$ and $Y$ are not independent \item $Y$ and $Z$ are dependent \item $Y$ and $Z$ are independent \item $X$ and $Z$ are independent \end{enumerate}
  11. 1 Let $T : \mathbb{R}^4 \to \mathbb{R}^4$ be a linear map defined by \[ T(x, y, z, w) = (x + z,\; 2x + y + 3z,\; 2y + 2z,\; w). \] Then the rank of $T$ is equal to \underline{\hspace{2cm}}.
  12. 2 Let $M$ be a $3 \times 3$ matrix and suppose that $1, 2$ and $3$ are the eigenvalues of $M$. If \[ M^{-1} = \frac{M^2}{\alpha} - M + \frac{11}{\alpha} I_3 \] for some scalar $\alpha \neq 0$, then $\alpha$ is equal to \underline{\hspace{2cm}}.
  13. 3 Let $M$ be a $3 \times 3$ singular matrix and suppose that $2$ and $3$ are eigenvalues of $M$. Then the number of linearly independent eigenvectors of $M^3 + 2M + I_3$ is equal to \underline{\hspace{2cm}}.
  14. 4 Let $M$ be a $3 \times 3$ matrix such that $M \begin{pmatrix} -2 \\ 1 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 6 \\ -3 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}$ and suppose that $M^3 \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ -1/2 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} \alpha \\ \beta \\ \gamma \end{pmatrix}$ for some $\alpha, \beta, \gamma \in \mathbb{R}$. Then $|\alpha|$ is equal to \underline{\hspace{2cm}}.
  15. 5 Let $f : [0, \infty) \to \mathbb{R}$ be defined by \[ f(x) = \int_0^x \sin^2(t^2)\,dt. \] Then the function $f$ is \begin{enumerate}[label=(\Alph*)] \item uniformly continuous on $[0, 1)$ but NOT on $(0, \infty)$ \item uniformly continuous on $(0, \infty)$ but NOT on $[0, 1)$ \item uniformly continuous on both $[0, 1)$ and $(0, \infty)$ \item neither uniformly continuous on $[0, 1)$ nor uniformly continuous on $(0, \infty)$ \end{enumerate}
  16. 6 Consider the power series $\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} a_n z^n$, where $a_n = \begin{cases} \frac{1}{3^n} & \text{if } n \text{ is even} \\ \frac{1}{5^n} & \text{if } n \text{ is odd.} \end{cases}$\\ The radius of convergence of the series is equal to \underline{\hspace{2cm}}.
  17. 7 Let $C = \{z \in \mathbb{C} : |z - i| = 2\}$. Then $\dfrac{1}{2\pi} \displaystyle\oint_C \dfrac{z^2 - 4}{z^2 + 4}\,dz$ is equal to \underline{\hspace{2cm}}.
  18. 8 Let $X \sim B\!\left(5, \frac{1}{2}\right)$ and $Y \sim U(0, 1)$. Then $\dfrac{P(X + Y \le 2)}{P(X + Y \ge 5)}$ is equal to \underline{\hspace{2cm}}.
  19. 9 Let the random variable $X$ have the distribution function \[ F(x) = \begin{cases} 0 & \text{if } x < 0 \\[2pt] \dfrac{x}{2} & \text{if } 0 \le x < 1 \\[6pt] \dfrac{3}{5} & \text{if } 1 \le x < 2 \\[6pt] \dfrac{1}{2} + \dfrac{x}{8} & \text{if } 2 \le x < 3 \\[6pt] 1 & \text{if } x \ge 3. \end{cases} \] Then $P(2 \le X < 4)$ is equal to \underline{\hspace{2cm}}.
  20. 10 Let $X$ be a random variable having the distribution function \[ F(x) = \begin{cases} 0 & \text{if } x < 0 \\[2pt] \dfrac{1}{4} & \text{if } 0 \le x < 1 \\[6pt] \dfrac{1}{3} & \text{if } 1 \le x < 2 \\[6pt] \dfrac{1}{2} & \text{if } 2 \le x < \dfrac{11}{3} \\[6pt] 1 & \text{if } x \ge \dfrac{11}{3}. \end{cases} \] Then $E(X)$ is equal to \underline{\hspace{2cm}}.
  21. 11 In an experiment, a fair die is rolled until two sixes are obtained in succession. The probability that the experiment will end in the fifth trial is equal to \begin{enumerate}[label=(\Alph*)] \item $\dfrac{125}{6^5}$ \item $\dfrac{150}{6^5}$ \item $\dfrac{175}{6^5}$ \item $\dfrac{200}{6^5}$ \end{enumerate}
  22. 12 Let $x_1 = 2.2,\; x_2 = 4.3,\; x_3 = 3.1,\; x_4 = 4.5,\; x_5 = 1.1$ and $x_6 = 5.7$ be the observed values of a random sample of size $6$ from a $U(\theta - 1, \theta + 4)$ distribution, where $\theta \in (0, \infty)$ is unknown. Then a maximum likelihood estimate of $\theta$ is equal to \begin{enumerate}[label=(\Alph*)] \item $1.8$ \item $2.3$ \item $3.1$ \item $3.6$ \end{enumerate}
  23. 13 Let $\Omega = \{(x, y) \in \mathbb{R}^2 \mid x^2 + y^2 < 1\}$ be the open unit disc in $\mathbb{R}^2$ with boundary $\partial\Omega$. If $u(x, y)$ is the solution of the Dirichlet problem \begin{align*} u_{xx} + u_{yy} &= 0 && \text{in } \Omega \\ u(x, y) &= 1 - 2y^2 && \text{on } \partial\Omega, \end{align*} then $u\!\left(\frac{1}{2}, 0\right)$ is equal to \begin{enumerate}[label=(\Alph*)] \item $-1$ \item $\dfrac{-1}{4}$ \item $\dfrac{1}{4}$ \item $1$ \end{enumerate}
  24. 14 Let $c \in \mathbb{Z}_3$ be such that $\dfrac{\mathbb{Z}_3[X]}{\langle X^3 + cX + 1 \rangle}$ is a field. Then $c$ is equal to \underline{\hspace{2cm}}.
  25. 15 Let $V = C^1[0, 1]$, $X = (C[0, 1], \|\cdot\|_\infty)$ and $Y = (C[0, 1], \|\cdot\|_2)$. Then $V$ is \begin{enumerate}[label=(\Alph*)] \item dense in $X$ but NOT in $Y$ \item dense in $Y$ but NOT in $X$ \item dense in both $X$ and $Y$ \item neither dense in $X$ nor dense in $Y$ \end{enumerate}
  26. 16 Let $T : (C[0, 1], \|\cdot\|_\infty) \to \mathbb{R}$ be defined by $T(f) = \int_0^1 2x f(x)\,dx$ for all $f \in C[0, 1]$. Then $\|T\|$ is equal to \underline{\hspace{2cm}}.
  27. 17 Let $\tau_1$ be the usual topology on $\mathbb{R}$. Let $\tau_2$ be the topology on $\mathbb{R}$ generated by $\mathcal{B} = \{[a, b) \subset \mathbb{R} : -\infty < a < b < \infty\}$. Then the set $\{x \in \mathbb{R} : 4\sin^2 x \le 1\} \cup \left\{\frac{\pi}{2}\right\}$ is \begin{enumerate}[label=(\Alph*)] \item closed in $(\mathbb{R}, \tau_1)$ but NOT in $(\mathbb{R}, \tau_2)$ \item closed in $(\mathbb{R}, \tau_2)$ but NOT in $(\mathbb{R}, \tau_1)$ \item closed in both $(\mathbb{R}, \tau_1)$ and $(\mathbb{R}, \tau_2)$ \item neither closed in $(\mathbb{R}, \tau_1)$ nor closed in $(\mathbb{R}, \tau_2)$ \end{enumerate}
  28. 18 Let $X$ be a connected topological space such that there exists a non-constant continuous function $f : X \to \mathbb{R}$, where $\mathbb{R}$ is equipped with the usual topology. Let $f(X) = \{f(x) : x \in X\}$. Then \begin{enumerate}[label=(\Alph*)] \item $X$ is countable but $f(X)$ is uncountable \item $f(X)$ is countable but $X$ is uncountable \item both $f(X)$ and $X$ are countable \item both $f(X)$ and $X$ are uncountable \end{enumerate}
  29. 19 Let $d_1$ and $d_2$ denote the usual metric and the discrete metric on $\mathbb{R}$, respectively. Let $f : (\mathbb{R}, d_1) \to (\mathbb{R}, d_2)$ be defined by $f(x) = x$, $x \in \mathbb{R}$. Then \begin{enumerate}[label=(\Alph*)] \item $f$ is continuous but $f^{-1}$ is NOT continuous \item $f^{-1}$ is continuous but $f$ is NOT continuous \item both $f$ and $f^{-1}$ are continuous \item neither $f$ nor $f^{-1}$ is continuous \end{enumerate}
  30. 20 If the trapezoidal rule with single interval $[0, 1]$ is exact for approximating the integral $\int_0^1 (x^3 - c x^2)\,dx$, then the value of $c$ is equal to \underline{\hspace{2cm}}.
  31. 21 Suppose that the Newton-Raphson method is applied to the equation $2x^2 + 1 - e^{x^2} = 0$ with an initial approximation $x_0$ sufficiently close to zero. Then, for the root $x = 0$, the order of convergence of the method is equal to \underline{\hspace{2cm}}.
  32. 22 The minimum possible order of a homogeneous linear ordinary differential equation with real constant coefficients having $x^2 \sin(x)$ as a solution is equal to \underline{\hspace{2cm}}.
  33. 23 The Lagrangian of a system in terms of polar coordinates $(r, \theta)$ is given by \[ L = \frac{1}{2} m \dot{r}^2 + \frac{1}{2} m (\dot{r}^2 + r^2 \dot{\theta}^2) - m g r (1 - \cos(\theta)), \] where $m$ is the mass, $g$ is the acceleration due to gravity and $\dot{s}$ denotes the derivative of $s$ with respect to time. Then the equations of motion are \begin{enumerate}[label=(\Alph*)] \item $2\ddot{r} = r\dot{\theta}^2 - g(1 - \cos(\theta)),\; \dfrac{d}{dt}(r^2\dot{\theta}) = -gr\sin(\theta)$ \item $2\ddot{r} = r\dot{\theta}^2 + g(1 - \cos(\theta)),\; \dfrac{d}{dt}(r^2\dot{\theta}) = -gr\sin(\theta)$ \item $2\ddot{r} = r\dot{\theta}^2 - g(1 - \cos(\theta)),\; \dfrac{d}{dt}(r^2\dot{\theta}) = gr\sin(\theta)$ \item $2\ddot{r} = r\dot{\theta}^2 + g(1 - \cos(\theta)),\; \dfrac{d}{dt}(r^2\dot{\theta}) = gr\sin(\theta)$ \end{enumerate}
  34. 24 If $y(x)$ satisfies the initial value problem \[ (x^2 + y)\,dx = x\,dy, \qquad y(1) = 2, \] then $y(2)$ is equal to \underline{\hspace{2cm}}.
  35. 25 It is known that Bessel functions $J_n(x)$, for $n \ge 0$, satisfy the identity \[ e^{\frac{x}{2}\left(t - \frac{1}{t}\right)} = J_0(x) + \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} J_n(x) \left(t^n + \frac{(-1)^n}{t^n}\right) \] for all $t > 0$ and $x \in \mathbb{R}$. The value of $J_0\!\left(\frac{\pi}{3}\right) + 2 \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} J_{2n}\!\left(\frac{\pi}{3}\right)$ is equal to \underline{\hspace{2cm}}.
  36. 26 Let $X$ and $Y$ be two random variables having the joint probability density function \[ f(x, y) = \begin{cases} 2 & \text{if } 0 < x < y < 1 \\ 0 & \text{otherwise.} \end{cases} \] Then the conditional probability $P\!\left(X \le \frac{2}{3} \;\middle|\; Y = \frac{3}{4}\right)$ is equal to \begin{enumerate}[label=(\Alph*)] \item $\dfrac{5}{9}$ \item $\dfrac{2}{3}$ \item $\dfrac{7}{9}$ \item $\dfrac{8}{9}$ \end{enumerate}
  37. 27 Let $\Omega = (0, 1]$ be the sample space and let $P(\cdot)$ be a probability function defined by \[ P((0, x]) = \begin{cases} \dfrac{x}{2} & \text{if } 0 \le x < \dfrac{1}{2} \\[6pt] x & \text{if } \dfrac{1}{2} \le x \le 1. \end{cases} \] Then $P\!\left(\left\{\frac{1}{2}\right\}\right)$ is equal to \underline{\hspace{2cm}}.
  38. 28 Let $X_1, X_2$ and $X_3$ be independent and identically distributed random variables with $E(X_1) = 0$ and $E(X_1^2) = \frac{15}{4}$. If $\psi : (0, \infty) \to (0, \infty)$ is defined through the conditional expectation \[ \psi(t) = E(X_1^2 \mid X_1^2 + X_2^2 + X_3^2 = t), \qquad t > 0, \] then $E(\psi((X_1 + X_2)^2))$ is equal to \underline{\hspace{2cm}}.
  39. 29 Let $X \sim \text{Poisson}(\lambda)$, where $\lambda > 0$ is unknown. If $\delta(X)$ is the unbiased estimator of $g(\lambda) = e^{-\lambda}(3\lambda^2 + 2\lambda + 1)$, then $\sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \delta(k)$ is equal to \underline{\hspace{2cm}}.
  40. 30 Let $X_1, \dots, X_n$ be a random sample from $N(\mu, 1)$ distribution, where $\mu \in \left\{0, \frac{1}{2}\right\}$. For testing the null hypothesis $H_0 : \mu = 0$ against the alternative hypothesis $H_1 : \mu = \frac{1}{2}$, consider the critical region \[ R = \left\{(x_1, x_2, \dots, x_n) : \sum_{i=1}^{n} x_i > c\right\}, \] where $c$ is some real constant. If the critical region $R$ has size $0.025$ and power $0.7054$, then the value of the sample size $n$ is equal to \underline{\hspace{2cm}}.
  41. 31 Let $X$ and $Y$ be independently distributed central chi-squared random variables with degrees of freedom $m$ ($\ge 3$) and $n$ ($\ge 3$), respectively. If $E\!\left(\frac{X}{Y}\right) = 3$ and $m + n = 14$, then $E\!\left(\frac{Y}{X}\right)$ is equal to \begin{enumerate}[label=(\Alph*)] \item $\dfrac{2}{7}$ \item $\dfrac{3}{7}$ \item $\dfrac{4}{7}$ \item $\dfrac{5}{7}$ \end{enumerate}
  42. 32 Let $X_1, X_2, \dots$ be a sequence of independent and identically distributed random variables with $P(X_1 = 1) = \frac{1}{4}$ and $P(X_1 = 2) = \frac{3}{4}$. If $\bar{X}_n = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^{n} X_i$, for $n = 1, 2, \dots$, then $\lim_{n\to\infty} P(\bar{X}_n \le 1.8)$ is equal to \underline{\hspace{2cm}}.
  43. 33 Let $u(x, y) = 2f(y)\cos(x - 2y)$, $(x, y) \in \mathbb{R}^2$, be a solution of the initial value problem \begin{align*} 2u_x + u_y &= u \\ u(x, 0) &= \cos(x). \end{align*} Then $f(1)$ is equal to \begin{enumerate}[label=(\Alph*)] \item $\dfrac{1}{2}$ \item $\dfrac{e}{2}$ \item $e$ \item $\dfrac{3e}{2}$ \end{enumerate}
  44. 34 Let $u(x, t)$, $x \in \mathbb{R}$, $t \ge 0$, be the solution of the initial value problem \begin{align*} u_{tt} &= u_{xx} \\ u(x, 0) &= x \\ u_t(x, 0) &= 1. \end{align*} Then $u(2, 2)$ is equal to \underline{\hspace{2cm}}.
  45. 35 Let $W = \operatorname{Span}\left\{\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(0, 0, 1, 1),\; \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(1, -1, 0, 0)\right\}$ be a subspace of the Euclidean space $\mathbb{R}^4$. Then the square of the distance from the point $(1, 1, 1, 1)$ to the subspace $W$ is equal to \underline{\hspace{2cm}}.
  46. 36 Let $T : \mathbb{R}^4 \to \mathbb{R}^4$ be a linear map such that the null space of $T$ is \[ \{(x, y, z, w) \in \mathbb{R}^4 : x + y + z + w = 0\} \] and the rank of $(T - 4I_4)$ is $3$. If the minimal polynomial of $T$ is $x(x - 4)^\alpha$, then $\alpha$ is equal to \underline{\hspace{2cm}}.
  47. 37 Let $M$ be an invertible Hermitian matrix and let $x, y \in \mathbb{R}$ be such that $x^2 < 4y$. Then \begin{enumerate}[label=(\Alph*)] \item both $M^2 + xM + yI$ and $M^2 - xM + yI$ are singular \item $M^2 + xM + yI$ is singular but $M^2 - xM + yI$ is non-singular \item $M^2 + xM + yI$ is non-singular but $M^2 - xM + yI$ is singular \item both $M^2 + xM + yI$ and $M^2 - xM + yI$ are non-singular \end{enumerate}
  48. 38 Let $G = \{e, x, x^2, x^3, y, xy, x^2y, x^3y\}$ with $o(x) = 4$, $o(y) = 2$ and $xy = yx^3$. Then the number of elements in the center of the group $G$ is equal to \begin{enumerate}[label=(\Alph*)] \item $1$ \item $2$ \item $4$ \item $8$ \end{enumerate}
  49. 39 The number of ring homomorphisms from $\mathbb{Z}_2 \times \mathbb{Z}_2$ to $\mathbb{Z}_4$ is equal to \underline{\hspace{2cm}}.
  50. 40 Let $p(x) = 9x^5 + 10x^3 + 5x + 15$ and $q(x) = x^3 - x^2 - x - 2$ be two polynomials in $\mathbb{Q}[x]$. Then, over $\mathbb{Q}$, \begin{enumerate}[label=(\Alph*)] \item $p(x)$ and $q(x)$ are both irreducible \item $p(x)$ is reducible but $q(x)$ is irreducible \item $p(x)$ is irreducible but $q(x)$ is reducible \item $p(x)$ and $q(x)$ are both reducible \end{enumerate}
  51. 41 Consider the linear programming problem \begin{align*} \text{Maximize } & 3x + 9y, \\ \text{subject to } & 2y - x \le 2 \\ & 3y - x \ge 0 \\ & 2x + 3y \le 10 \\ & x, y \ge 0. \end{align*} Then the maximum value of the objective function is equal to \underline{\hspace{2cm}}.
