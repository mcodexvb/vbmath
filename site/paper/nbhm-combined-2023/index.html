NBHM · 2023 · Master’s and doctoral scholarship

  1. 1 There are exactly $2023$ elements in a set $X$. What is the number of ordered $3$-tuples $(P, Q, R)$, where $P, Q, R$ are subsets (possibly empty) of $X$ such that no two of them intersect (that is, all three intersections $P \cap Q$, $Q \cap R$, and $R \cap P$ are empty)?
  2. 2 A complex number $\lambda$ is called a \emph{period} of a function $f(z)$ on the complex plane if $f(z + \lambda) = f(z)$ (for all $z \in \mathbb{C}$). The set of periods of any function is a subgroup of the group $\mathbb{C}$ under addition. Find all possible generators of this group when $f(z) = |\sin z| + |\cos z|$.
  3. 3 Let $\mathscr{F}$ be the set of all continuous functions $f : [1, 3] \to [-1, 1]$ such that $\int_1^3 f(x)\,dx = 0$. Find: \[ \sup_{f \in \mathscr{F}} \int_1^3 \frac{f(x)}{x}\,dx. \]
  4. 4 A finite set of positive integers is called \emph{selfish} if it contains its own cardinality as a member. For example, $\{2, 5\}$ is selfish but $\{1, 2, 5\}$ is not. A selfish set is called \emph{simple selfish} if none of its proper subsets is selfish. For example, $\{2, 5\}$ is simple selfish; and $\{1, 3, 7\}$ is selfish but not simple selfish. How many simple selfish subsets does $\{1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9\}$ have?
  5. 5 A sequence $\{x_n\}$ of real numbers converges to a real number $x$: $\lim_{n \to \infty} x_n = x$. What is the maximum possible number of limit points that the sequence $\{\lfloor x_n^2 \rfloor + \lfloor x_n \rfloor\}$ can have, where $\lfloor y \rfloor$ denotes the greatest integer less than or equal to the real number $y$?
  6. 6 A tractor is moving with a (non-zero) constant velocity (with its wheels turning normally, without slipping). Its back wheels have twice the radius of its front wheels. Let $f$ be a point on the rim of a front wheel and $b$ a point on the rim of a back wheel. Let $a_f$ and $a_b$ be respectively the magnitudes of the accelerations of the points $f$ and $b$ at some instant of time. If $a_f : a_b = 1 : k$, what is $k$? (All displacements, velocities, and accelerations are with respect to the ground.)
  7. 7 Consider planes passing through the centre of a regular octahedron. With respect to how many of these is the octahedron symmetric? (To say that the octahedron is symmetric with respect to a plane means that the mirror reflection in the plane acts as a bijection on the points of the octahedron.)
  8. 8 Let $A$ be a $4 \times 4$ complex matrix with four distinct eigenvalues. Suppose that $0$ is one of the eigenvalues. How many distinct square roots does $A$ have? (A square root of $A$ is any $4 \times 4$ complex matrix $B$ such that $B^2 = A$.)
  9. 9 Let $G$ be a group of order $100$. Let $n$ be the order of the normalizer (in $G$) of a Sylow-$2$ subgroup of $G$. What values can $n$ take?
  10. 10 Consider the unit circle $\{z \in \mathbb{C} \mid |z| = 1\}$ in the complex plane oriented in the anti-clockwise direction. Let $C$ be the portion of this oriented curve that lies in the lower half plane. Note that $C$ starts at $z = -1$ and ends at $z = 1$. Evaluate the following line integral: \[ \int_C (1 + z)\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{2}z\right)\,dz. \]
  11. 11 Find the volume of the space in $\mathbb{R}^3$ bounded by the six planes $x + y + z = \pm 1$, $x - y + z = \pm 1$, and $x + y - z = \pm 1$.
  12. 12 Compute the following limit: \[ \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{(\sqrt{1} + \sqrt{2} + \cdots + \sqrt{n}) \cdot (\sqrt[3]{1} + \sqrt[3]{2} + \cdots + \sqrt[3]{n})}{(\sqrt[6]{1} + \sqrt[6]{2} + \cdots + \sqrt[6]{n}) \cdot (\sqrt[3]{1^2} + \sqrt[3]{2^2} + \cdots + \sqrt[3]{n^2})}. \]
  13. 13 Find all integers $m$, $2 \le m \le 999$, such that $m^2 \equiv m \pmod{1000}$.
  14. 14 Let $A$ be a real $n \times n$ matrix such that $A^{10} = I$, where $I$ denotes the $n \times n$ identity matrix. Suppose that $A$ has no real eigenvalues. Specify all values that $n$ could possibly take.
  15. 15 Let $C_9$ denote the cyclic group of order $9$. What is the number of subgroups of the Cartesian product $C_9 \times C_9$ that are isomorphic to $C_9$?
  16. 16 Let $A$ be a real matrix of size $3 \times n$ and rank $r$. Given that the equation on the left below has no solution and that on the right below has exactly one solution (as $\mathbf{x}$ varies over all $n \times 1$ real matrices), specify all values that $n + r$ can take. \[ A\mathbf{x} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad A\mathbf{x} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}. \]
  17. 17 Let $A$, $\mathbf{b}$, and $\mathbf{x}$ be the following matrices: \[ A := \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ -1 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad \mathbf{b} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 3 \\ 0 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad \mathbf{x} = \begin{pmatrix} x \\ y \end{pmatrix}. \] Let $C$ be the region of $\mathbb{R}^2$ defined as follows: $C := \{\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^2 \mid A\mathbf{x} \le \mathbf{b}\}$. ($A\mathbf{x} \le \mathbf{b}$ means that the entries of $A\mathbf{x}$ are component-wise less than or equal to those of $\mathbf{b}$.) Find the minimum value of the function $f(\mathbf{x}) = -3x - 4y$ as $\mathbf{x}$ varies over the region $C$.
  18. 18 For $\mathbf{x} = (x_1, x_2, x_3) \in \mathbb{R}^3$, let $\|\mathbf{x}\|_{\infty} := \max\{|x_1|, |x_2|, |x_3|\}$ and $\|\mathbf{x}\|_1 := |x_1| + |x_2| + |x_3|$. What is the largest positive $\lambda$ such that $\lambda \|\mathbf{x}\|_1 \le \|\mathbf{x}\|_{\infty}$ for all $\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^3$?
  19. 19 Let $f : \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ be the function $f(y) := (y^2 + y)/2$. Given a real number $x$, we take it to be the zeroth term $x_0$ of a sequence $\{x_n\}_{n \ge 0}$ where, for every $n \ge 1$, the number $x_n$ is defined by induction as $f(x_{n-1})$. We define $g(x)$ to be $\lim_{n \to \infty} x_n$ provided that the limit exists as a real number ($\pm\infty$ are not considered real numbers for the present purpose), and say that $g(x)$ is undefined otherwise. What is the domain of definition of the function $g(x)$?
  20. 20 Let $C$ be an $n \times n$ real matrix of rank $r$ and consider the real vector space: \[ V_C := \{A \text{ is a real } n \times n \text{ matrix} \mid CAC = 0\}. \] What is the dimension (as a real vector space) of $V_C$? If this dimension is only a function of $n$ and $r$, then determine that function (in terms of $n$ and $r$); otherwise write ``varies''.
  21. 21 Let $f$ be a real valued function on the interval $[0, 1]$ such that: \[ f(\lambda x + (1 - \lambda)y) \ge \lambda f(x) + (1 - \lambda)f(y) \qquad \text{for all } x, y, \lambda \text{ in } [0, 1]. \] Then: \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)] \item The set $\{u \in [0, 1] \mid f(u) \le \alpha\}$ is closed for all real $\alpha$. \item The set $\{u \in [0, 1] \mid f(u) \ge \alpha\}$ is closed for all real $\alpha$. \end{enumerate}
  22. 22 Let $\{\alpha_n\}$ be a strictly increasing sequence of positive integers. For $k$ a positive integer, set: \[ \beta_k := \begin{cases} 1, & \text{if } k \text{ belongs to the sequence } \{\alpha_n\}, \\ 0, & \text{otherwise.} \end{cases} \] In each of the following cases, determine whether the series $\sum_{k \ge 1} (-1)^{\beta_k}/k$ converges. \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)] \item The above series converges when $\alpha_n := n^2$. \item The above series converges when $\alpha_n := 2n - 1$. \end{enumerate}
  23. 23 Let $\{a_1, \dots, a_n\} \subseteq G$ be a conjugacy class in a finite group $G$, with $n \ge 2$. Let $H_1, \dots, H_n$ be the centralisers of $a_1, \dots, a_n$ respectively: $H_i := \{g \in G \mid g a_i = a_i g\}$. Then: \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)] \item The union of $H_1, \dots, H_n$ can never equal $G$. \item No two $H_i$ can ever be equal. \end{enumerate}
  24. 24 Let $f : [0, \infty) \to \mathbb{R}$ be continuous and such that $\int_0^{\infty} f(x)\,dx < \infty$. Then: \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)] \item If $f$ is uniformly continuous, then $\lim_{t \to \infty} f(t)$ exists. \item The function $f$ is uniformly continuous. \end{enumerate}
  25. 25 Let $V$ be a real $3$-dimensional vector space and let $T : V \to V$ be a linear transformation of $V$. Recall that a subspace $W$ of $V$ is called \emph{invariant} (for $T$) if $T(w) \in W$ for every $w \in W$. \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)] \item Every $T$ as above admits an invariant one-dimensional subspace. \item Every $T$ as above admits an invariant two-dimensional subspace. \end{enumerate}
  26. 26 Let $A$ be a real matrix of size $7 \times 6$ and rank $4$. Let $n$ be the number of real (strictly) positive eigenvalues of $A^t A$, counted with multiplicity, where $A^t$ denotes the transpose of $A$. Specify all possibilities for $n$.
  27. 27 There are precisely seven open sets in a topological space on a finite set $X$. What is the minimum possible cardinality of $X$?
  28. 28 Let $C := \{z \in \mathbb{C} \mid |z| = 2023\}$ be the circle in the complex plane with centre at the origin and radius $2023$, oriented in the anti-clockwise direction. Let $f(z) = e^{\frac{\pi}{2}z} - e^{-\frac{\pi}{2}z}$. Evaluate the contour integral: \[ \frac{1}{2\pi i} \oint_C \frac{f'(z)}{f(z)}\,dz. \]
  29. 29 How many different (non-isomorphic) structures of a ring with identity (not necessarily commutative) can a set with four elements carry?
  30. 30 Find the set of real values of $y_0$ such that there exists a bounded function $y(t) : [0, \infty) \to \mathbb{R}$ that solves the following initial value problem: \[ y'(t) = (1 - y^2(t))\cosh y(t) \text{ for } t > 0; \qquad y(0) = y_0. \]
  31. 31 Consider the subgroup $V$ of $\mathbb{Q}^2$ (under addition) generated by $\mathbf{v}_1 = \left(\frac{1}{2}, \frac{1}{3}\right)$ and $\mathbf{v}_2 = \left(\frac{1}{4}, \frac{1}{5}\right)$: in other words, $V := \{a\mathbf{v}_1 + b\mathbf{v}_2 \mid a, b \in \mathbb{Z}\}$. Let $U$ be the subgroup $\mathbb{Z}^2$ of $\mathbb{Q}^2$. What is the index of $U$ in $V$, or, in other words, what is the cardinality of the quotient group $V/U$?
  32. 32 Consider the complex equation $e^z = 3z + 1$. How many roots does this equation admit in the region $|z| < 1$?
  33. 33 Let $G$ be the group of invertible $2 \times 2$ matrices with entries in the field $\mathbb{Z}/5\mathbb{Z}$ of $5$ elements, with respect to the usual matrix multiplication. Let $S$ be the subset of those elements of $G$ that can be written in the form $LU$ where $L$ is a lower triangular matrix in $G$ and $U$ an upper triangular matrix in $G$. How many elements does $S$ have? (A $2 \times 2$ matrix is lower triangular if its entry in position $(1, 2)$ is zero; it is upper triangular if its entry in position $(2, 1)$ is zero.)
  34. 34 Let $A$, $B$, and $C$ be matrices with real entries of sizes $40 \times 20$, $20 \times 30$, and $30 \times 40$ respectively. Given that the ranks of the matrices $AB$, $BC$, and $B$ are respectively $11$, $12$, and $15$, what is the minimum possible rank of the matrix $ABC$?
  35. 35 Find $u(\pi/2, 7\pi/4)$, where $u$ is the solution of: \[ u_{tt}(x, t) = u_{xx}(x, t) \text{ for } x \in (0, \pi),\ t > 0; \qquad u(x, 0) = \sin^{10} x \text{ for } x \in (0, \pi), \] \[ u_t(x, 0) = 0 \text{ for } x \in (0, \pi), \qquad u(0, t) = u(\pi, t) = 0 \text{ for } t > 0. \]
  36. 36 Let $v_1, v_2, v_3, v_4, v_5$, and $v_6$ be six distinct vectors in a vector space. There are $\binom{6}{4} = 15$ distinct subsets consisting precisely of four of these vectors. Suppose that, out of these $15$ subsets, only $\{v_1, v_2, v_3, v_4\}$ and $\{v_1, v_2, v_3, v_5\}$ are linearly independent, and the remaining $13$ are linearly dependent. Then: \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)] \item $v_6$ is the zero vector. \item $v_4$ and $v_5$ are non-zero scalar multiples of each other. \end{enumerate}
  37. 37 Let $f(z)$ be an arbitrary non-constant entire function on the complex plane. \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)] \item It is possible for $f(z)$ not to take any real value; in other words, the image of $f(z)$ could possibly be contained in $\mathbb{C} \setminus \mathbb{R}$. \item A complex number $z_0$ is called a \emph{local minimum} of $f(z)$ if $|f(z_0)| \le |f(z)|$ for all $z$ in some open set $U$ around $z_0$. If $z_0$ is a local minimum of $f(z)$ then $f(z_0) = 0$. \end{enumerate}
  38. 38 Let $k$ be the field $\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}$ of integers modulo a prime $p$. Any polynomial $f(x)$ with coefficients in $k$ can be considered a $k$-valued function on $k$: by $\alpha \mapsto f(\alpha)$ for all $\alpha \in k$. Such a polynomial of degree $d$ is called \emph{monic} if its leading coefficient (the coefficient of $x^d$) is $1$. \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)] \item For any function $\varphi : k \to k$, and any integer $d$ such that $d \ge p$, there exists a monic polynomial $f(x)$ of degree $d$ such that $\varphi(\alpha) = f(\alpha)$ for all $\alpha$ in $k$. \item For any integer $d \ge 1$, and any monic polynomial $f(x)$ of degree $d$, the number of polynomials $g(x)$ (not necessarily monic) of degree exactly $d$ such that $f(\alpha) = g(\alpha)$ for all $\alpha \in k$ is independent of $f(x)$ (that is, the same for all monic $f(x)$ of degree $d$). \end{enumerate}
  39. 39 Let $p(x) = x^3 + ax^2 + bx + c$ be a cubic polynomial with real coefficients $a, b, c$, and define: \[ D := \{(a, b, c) \in \mathbb{R}^3 \mid \text{the polynomial } p(x) \text{ factors into linear factors over } \mathbb{R}\}. \] Then: \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)] \item $D$ is connected (as a subset of the topological space $\mathbb{R}^3$). \item For any $(a, b, c) \in D$, we have $a^2 \ge 3b$. \end{enumerate}
  40. 40 Let $\mathbb{Q}[x]$ be the ring of polynomials in the variable $x$ with rational coefficients. Let $S$ be the sub-ring of $\mathbb{Q}[x]$ consisting of all those polynomials $f(x)$ with the property that $f(\alpha)$ is an integer whenever $\alpha$ is an integer. Then: \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)] \item Given a positive integer $n$, distinct integers $a_1, \dots, a_n$ and any integers $b_1, \dots, b_n$, there exists a polynomial $f(x)$ in $S$ such that $f(a_i) = b_i$ for all $1 \le i \le n$. \item The ring $S$ is finitely generated as a ring over the ring $\mathbb{Z}$ of integers. \end{enumerate}
