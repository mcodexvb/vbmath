MJMA-5

Analysis-III
B.Sc. Mathematics · Semester III2024
  1. 1(a) Discuss the convergence of the series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n^p}$, where $p\in\mathbb R$. [6]
  2. 1(b) Examine the convergence of the series $a+b+a^2+b^2+a^3+b^3+\cdots$, where $0<a<b<1$. [4]
  3. 2(a) Prove or disprove: There exists a convergent series $\sum a_n$ such that $\sum a_n^2$ is divergent. [4]
  4. 2(b) Describe Cauchy's condensation test for positive series. Use it to discuss the convergence of the series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n(\log n)^p}$, where $p>0$. [2+4]
  5. 3(a) If $\{n^2a_n\}$ is a convergent sequence, show that the series $\sum a_n$ is absolutely convergent. [4]
  6. 3(b) State Leibnitz's test and Dirichlet's test for infinite series. Give application of these. Show that Leibnitz's test is a particular case of Dirichlet's test. [2+2+2]
  7. 4(a) Discuss in how many ways a function $f$ may be discontinuous at a point $x=c$. Explain with examples. [4+2]
  8. 4(b) If $f$ and $g$ are two function continuous at $c$, show that $f\cdot g$ is also continuous at $c$. [4]
  9. 5(a) Let $f,g:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R$ be continuous on $\mathbb R$. Show that $A=\{x\in\mathbb R:f(x)=g(x)\}$ is a closed set in $\mathbb R$. [4]
  10. 5(b) If $f$ is continuous on a closed and bounded interval $I=[a,b]$, show that $f(I)$ is a closed and bounded interval. Is $f(I)$ an open and bounded interval provided $I$ is so? Justify your answer. [4+2]
  11. 6(a) Let $f:[0,1]\to\mathbb R$ be continuous on $[0,1]$ and let $f$ assume only irrational values. If $f(\frac1e)=e$, show that $f(x)=e$ for all $x\in[0,1]$. [4]
  12. 6(b) If $f$ is uniformly continuous on a bounded interval $I$, show that $f$ is bounded on $I$. Is it true if $f$ is continuous but not uniformly continuous on $I$? Justify your answer. [4+2]
  13. 7(a) Let a function $f$ be continuous on an open bounded interval $(a,b)$ and have continuous extension to $\mathbb R$. Prove that $f$ is uniformly continuous on $(a,b)$. [4]
  14. 7(b) Define a Lipschitz function on $[a,b]$. If $f:[a,b]\to\mathbb R$ is a Lipschitz function on $[a,b]$, show that $f$ is a function of bounded variation on $[a,b]$. Is the converse true? Justify. [1+3+2]
  15. 8(a) Let $f:[a,b]\to\mathbb R$ be a BV-function on $[a,b]$ and $Q$ be a refinement of a partition $P$ of $[a,b]$. Prove that $V(P,f)\le V(Q,f)$. [4]
  16. 8(b) If $f(x)=[x]$, $x\in[1,3]$, show that $f$ is a function of bounded variation on $[1,3]$. Find the variation function $V$ on $[1,3]$. Express $f$ as the difference of two monotone increasing functions on $[1,3]$. [2+2+2]
  17. 9(a) If $f$ is a bounded function on $[a,b]$ and $P,Q$ are two partitions of $[a,b]$, show that $L(P,f)\le U(Q,f)$ and $L(Q,f)\le U(P,f)$. [4]
  18. 9(b) Define lower and upper Riemann integrals of bounded function on a closed and bounded interval. Find these for the function $f(x)=x^2$, $x\in[a,b]$, $a>0$ and determine if $f$ is Riemann integrable on $[a,b]$. [2+2+2]
  19. 10(a) If $f$ is a Riemann integrable function on $[a,b]$ and $f(x)>0$ for all $x\in[a,b]$, show that $f$ may not be Riemann integrable on $[a,b]$. Under what additional condition, $f$ is necessarily Riemann integrable on $[a,b]$? Establish it. [3+4]
  20. 10(b) Let $f:[a,b]\to\mathbb R$ be bounded on $[a,b]$ and be integrable on $[c,b]$ for every $c\in[a,b]$. Prove that $f$ is integrable on $[a,b]$. [3]
  21. 11(a) Let $f:[0,1]\to\mathbb R$ be defined by \[f(x)=\begin{cases}x^2+x^3&\text{if }x\text{ is rational}\\x+x^2&\text{if }x\text{ is irrational}\end{cases}.\] Find $\underline{\int}_0^1 f$ and $\overline{\int}_0^1 f$. Hence determine if $f$ is integrable on $[0,1]$. [3+3+1]
  22. 11(b) Examine the convergence of the improper integral $\displaystyle\int_0^1\frac{x^{p-1}}{1+x}$. \textit{[Differential absent in print.]} [3]
  23. 12(a) When is an improper integral $\displaystyle\int_a^\infty f(x)\,dx$ called convergent? Explain with examples. [3]
  24. 12(b) Show that the improper integral $\displaystyle\int_a^\infty\frac{dx}{x^\alpha}$ (where $a>0$) is convergent if and only if $\alpha>1$. Use it to show that $\displaystyle\int_0^\infty x^{m-1}e^{-x}\,dx$. \par\textit{[Printed continuation unavailable. A handwritten addition reads “is convergent if \(m>0\).”]} [3+4]
