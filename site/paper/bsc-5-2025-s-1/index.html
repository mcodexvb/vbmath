MJMA-11

Analysis V
B.Sc. Mathematics · Semester V2025
  1. 1(a) Find $\bigcap_{n\in\mathbb N}(0,\frac1n]$ with justification. [2]
  2. 1(b) Show that the intersection of any two neighbourhoods of a point $c\in\mathbb R$ is a neighbourhood of $c$. [2]
  3. 1(c) Define an interior point of a subset $S\subseteq\mathbb R$. Give examples of an open subset and of a subset which is not open. [3]
  4. 1(d) Prove that an arbitrary union of open subsets is open. Hence or otherwise show that $S=\{x\in\mathbb R\mid\sin x>0\}$ is an open subset. [3]
  5. 2(a) Let $G$ be an open subset and $S$ be a finite subset of $\mathbb R$. Show that $G\setminus S$ is an open subset. [3]
  6. 2(b) Let $S$ and $T$ be two subsets of $\mathbb R$. Show that $(S\cap T)^o=S^o\cap T^o$. [3]
  7. 2(c) Let $S\subseteq\mathbb R$. prove that $S^o$ is the largest open subset contained in $S$. [4]
  8. 3(a) Prove that every nonempty open subset of $\mathbb R$ is a disjoint countable union of open intervals. [4]
  9. 3(b) Define a limit point of a subset $S\subseteq\mathbb R$. Let $S=\{1,\frac12,\frac13,\cdots\}$. Show that $0$ is a limit point and $\frac1{50}$ is an isolated point of $S$. [3]
  10. 3(c) Let $p$ be a limit point of $S$. Show that every neighbourhood of $p$ contains infinitely many points of $S$. [3]
  11. 4(a) Give an example to show that an arbitrary union of closed subsets may not be closed. [2]
  12. 4(b) Let $F\subseteq\mathbb R$. Show that $F$ is a closed subset if and only if its complement $F^c$ is open. [3]
  13. 4(c) Prove that the closure $\overline F$ of a subset $F\subseteq\mathbb R$ is the intersection of all closed subsets containing $F$. [3]
  14. 4(d) Let $S$ be a bounded subset of $\mathbb R$, and $u=\sup S$. If $u\notin S$, show that $u$ is a limit point of $S$. [2]
  15. 5(a) Give an example of an open cover of $S=(0,1]$ that has no finite subcover. [2]
  16. 5(b) Show that every compact subset is closed. Give an example to show that the converse does not hold. [3+2]
  17. 5(c) Let $K$ be a compact subset of $\mathbb R$, and $S$ be an infinite subset of $K$. Show that $S$ has a limit point in $K$. [3]
  18. 6(a) A binary relation $\rho$ is defined on $\mathbb N\times\mathbb N$ by: $(a,b)\rho(c,d)$ if $a+d=b+c$. Find the zero element and the negative of every element in the ring $\mathbb Z=\mathbb N\times\mathbb N/\rho$. [3]
  19. 6(b) A binary relation $\sigma$ is defined on $\mathbb Z\times\mathbb Z^*$ by: $(a,b)\sigma(c,d)$ if $ad=bc$. Find the unity and multiplicative inverse of every element in the field $\mathbb Q=\mathbb Z\times\mathbb Z^*/\sigma$. [3]
  20. 6(c) Let $C$ be the commutative ring of all Cauchy sequences over $\mathbb Q$. Show that the set $N$ of all rational null sequences is a maximal ideal in $C$. [4]
  21. 7(a) Show that for any set $X$, $|\mathcal P(X)|=2^{|X|}$ [3]
  22. 7(b) State and prove Schroder-Bernstein theorem for cardinal numbers. [4]
  23. 7(c) Define a maximal element of a poset. Show that it is not unique. [3]
  24. 8 Let $a=|\mathbb N|$ and $c=|\mathbb R|$. Establish the following identities of cardinal arithmetic:
  25. 8(a) $c+c=c$. [3]
  26. 8(b) $a\cdot c=c$. [2]
  27. 8(c) $2^a=c$. [5]
  28. 9(a) When is the trigonometric series $\frac{a_0}2+\sum_{n=1}^\infty(a_n\cos nx+b_n\sin nx)$ referred to as a Fourier series? Show that if $\frac{a_0}2+\sum_{n=1}^\infty(a_n\cos nx+b_n\sin nx)$ converges uniformly in $[-\pi,\pi]$ to a function $f$ then it is the Fourier series of $f$. [1+4]
  29. 9(b) If $f$ is Riemann-integrable in $[a,b]$ then show that $\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_a^bf(x)\sin nxdx=0$. [5]
  30. 10(a) Write the Bessel's inequality of Fourier series and prove it. [4]
  31. 10(b) If $a_n$ and $b_n$ are Fourier coefficients of $f$ then prove that $\sum_{n=1}^\infty(a_n^2+b_n^2)$, $\sum_{n=1}^\infty a_n^2$, $\sum_{n=1}^\infty b_n^2$, are all convergent. [2]
  32. 10(c) Show that the Fourier series of $x^2$ in $[-\pi,\pi]$ is $\frac{\pi^2}3+4\sum_{n=1}^\infty(-1)^n\left[\frac{\cos nx}{n^2}\right]$ [4]
  33. 11(a) Show that the Cantor set is a closed set. Also find its measure. [3]
  34. 11(b) Find the measures of the following sets: (i) the set of irrationals in $[0,2026]$ (ii) $\{\sin x:0<x<40\pi\}$ (iii) $\{\frac1{n^2}:n\in\mathbb Z\}$ (iv) $\{\frac1{x^2}:0\le x<\infty\}$ [1+1+1+1]
  35. 11(c) Show that Lebesgue measure is invariant under translations. [3]
  36. 12(a) If $B\subset A$ are measurable sets then show that $\mu(A\sim B)=\mu(A)-\mu(B)$ [2]
  37. 12(b) Prove that for any two measurable sets $A$ and $B$ $\mu(A\cup B)+\mu(A\cap B)=\mu(A)+\mu(B)$ [2]
  38. 12(c) Suppose $\{E_n\}$ be a sequence of sets, construct a disjoint sequence of sets $\{F_n\}$ such that $\cup E_n=\cup F_n$ [2]
  39. 12(d) Prove that there exist sets that are not measurable. \fbox{\parbox{3.2cm}{\small Use separate answer script for each unit}} [4]
