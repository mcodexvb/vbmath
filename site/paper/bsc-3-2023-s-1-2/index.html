GEC-3

Differential Equations and its Applications
B.Sc. Mathematics · Semester III2023
  1. 1(a) For which value of $m$, the function $f$ defined by $f(x)=(2x^2+2e^x+3)e^{mx}$ is a solution of the differential equation \[\frac{dy}{dx}=6e^x+4xe^{-2x}.\]
  2. 1(b) Obtain the differential equation of all the circles each of which touches the $x$-axis at the origin.
  3. 1(c) When a differential equation of the form $M(x,y)\,dx+N(x,y)\,dy=0$ is called exact? Examine whether the equation $(1-2xy-y^2)\,dx-(x+y)^2\,dy=0$ is exact or not. [2+5+3]
  4. 2(a) Solve: $\cos y\frac{dy}{dx}+\frac1x\sin y=1$.
  5. 2(b) Solve: $(x^3+3xy^2)\,dx+(y^3+3x^2y)\,dy=0$.
  6. 2(c) Show that $x(x^2-y^2)^{-1}$ is an integrating factor of the equation $(x^2+y^2)\,dx-2xy\,dy=0$ and hence solve the equation. [2+4+4]
  7. 3(a) Find the orthogonal trajectories of the family of curves $x=\frac{y^4}{4}+\frac{c}{y^2}$, $c$ being a variable parameter.
  8. 3(b) Solve: $x\frac{dy}{dx}+y=y^2\ln x$, $y(x=1)=\frac12$. [5+5]
  9. 4(a) Write down the geometrical significance of the singular solution in Clairaut's equation.
  10. 4(b) Solve: $\alpha yp^2+(2x-\beta)p-y=0$, $p=\frac{dy}{dx}$, $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are two parameters.
  11. 4(c) Solve the equation $y=px+\sqrt{a^2p^2+b^2}$ and show that the singular solution lies on a parabola, $a$ and $b$ are two parameters. [2+3+5]
  12. 5(a) Show that the functions $\{e^{2x},e^{2x}\cos 4x,e^{2x}\sin 4x\}$ are linearly independent.
  13. 5(b) Solve: $\frac{d^2y}{dx^2}-4y=xe^{2x}$.
  14. 5(c) Solve: $(D^2+4)(D^2+1)y=\cos 2x$, $D^n\equiv\frac{d^n}{dx^n}$. [2+3+5]
  15. 6(a) Solve the system of equations $\frac{dx}{dt}=-\omega y$ and $\frac{dy}{dt}=\omega x$. Hence show that the solution lies on a circle.
  16. 6(b) Solve by using the method of variation of parameters $\frac{d^2y}{dx^2}-4\frac{dy}{dx}+4y=xe^{2x}$.
  17. 6(c) Solve: $x^2\frac{d^2y}{dx^2}-x\frac{dy}{dx}+4y=\cos(\ln x)+x\sin(\ln x)$. [3+3+4]
  18. 1(a) Form the partial differential equation by eliminating arbitrary constants \lq a\rq{} and \lq b\rq{} from \[\ln(az-1)=x^2+ax^3y^3+b.\] What kind of partial differential equation is this?
  19. 1(b) Find the order and degree of the following partial differential equations: \begin{romanparts} \item $\left(\dfrac{\partial^3z}{\partial x^3}+\dfrac{\partial z}{\partial x}\right)^{1/4}=xy^2z^{1/2}+\cos\left(\dfrac{\partial z}{\partial x}\right)$ \item $\left(\dfrac{\partial^5z}{\partial y^5}\right)^{1/9}+xz\dfrac{\partial^2z}{\partial x^2}=(x^2+y^2)e^xz^{1/3}$. \end{romanparts}
  20. 1(c) Solve the partial differential equation $\frac{(b-c)}{a}yzp+\frac{(c-a)}{b}zxq+\frac{(a-b)}{c}xy=0$, $p=\frac{\partial z}{\partial x}$, $q=\frac{\partial z}{\partial y}$. [4+2+4]
  21. 2(a) Form the partial differential equation by eliminating the arbitrary function $\phi$ from \[\sin z=xy\,\phi\left(\frac{x^2}{y}-\frac{z}{x}\right).\]
  22. 2(b) Classify the following partial differential equations: \begin{romanparts} \item $(\ln x-xy^2)p+(y-z)q=ze^x$, \item $xyp+e^yq=\sin(x+y+z)$. \end{romanparts}
  23. 2(c) Solve the partial differential equation $qxy+pq+px-xz=0$, using Charpit's method. [3+2+5]
  24. 3(a) Find the general integral of the partial differential equation \[(x^3+4xy^2)p-(4x^2y+y^3)q+(y^2-x^2)z=0.\] Hence find the particular integral which passes through the straight line $z=1$, $x=y$.
  25. 3(b) Solve the partial differential equation $(p^2+q^2)y=qz$, using Charpit's method. [5+5]
