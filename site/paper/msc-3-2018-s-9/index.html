MMC-33

Fluid Mechanics
M.Sc. Mathematics · Semester III2018
  1. 1(a) Show that the equation of continuity by the Eulerian method and Lagrangian method are equivalent. [3]
  2. 1(b) Consider a two dimensional incompressible steady flow field with velocity components in spherical coordinates $(r,\theta,\varphi)$ given by\par \[v_r=c_1\left(1-\frac32\frac{r_0}{r}+\frac12\frac{r_0^2}{r^2}\right)\cos\theta,\] \[v_\theta=-c_1\left(1-\frac34\frac{r_0}{r}-\frac14\frac{r_0^2}{r^2}\right)\sin\theta,\ v_\varphi=0,\] $r\ge r_0>0$ where $c_1$ and $r_0$ are arbitrary constants. Is the equation of continuity satisfied? [3]
  3. 1(c) If the velocity components for a two-dimensional fluid system are given in the Eulerian system by $u=2x+2y+3t$, $v=x+y+\frac t2$, find the displacement of a fluid particle in the Lagrangian system. [4]
  4. 2(a) Find the condition that the surface $F(x,y,z,t)=0$ may be a boundary surface of a fluid in motion. Also find the normal velocity of the boundary. [2]
  5. 2(b) Establish the relation connecting the angular velocity and the vorticity vector. [2]
  6. 2(c) Show that if the motion of an ideal fluid, for which density is a function of pressure only, is steady and the external forces are conservative, then there exists a family of surfaces which contain the stream lines and vortex lines. [3]
  7. 2(d) At a point in an incompressible fluid having spherical polar co-ordinates $(r,\theta,\varphi)$, the velocity components are $\left(2Mr^{-3}\cos\theta,\,Mr^{-3}\sin\theta,\,0\right)$, where $M$ is a constant. Show the velocity is of the potential kind. Find the velocity potential and the equations of the stream lines. [3]
  8. 3(a) Find the physical significance of stream function. [2]
  9. 3(b) State and prove Milne-Thomson circle theorem. [3]
  10. 3(c) Determine image system for a source outside a circle of radius $a$ with the help of the circle theorem. \par\hfill P.T.O. [2]
  11. 3(d) In the case of the two-dimensional fluid motion produced by a source of strength $m$ placed at a point $S$ outside a rigid circular disc of radius $a$ whose centre is $O$, show that the velocity of slip of the fluid in contact with the disc is greatest at the points where the lines joining $S$ to the ends of the diameter at right angles to $OS$ meet the circle. Prove that its magnitude at these points is $(2m\times OS)/\left(OS^2-a^2\right)$. [3]
  12. 4(a) State and prove Kelvin's minimum energy theorem. [1+2]
  13. 4(b) A velocity field is given by $\vec q=\left(-\vec iy+\vec jx\right)/\left(x^2+y^2\right)$. Determine whether the flow is irrotational. Calculate the circulation round a unit circle with centre at the origin. [2]
  14. 4(c) Determine the motion of a circular cylinder moving in an infinite mass of the liquid at rest at infinity, with velocity $U$ in the direction of $x$-axis. Also find the equation of the stream lines. [4+1]
  15. 5(a) Prove that the state of stress at a point is completely known if the nine components of stress tensor at that point are known. [3]
  16. 5(b) If in two dimensions, $x$ axis and $y$ axis are rotated through an angle $\theta$, then find the expressions for new stress components in terms of old stress components. Also find the basic invariants for two-dimensional stress components. [4]
  17. 5(c) The stress matrix at a point $P$ is given by $\begin{pmatrix}2&1&-3\\1&1&2\\-3&2&1\end{pmatrix}$. Find the stress vector on the plane passing through $P$ and parallel to the plane whose unit normal is $\dfrac37\vec i+\dfrac67\vec j+\dfrac27\vec k$. Also determine the principal stresses and principal directions for this stress tensor. [3]
  18. 6(a) Derive the Navier-Stokes equation of motion for an incompressible viscous fluid assuming the constitutive equations for Newtonian fluid. [5]
  19. 6(b) Discuss the generalized Coutte flow. Determine the volumetric flow rate, maximum and minimum velocities, shearing stress, skin friction and the coefficient of friction for this flow. [5]
