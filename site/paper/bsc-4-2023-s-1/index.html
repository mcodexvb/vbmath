CCMA-8

B.Sc. Mathematics · Semester IV2023
  1. 1(a) Prove that if $\lim_{(x,y)\to(a,b)}f(x,y)$ exists finitely then $f(x,y)$ is necessarily bounded in some deleted neighbourhood of the point $(a,b)$. [3]
  2. 1(b) Using $\epsilon-\delta$ definition, show that $\lim_{(x,y)\to(0,0)}\frac{xy}{|x|+|y|}=0$. [3]
  3. 1(c) Prove that if $f(x,y)$ is continuous at a point $(a,b)$ then so is $|f(x,y)|$. Show by an example the converse may not be true. [2+2]
  4. 2(a) Show that the function $f(x,y)=\begin{cases}\frac{xy}{\sqrt{x^2+y^2}}&\text{for }(x,y)\ne(0,0)\\0&\text{for }(x,y)=(0,0)\end{cases}$ is continuous at the $(0,0)$. [2]
  5. 2(b) Show by an example for a function $f(x,y)$ of two variables $x$ and $y$ that $f_x(a,b)$ and $f_y(a,b)$ both exist but $f(x,y)$ is not continuous at $(a,b)$. [4]
  6. 2(c) State Euler's theorem for a homogeneous function of degree $n$. If $f(x,y)$ is homogeneous function of degree $n$ and if $f(x,y)$ possesses continuous partial derivatives upto second order then show that \[x^2\frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial x^2}+2xy\frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial x\partial y}+y^2\frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial y^2}=n(n-1)f.\] [1+3]
  7. 3(a) When a function $f(x,y)$ is said to be differentiable at a point $(a,b)$. If $f(x,y)$ is differentiable at a point $(a,b)$ then show that the partial derivatives $\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}$ and $\frac{\partial f}{\partial y}$ both exist finitely at $(a,b)$. [1+2]
  8. 3(b) State and prove Schwarz's theorem for equality of two mixed second order partial derivatives of a function $f(x,y)$ at a point $(a,b)$. [1+4]
  9. 3(c) Show that $x^2-xy^2-2y^5=0$ determines $x$ uniquely as a function of $y$ near the point $(-1,1)$. [2]
  10. 4(a) If $f(x,y)=x^2-3xy+2y^2$, then by using mean value theorem express the difference $f(1,2)-f(2,-1)$ by partial derivatives. Also compute $\theta$ and check that it lies between 0 and 1. [3]
  11. 4(b) State and prove Taylor's theorem for a function of two variables. [5]
  12. 4(c) Expand $x^4+x^2y^2-y^4$ about $(1,1)$ up to terms of the second degree. [2]
  13. 5(a) Show that the function $f(x,y)=2x^4-3x^2y+y^2$ has neither a maximum nor a minimum at $(0,0)$. [4]
  14. 5(b) Use Lagrange method to find the shortest distance from the point $(1,0)$ to the parabola $y^2=4x$. [3]
  15. 5(c) Evaluate $\iint_R x^3y\,dx\,dy$ over $R:\{(x,y):0\le x\le1;0\le y\le2\}$. [3]
  16. 1 Answer \emph{any five} questions. [$5\times2=10$]
  17. 1(a) Let $y_1(x)=1/x$ and $y_2(x)=x^{3/2}$ be a fundamental set of solutions of the following ordinary differential equation (ODE), and $c_1$ and $c_2$ be any two arbitrary constants. \[2x^2\frac{d^2y}{dx^2}+x\frac{dy}{dx}-3y=0.\] Use the principle of superposition to show that the function $y(x)=c_1y_1(x)+c_2y_2(x)$ is also a solution of the ODE.
  18. 1(b) Factorize the operator of the differential equation: $[D^2+(2x-3)D-6x]y=0$ and hence solve. Here, $D^2\equiv d^2/dx^2$ and $D\equiv d/dx$.
  19. 1(c) Define Pfaffian differential equation in $n$ variables and define its integrating factor in the case of two variables.
  20. 1(d) Define regular and irregular singular points of a second order ODE of the form: \[a_0(x)\frac{d^2y}{dx^2}+a_1(x)\frac{dy}{dx}+a_2(x)y=0,\] and give examples.
  21. 1(e) State the basic differences between the conditions of integrability and exactness of the differential equation: $P(x,y,z)\,dx+Q(x,y,z)\,dy+R(x,y,z)\,dz=0$.
  22. 1(f) Convert the following second order ODE into two first-order ODEs. \[\frac{d^2y}{dx^2}+2\frac{dy}{dx}-143y=0,\] with the initial conditions: $y=-13$ and $dy/dx=11$ at $x=0$.
  23. 1(g) Show that the following nonlinear ODE is exact and find its first integral. \[2x^2\cos y\frac{d^2y}{dx^2}-2x^2\sin y\left(\frac{dy}{dx}\right)^2+x\cos y\frac{dy}{dx}-\sin y=x^2.\]
  24. 1(h) Identify the number of independent arbitrary constants in the general solution of the following system of equations: \[\begin{aligned}\phi_1(D)x+\phi_2(D)y&=\phi(t),\\\psi_1(D)x+\psi_2(D)y&=\psi(t),\quad D\equiv d/dt.\end{aligned}\]
  25. 2(a) If $y=u(x)$ is a solution of the differential equation: \[\frac{d^2y}{dx^2}+P(x)\frac{dy}{dx}+Q(x)y=0,\] then show that $y=c_1u(x)+c_2v(x)$ is its general solution, where $c_1$ and $c_2$ are two arbitrary constants, and \[v(x)=u(x)\int\frac{\exp[-\int P(x)\,dx]}{[u(x)]^2}.\] [5]
  26. 2(b) Solve the following system of differential equations by evaluating the eigenvalues and the corresponding eigenvectors of the coefficient matrix. \[\frac{dx}{dt}=-x-y,\qquad\frac{dy}{dt}=x-y.\] Given that $x(t=0)=-4$, and $y(t=0)=2$. [5]
  27. 3(a) Solve the differential equation: \[\frac{d^2y}{dx^2}+2x\frac{dy}{dx}+(x^2-8)y=x^2e^{-x^2/2}\] by reducing it to its normal form. [4]
  28. 3(b) Using the power series method, solve the following ODE about an ordinary point $x_0=0$. \[\frac{d^2y}{dx^2}+(9-x)y=0.\] [6]
  29. 4(a) Find the complete integral of the following set of differential equations and hence give its geometrical interpretation. \[\frac{dx}{y-zx}=\frac{dy}{yz+x}=\frac{dz}{x^2+y^2}.\] [1+4]
  30. 4(b) Verify that the following differential equation is integrable and hence find its solution. \[(ye^x+e^z)\,dx+(ze^y+e^x)\,dy+(e^y-ye^x-ze^y)\,dz=0.\] [2+3]
