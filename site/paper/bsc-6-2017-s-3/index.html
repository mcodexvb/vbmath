BMC-63

B.Sc. Mathematics · Semester VI2017
  1. 1(a) Find the spherical coordinates of a point whose cylindrical coordinates are $\left(2\sqrt2,\frac\pi4,1\right)$. [2]
  2. 1(b) Show that the equation $x^1=4\cos x^2$ in spherical coordinates represents a sphere. [2]
  3. 1(c) A covariant vector has components $xy,2y-z^2$ and $xz$ in rectangular cartesian coordinates. Find its covariant components in spherical polar coordinates. [6]
  4. 2(a) Let a quantity $A(p,q,r,s)$ in the coordinate system $(x^i)$ be such that $A(p,q,r,s)B_n^{qsm}=C_{rn}^{pm}$, where $B_n^{qsm}$ is an arbitrary tensor and $C_{rn}^{pm}$ is a given tensor. Prove that $A(p,q,r,s)$ is a tensor. [5]
  5. 2(b) If $A_i$ is a covariant vector, then show that $\frac{\partial A_i}{\partial x^j}-\frac{\partial A_j}{\partial x^i}$ are the components of a tensor of type $(0,2)$. [2]
  6. 2(c) If $A_{ijkl}$ is symmetric in the first two indices from left and skew symmetric in the second and fourth indices from the left then show that $A_{ijkl}=O$. [2]
  7. 2(d) If $A_{ij}$ is skew symmetric, then show that $\left(\delta_j^i\delta_p^k+\delta_p^i\delta_j^k\right)A_{ik}=0$. [1]
  8. 3(a) Calculate the Christoffel symbols $\left\{{1\atop33}\right\},\left\{{2\atop33}\right\},\left\{{3\atop32}\right\},\left\{{2\atop12}\right\},\left\{{3\atop13}\right\}$ corresponding to the metric $dS^2=(dx^1)^2+(x^1)^2(dx^2)^2+(x^1\sin x^2)^2(dx^3)^2$. [6]
  9. 3(b) If the metric in rectangular cartesian coordinates is given by $dS^2=(dx^1)^2+(dx^2)^2$, then find the metric in polar coordinates. [2.5]
  10. 3(c) If $A^i$ and $B^i$ are orthogonal vectors each of length $l$, then find the value of $(g_{hj}g_{ki}-g_{hk}g_{ji})A^hB^jA^kB^i$. [1.5]
  11. 4(a) Explain the difference between a distribution of sample and a sampling distribution. A sample of size $n$ ($X:x_1,x_2,\dots,x_n$) is drawn from a population. How does the distribution function of the sample approximates to that of the population? What does it imply in this context?
  12. 4(b) What is estimation of parameter? If $f(x)=2\gamma\sqrt{\frac\gamma\pi}x^2e^{-\gamma x^2}$, $-\infty<x<\infty$, find the maximum likelihood estimate (MLE) for $\gamma$. Show that the estimate is \textit{unbiased}. [1+2+2]
  13. 5(a) Let $T_1$ and $T_2$ are two independent unbiased estimators of parameter $\theta$ having the same variance. Which of the estimators $\frac12(T_1+T_2)$ and $\frac13(T_1+2T_2)$ you would prefer for estimating $\theta$? Why? [2]
  14. 5(b) The heights in inches of 7 students of a college, chosen randomly were as follows: 62.2, 63.1, 62.4, 63.2, 65.5, 66.2, 66.3.\\Compute 95\% confidence interval for the mean of the population of heights of students of the college, assuming it to be normal.\\{}[Given $P(t>2.447)=0.025$] [4]
  15. 5(c) Fit a straight line $y=a+bx$ to the following data:\\[2pt] \begin{tabular}{|c|c|c|c|c|c|}\hline $x$&0&1&2&3&4\\\hline $y$&1.0&1.8&3.3&4.5&6.3\\\hline\end{tabular}\\[2pt] Hence, find the difference between the actual value of $y$ and its value obtain from the fitted line when $x=4$. [3+1]
  16. 6(a) In the context of statistical hypothesis, explain what do you mean by the following:\\i) Simple and Composite hypotheses,\\ii) Type I and Type II errors. [2+2]
  17. 6(b) State Neyman-Pearson theorem on the best critical region for testing a simple hypothesis $H_o:\theta=\theta_0$ against an alternative simple hypothesis $H_o:\theta=\theta_1$. [2]
  18. 6(c) In order to test whether a coin is perfect, the coin is tossed 5 times. The null hypothesis of the perfectness is rejected if and only if more than 4 heads are obtained. What is the probability of Type I error? Find the probability of Type II error when the corresponding probability of head is 0.2. \fbox{\parbox{3.4cm}{\small Use separate answer scripts for each unit}} [2+2]
