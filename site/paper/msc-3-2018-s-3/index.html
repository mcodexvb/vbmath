MMC-13

Algebra-I
M.Sc. Mathematics · Semester III2018
  1. 1(a) Let $G_1$ and $G_2$ be two groups and $a\in G_1,b\in G_2$. Show that $0((a,b))=\mathrm{lcm}(0(a),0(b))$. Also find the number of generators of $\mathbb Z_{30}\times\mathbb Z_{77}$. [3]
  2. 1(b) Show that the group $(\mathbb Q,+)$ can not be expressed as an internal direct product of two nontrivial subgroups. [2]
  3. 1(c) Find the conjugacy class equation of $S_4$. [2]
  4. 1(d) Let $H$ be a subgroup of a group $G$. Show that $H$ is a normal subgroup if and only if $H$ is a union of conjugacy classes of $G$. [3]
  5. 2(a) State and prove Cauchy's theorem for abelian groups. [4]
  6. 2(b) Let $G$ be a noncyclic group of order 21. Find the number of elements of order 3 in $G$. [3]
  7. 2(c) Let $G$ be a finite group and $p$ be the smallest prime divisor of $|G|$. Then prove that every subgroup $H$ of $G$ of index $p$ is normal in $G$. [3]
  8. 3(a) Let $G$ be a group of order $p^nm$ where $p$ is a prime and $n\in\mathbb N$. If $H$ is a subgroup of $G$ of order $p^i$, $i<n$ then show that $G$ has a subgroup $K$ of order $p^{i+1}$ such that $H\subseteq K$. [2]
  9. 3(b) Show that every group of order 99 is abelian. [3]
  10. 3(c) Let $H$ and $K$ be two subgroups of a group $G$ such that $H$ is normal in $G$. If both $H$ and $K$ are solvable then show that $HK$ is solvable. [2]
  11. 3(d) Let $G$ be a group. Prove that $G$ is solvable if and only if $G^{(n)}=\{e\}$ for some positive integer $n$. Hence or otherwise show that $S_5$ is not solvable. [3]
  12. 4(a) A linear transformation $T:\mathbb R^2\to\mathbb R^2$ is given by $T(x,y)=(x+y,\ x-y)$ for all $(x,y)\in\mathbb R^2$. Find the image of the line $2x+3y=6$ under $T$. [2]
  13. 4(b) Find the rank of the linear transformation $T:P_2(\mathbb R)\to M_2(\mathbb R)$ defined by\par \[T(p(t))=\begin{pmatrix}f(0)+f(1)&0\\0&f(2)\end{pmatrix}.\] \par\hfill P.T.O. [2]
  14. 4(c) Let $T:V\to V$ be a linear transformation. If the matrix representations of $T$ relative to any basis are the same then show that $T=c\,I_V$. For some $c\in F$. Does the converse hold? [3]
  15. 4(c) Let $P$ be the change of basis matrix from a basis $\beta$ into the basis $\beta'$. Show that $P$ is invertible and $P^{-1}$ is the change of basis matrix from $\beta'$ into $\beta$. [3]
  16. 5(a) With the help of a suitably defined mapping from $V$ onto $V^{**}$, show that $V$ and $V^{**}$ are isomorphic. [3]
  17. 5(b) Let $V$ be a finite dimensional vector space of dimension $n$. Show that $f,g\in V^*$ are linearly independent if and only if $\dim(\ker f\cap\ker g)=n-2$. What happens, if we consider three linear functionals? [4]
  18. 5(c) Let $U=L(\{(1,2,1),(2,-3,1)\})$. Find $U^0$. [3]
  19. 6(a) Find all eigen values and a basis of the eigen space of the eigen value of algebraic multiplicity 2 of $T:\mathbb R^3\to\mathbb R^3$ defined by $T(x,y,z)=(2x+y,\ y-z,\ 2y+4z)$. [4]
  20. 6(b) Let $T:V\to V$ be a linear transformation. If $W$ is a $T$-invariant subspace of $V$, prove that $\chi_{T_W}(t)\,|\,\chi_T(t)$. Hence or otherwise prove that geometric multiplicity of an eigen value cannot exceed its algebraic multiplicity. [3]
  21. 6(c) Let $T:V\to V$ be a linear transformation such that $V$ is $T$-cyclic. If $S:V\to V$ is a linear transformation such that $ST=TS$, then show that $S=g(T)$ for some polynomial $g(t)$. [3]
