MMC-22

Topology
M.Sc. Mathematics · Semester II2026
  1. 1(a) Prove Cantor's theorem: for every set $A$, there is no onto map from $A$ to $\mathcal P(A)$. Hence show that $|A|<|\mathcal P(A)|$. [4]
  2. 1(b) Let $u$ and $v$ be cardinal numbers. Define the cardinal sum $u+v$, the cardinal product $uv$, and the cardinal power $u^v$. If $c=|\mathbb R|$ and $a=|\mathbb N|$, prove that\par \[c+c=c+a=c\cdot c=a+c=a\cdot c=c.\] [2+4]
  3. 2(a) Define a metrizable space. Prove that the space $X=\{a,b\}$ with the topology $\tau=\{X,\emptyset,\{a\}\}$ is not metrizable. [3]
  4. 2(b) Let $X=\{a,b,c,d\}$ and\par \[\tau=\{\emptyset,X,\{a\},\{a,b\},\{a,c\},\{a,b,c\}\}.\] Show that $\tau$ is a topology on $X$. Also determine whether $(X,\tau)$ is normal. [2+2]
  5. 2(c) For a subset $A$ of a topological space $X$, prove that $\bar A=X\setminus(X\setminus A)^\circ$. [3]
  6. 3(a) Define when one topology on a set $X$ is said to be finer than another topology on $X$. State and prove the characterization of finer topologies in terms of bases. [1+3]
  7. 3(b) Let $f:X\to Y$ be a map between topological spaces. Show that the following statements are equivalent:\par (i) $f$ is continuous.\par (ii) $f^{-1}(F)$ is closed in $X$ for every closed set $F$ in $Y$.\par (iii) $f(\bar A)\subset\overline{f(A)}$ for every subset $A\subseteq X$. [6]
  8. 4(a) Let $X$ be a topological space. Show that the following statements are equivalent:\par (i) $X$ is Hausdorff.\par (ii) The diagonal\par \[\Delta=\{(x,x):x\in X\}\] is closed in the product space $X\times X$.\par (iii) Every convergent filter on $X$ has a unique limit. [6]
  9. 4(b) Define completely regular spaces and $T_{3\frac12}$-spaces. Give an example to show that a Hausdorff space need not be $T_{3\frac12}$. [4]
  10. 5(a) Prove that every closed subspace of a compact topological space is compact. [2]
  11. 5(b) Prove that every compact Hausdorff space is normal. [5]
  12. 5(c) Prove that a continuous bijection from a compact space onto a Hausdorff space is a homeomorphism. [3]
  13. 6(a) Let $X$ be a first countable space and $A\subseteq X$. Show that a point $a\in X$ belongs to $\bar A$ if and only if there is a sequence $\{a_n\}$ in $A$ converging to $a$. Is the first countability assumption essential? Justify your answer. [5]
  14. 6(b) Show that every metric space is first countable in its metric topology. [2]
  15. 6(c) Let $\mathbf R_\ell$ denote the Sorgenfrey line, show that $\mathbf R_\ell$ is separable but not second countable. Deduce that $\mathbf R_\ell$ is not metrizable. [3]
