MMO-41(P4)

Advanced Complex Analysis-II
M.Sc. Mathematics · Semester IV2018
  1. 1(a) Prove that if $f$ is meromorphic in the extended complex plane then it is a rational function.
  2. 1(b) Prove that $\mathrm{cosec}\,z=\dfrac1z+\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^n\dfrac{2z}{z^2-n^2\pi^2}.$
  3. 1(c) Let $f(z)$ be meromorphic in $|z|<R$ and $f(0)\ne0$. Further suppose that it has zeros at $\alpha_1,\alpha_2,\ldots\alpha_m$ and poles at $\beta_1,\beta_2,\ldots\beta_n$ with moduli not exceeding $r\,(0<r<R)$. Then show that $\log\left\{\left|\dfrac{\beta_1,\beta_2,\ldots\beta_n}{\alpha_1,\alpha_2,\ldots\alpha_m}f(0)\right|r^{m-n}\right\}=\dfrac1{2\pi}\displaystyle\int_0^{2\pi}\log\left|f\left(re^{i\theta}\right)\right|d\theta.$
  4. 1(d) State Poisson's integral formula. [3+4+2+1]
  5. 2(a) Let $b_1,b_2,\ldots b_n$ be the poles of $f(z)$ in $|z|<R$ and be analytic and non-zero else where inside and on $|z|=R$. Then show that if $z=re^{i\theta}\,(0\le r<R),\ f(z)\ne\infty$, then \[\log\left|f\left(re^{i\theta}\right)\right|=\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}\frac{R^2-r^2}{R^2-2rR\cos(\theta-\varphi)+r^2}\log\left|f\left(Re^{i\varphi}\right)\right|d\varphi+\sum_{\gamma=1}^{n}\log\left|\frac{R^2-\bar b_\gamma re^{i\theta}}{R\left(re^{i\theta}-b_\gamma\right)}\right|.\]
  6. 2(b) For any meromorphic function $f$ and finite complex constant `a', define $m(r,a),\ n(r,a),\ N(r,a).$
  7. 2(c) For any meromorphic function $f$ with $f(0)\ne0,\infty$, establish the formula $T(r,f)-T\left(r,\dfrac1f\right)=\log|f(0)|.$
  8. 2(d) If $f_1,f_2,\ldots,f_p$ be $p$ meromorphic functions, then show that $T\left(r,\displaystyle\prod_{\gamma=1}^{p}f_\gamma\right)\le\displaystyle\sum_{\gamma=1}^{p}T(r,f_\gamma).$\par Give an example to show sign of equality may occur for two meromorphic functions. [3+2+2+3]
  9. 3(a) Find $T(r,f)$ when $f(z)=\dfrac{z^2+1}{(z-1)^2(z-2)}.$
  10. 3(b) Show that $|T(r,f+a)-T(r,f-a)|\le2\log^+|a|+\log4,$ where `$a$'$(\ne\infty)$ is a complex constant.
  11. 3(c) State and Prove Cartan's identity. \par\hfill P.T.O. \begin{center}[ 2 ]\end{center}
  12. 3(d) Prove that for a meromorphic function $f,\ T(r,f)$ is an increasing convex function of $\log r$. [2+2+4+2]
  13. 4(a) Let $S(r)$ be a real valued and non negative increasing function for $r_0<r<\infty$ and $r_0>0$. If $S(r)$ is of order $\rho\,(0<\rho<\infty)$ and has convergence class, then prove that $S(r)$ has minimal type. Does the converse hold? Support your answer.
  14. 4(b) If $f$ is an entire function then prove that the functions $S_1(r)=\log^+M(r,f)$ and $S_2(r)=T(r,f)$ have the same order. Further if the value of the common order be $\rho\,(0<\rho<\infty)$, then show $S_1(r)$ and $S_2(r)$ are together minimal type, mean type, maximal type or convergence class.
  15. 4(c) Define the order of a meromorphic function. If $\rho_1,\rho_2$ be the orders of the meromorphic functions $f_1(z)$ and $f_2(z)$ respectively, then show that order of $f_1-f_2\le\max\{\rho_1,\rho_2\}.$ [2.5+5+2.5]
  16. 5(a) Let $f(z)$ be a meromorphic function of order $\rho$. Then prove that for every `a' and arbitrary $\epsilon>0$\par \hspace*{1em}(i)\enspace $n(r,a)=O\left(r^{\rho+\epsilon}\right)$\par \hspace*{1em}(ii)\enspace $\sum\left(\dfrac1{r_n(a)}\right)^{\rho+\epsilon}$ is convergent, where $r_1(a),r_2(a),\ldots$ are the moduli of `a' point of $f(z)$ ordered by increasing magnitude.
  17. 5(b) State and Prove Nevanlinna's theorem on deficient values.
  18. 5(c) Define $\Theta(a,f)$ and fine $\Theta\left(0,e^z\right).$
  19. 5(d) State Milloux's theorem. [3+4+2+1]
  20. 6(a) Let $f$ and $g$ be non-constant polynomials and `a' be a finite complex number. If $f$ and $g$ share `a' CM anf if there exists a point $\alpha$ such that $f(\alpha)=g(\alpha)\ne a$, then show that $f\equiv g.$
  21. 6(b) State and Prove Nevanlinna's five point uniqueness theorem.
  22. 6(c) Give an example (with proper justification) to show that there exist functions $f$ and $g$ share four values IM but $f\not\equiv g.$
  23. 6(d) If $f(z)$ be a non-constant meromorphic function, then show that there can be at most four distinct values `a' for which $f(z)=a$ has only multiple roots. [2+4+2+2]
