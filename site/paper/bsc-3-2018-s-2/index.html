CC-6

Algebra-II
B.Sc. Mathematics · Semester III2018
  1. 1 Answer \textbf{\textit{any two}} questions: \hfill \(2\times10=20\)
  2. 1(a) \begin{romanparts} \item In the group \(\mathbb C^*=\mathbb C\setminus\{0\}\), find all cosets of the subgroup \(H=\{z\in C^*:|z|=1\}\) and interpret geometrically. \hfill \(3+2\) \item Let \(G\) be a finite group of order \(n\) and \(a\in G\). Then show \(O(a)\) divides \(n\) and \(a^n=e\). \hfill 2 \item Let \(G\) be a group of order less than 320. Suppose \(G\) has subgroups of order 35 and 45. Find the exact order of \(G\). \hfill 3 \end{romanparts}
  3. 1(b) \begin{romanparts} \item Let \(H\) be a subgroup of a group \(G\). If \(x^2\in H\) for all \(x\in G\), then show that \(H\) is a normal subgroup of \(G\) and \(G/H\) is commutative. \hfill \(1+2\) \item Let \(H\) be a normal subgroup of a group \(G\) such that \(H\) contains only two elements. Show that \(a,b\in G\). \hfill 3 \item Give an example of a group for which a subgroup of it is not normal. Justify your answer. \hfill 2 \item Let \(G=\langle a\rangle\) be the cyclic group such that \(O(a)=12\). Let \(H=\langle a^4\rangle\). Find the order of \(a^3H\) in \(G/H\). \hfill 2 \end{romanparts}
  4. 1(c) \begin{romanparts} \item State and proof Cauchy's theorem for finite groups. \hfill \(1+4\) \item Let \(G\) be a group of order \(H\) where \(p\) is prime. Write the class equation for \(G\). \hfill 3 \item Let \(G\) be a commutative group of order 15. How many subgroups of order 5 are there in \(G\)? \hfill 2 \end{romanparts}
  5. 1(d) \begin{romanparts} \item Let \(\Pi=(123)\in S_4\). Find a conjugate of \(\Pi\). Give an example of an element of order 2 which is not a 2-cycle in \(S_4\). Justify your answer. \hfill \(2+2\) \item Show that the number of even permutations in \(S_n\), \((n\ge2)\) is the same as the number of odd permutations. \hfill 4 \item Find the number of distinct 5-cycles in \(S_7\). \hfill 2 \end{romanparts}
  6. 2 Answer \textbf{\textit{any two}} questions: \hfill \(2\times10=20\)
  7. 2(a) \begin{romanparts} \item Let \(R\) be a finite ring without zero divisors. Then show that \(R\) contains unity. Give an example to show that the result does not hold for an infinite ring without having zero divisors. \hfill \(4+1\) \item Prove that every maximal ideal in a commutative ring with unity is prime. If the condition of commutativity is dropped, does the result hold? Justify your answer. \hfill \(4+1\) \end{romanparts}
  8. 2(b) \begin{romanparts} \item Show that the field \(\mathbb Q\) of rational numbers has no proper subfields. \hfill 4 \item Give an example of a ring R and a subring \(S\) of \(R\) having different unities. \hfill 4 \item Let \(R\) be a ring with unity, and \(a\in R\) be a nilpotent in \(R\). Show that \(1+a\) is a unit in \(R\). \hfill 2 \end{romanparts}
  9. 2(c) \begin{romanparts} \item Show that characteristic of a finite integral domain is prime. Give an example of an integral domain which have finite characteristic. Justify your answer. \hfill \(3+2\) \item Show that the set of all nilpotent elements in a commutative ring \(R\) forms an ideal of \(R\). \hfill 2 \item Let \(I\) be an ideal of \(R\). Show that \((I,+)\) is a normal subgroup of \((R,+)\). \hfill 3 \end{romanparts}
  10. 2(d) \begin{romanparts} \item Show that the ideal \(M_c=\{f(x)\in C[0,1]:f(c)=0\}\) is a maximal ideal in the ring \(C[0,1]\). \hfill 3 \item Let \(R\) be an integral domain. Show that if every ideal of \(R\) is prime, then \(R\) is a field. Hence show that \(\mathbb Z\) is not a field. \hfill \(2+1\) \item Let \(I\) be an ideal of a ring \(R\) with unity. Prove that \(R/I\) is a skewfield if and only if \(I\) is maximal. \hfill 4 \end{romanparts}
  11. 3 Answer \textbf{\textit{any two}} questions: \hfill \(2\times10=20\)
  12. 3(a) \begin{romanparts} \item Is the set of all positive real numbers \(\mathbb R^+\) a vector space over \(\mathbb R\) with respect to usual addition in \(\mathbb R^+\) and scalar multiplication defined by \(cx=x^c\) for \(x\in\mathbb R^+,\ c\in\mathbb R\)? Justify your answer. \hfill 3 \item Let \(V\) be a vector space over \(F\). Show that \(V\) is also a vector space over any subfield of \(F\). \hfill 3 \item Let \(W_1\) and \(W_2\) be two subspaces of a vector space \(V\). Show that \(W_1+W_2\) is the subspace of \(V\) generated by \(W_1\cup W_2\). \hfill 4 \end{romanparts}
  13. 3(b) \begin{romanparts} \item Let \(S\) be a linearly independent subset of a vector space \(V\), and \(v\in V\setminus S\). Then \(S\cup\{v\}\) is linearly dependent if and only if \(v\in\operatorname{span}(s)\). \hfill 4 \item Let \(S=\{(1,2),(2,1),(1,0),(5,3)\}\subset\mathbb R^2\). Find a smallest subset \(T\) of \(S\) such that \(L(S)=L(T)\). \hfill 3 \item Let \(W\) be a subspace of \(V\). Show that there exist a subspace \(W'\) of \(V\) such that \(V=W\oplus W'\). \hfill 3 \end{romanparts}
  14. 3(c) \begin{romanparts} \item Extend the set \(S=\{(1,-2,5,-3),(0,7,-9,2)\}\) to a basis of \(\mathbb R^4\). \hfill 4 \item Give two different bases of \(\mathbb Z_5^3\) over \(\mathbb Z_5\). \hfill 3 \item Show that every finitely generated vector space has a basis. \hfill 3 \end{romanparts}
  15. 3(d) \begin{romanparts} \item Let \(V\) be an inner product space over a field \(F\). For \(x,y\in V\), show that \(\lvert\langle x,y\rangle\rvert\le\lVert x\rVert.\lVert y\rVert\). \hfill 2 \item Consider the subspace \(U\) of \(\mathbb R^4\) spanned by the vectors: \[v_1=(1,1,1,1),\quad v_2=(1,1,2,4),\quad v_3=(1,2,-4,-3).\] Find an orthonormal basis of \(U\). \hfill 4 \item Let \(\beta\) be a basis for a subspace \(W\) of an inner product space \(V\), and let \(z\in V\). Prove that \(z\in W^\perp\) if and only if \(\langle z,v\rangle=0\) for every \(v\in\beta\). \hfill 4 \end{romanparts}
