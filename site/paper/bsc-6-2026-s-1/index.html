MJMA-14

B.Sc. Mathematics · Semester VI2026
  1. 1(a) Let $F$ be a field, and $f(x),g(x)\in F[x]$ with $g(x)\ne0$. Prove that there exist two polynomials $q(x),r(x)\in F[x]$ such that $f(x)=g(x)q(x)+r(x)$ where either $r(x)=0$ or $\deg r(x)<\deg g(x)$. [You need not to prove uniqueness] [[4]]
  2. 1(b) Let $F$ be a field and $f(x)$ be a nonzero polynomial in $F[x]$ of degree $n$. Then show that $f(x)$ has at most $n$ roots in $F$ counting multiplicity. [[3]]
  3. 1(c) Find the quotient and the remainder if $f(x)=x^4+[3]x^3+[2]x^2+[2]$ is divided by $g(x)=x^2+[2]x+[1]$ over $\mathbb Z_5$. [[3]]
  4. 2(a) Let $F$ be a field. Show that every ideal in $F[x]$ is a principal ideal. [[4]]
  5. 2(b) Let $F$ be a field and $p(x)\in F[x]$ be a nonconstant polynomial. Show that $p(x)$ is irreducible if and only if for any $f(x),g(x)\in F[x]$, $p(x)\mid f(x)g(x)$ implies that $p(x)\mid f(x)$ or $p(x)\mid g(x)$. [[3]]
  6. 2(c) Let $F$ be a field and $f(x),g(x)\in F[x]$ be two nonzero polynomials. If $f(x)$ and $g(x)$ are relatively prime, then show that for any $h(x)\in F[x]$, $f(x)\mid h(x)$ and $g(x)\mid h(x)$ imply $f(x)g(x)\mid h(x)$. [[3]]
  7. 3(a) Let $F$ be a field, and $f(x)\in F[x]$ be a polynomial of degree 2 or 3. Prove that $f(x)$ is reducible over $F$ if and only if $f(x)$ has a root in $F$. [[3]]
  8. 3(b) State and prove the Eisenstein's irreducibility criterion for polynomials over $\mathbb Z$. [[4]]
  9. 3(c) Construct a field of order 8. [[3]]
  10. 4(a) Define associate in an integral domain. Let $D$ be an integral domain and $a,b\in D$. Prove that $a$ and $b$ are associates if and only if $\langle a\rangle=\langle b\rangle$. [[4]]
  11. 4(b) Show that both $[4]$ and $[6]$ are gcd of $[4]$ and $[6]$ in $\mathbb Z_{10}$. [[3]]
  12. 4(c) Find all units in $\mathbb Z[i]$. [[3]]
  13. 5(a) Define prime elements in an integral domain. Prove that in an integral domain every prime element is irreducible. [[4]]
  14. 5(b) Give an example of a prime element which is not irreducible. [[3]]
  15. 5(c) Let $E$ be a Euclidean domain with the valuation $v$, and $a\in E^*$. Show that $a$ is a unit if and only if $v(a)=v(1)$. [[3]]
  16. 6(a) Show that $\mathbb Z[x]$ is not a Euclidean domain. [[3]]
  17. 6(b) Let $D$ be a principal ideal domain, and $a,b\in D$ be two nonzero elements. Prove that $\gcd(a,b)$ exists in $D$. [[3]]
  18. 6(c) Let $D$ be a principal ideal domain and $p\in D$. Then $p$ is irreducible if and only if it is prime. [[4]]
  19. 7(a) Let $A\in M_{n\times n}(\mathbb F)$ be a constant row sum matrix with the row sum $c$. Show that $c$ is an eigen value of $A$. [[2]]
  20. 7(b) Let $A,B\in M_{3\times3}(\mathbb R)$. Show that $3\det(AB-BA)=\mathrm{trace}(AB-BA)^3$. [[3]]
  21. 7(c) Show that a scalar $\lambda$ is an eigenvalue of $A\in M_{n\times n}(\mathbb F)$ if and only if $\lambda$ is a root of the characteristic polynomial of $A$. [[5]]
  22. 8(a) Define eigen value of a linear operator $T:V\to V$ on a vector space $V$. Show that for every eigenvalue $c$ of $T$, $v\in E_c(T)$ if and only if $[v]_\beta\in E_c([T]_\beta)$. [[1+4]]
  23. 8(b) Find all eigen vectors of $T:P_2(\mathbb R)\to P_2(\mathbb R)$ defined by $T(a+bx+cx^2)=(a-3b+3c)+(3a-5b+3c)x+(6a-6b+4c)x^2$. [[5]]
  24. 9(a) Let $T$ be a linear operator on a finite-dimensional vector space $V$ such that the characteristic polynomial of $T$ splits. Let $\lambda_1,\lambda_2,\dots,\lambda_k$ be the distinct eigenvalues of $T$. Prove that $T$ is diagonalizable if and only if the multiplicity of $\lambda_i$ is equal to $\dim(E_{\lambda_i})$ for all $i$. [[3+3]]
  25. 9(b) Check the diagonalizability of the linear operator $T:P_2(\mathbb R)\to P_2(\mathbb R)$ defined by $T(f(x))=f(1)+f'(0)x+(f'(0)+f''(0))x^2$. [[4]]
  26. 10(a) Let $T$ be a linear operator on a finite-dimensional vector space $V$, and let $W$ denote the $T$-cyclic subspace of $V$ generated by a nonzero vector $v\in V$. Let $k=\dim(W)$. Suppose that $a_0v+a_1T(v)+\dots+a_{k-1}T^{k-1}(v)+T^k(v)=0$. Show that the characteristic polynomial of $T_W$ is $f(t)=(-1)^k(a_0+a_1t+\dots+a_{k-1}t^{k-1}+t^k)$. [[6]]
  27. 10(b) Let $T$ be a linear operator on a finite-dimensional vector space $V$, and let $W$ be a $T$-invariant subspace of $V$. Show that the characteristic polynomial of $T|_W$ divides the characteristic polynomial of $T$. [[4]]
  28. 11(a) Let $T$ be the linear operator on $P_2(\mathbb R)$ defined by $T(g(x))=-g(x)-g'(x)$. Find a Jordan canonical form of $T$ and a Jordan canonical basis for $T$. [[6]]
  29. 11(b) Define a generalized eigenvector of a linear operator $T$. Let $T$ be a linear operator on a vector space $V$, and let $\lambda$ be an eigenvalue of $T$. Show that for any scalar $\mu\ne\lambda$, the restriction of $T-\mu I$ to $K_\lambda$ is one-to-one. [[1+3]]
  30. 12(a) Let $T$ be a linear operator on a finite-dimensional vector space $V$. Suppose that the minimal polynomial of $T$ is of the form $p(t)=(t-\lambda_1)(t-\lambda_2)\cdots(t-\lambda_k)$, where $\lambda_1,\lambda_2,\dots,\lambda_k$ are the distinct eigenvalues of $T$. Show that $T$ is diagonalizable. [[5]]
  31. 12(b) Find the minimal polynomial $m(t)$ of the matrix $M=\begin{pmatrix}4&1&0&0&0\\0&4&1&0&0\\0&0&4&0&0\\0&0&0&4&1\\0&0&0&0&4\end{pmatrix}$. \fbox{\parbox{3.6cm}{\small Use separate answer script for each unit}} [[5]]
