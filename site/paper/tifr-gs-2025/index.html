TIFR Graduate School · GS 2025 · Mathematics

  1. 1 For a positive integer $m$, let $d(m)$ denote the number of divisors of $m$, including $1$ and $m$. Define a sequence $\{a_n\}_{n=1}^\infty$ by \[ a_n = \#\{1 \le m \le n \mid d(m) \text{ is odd}\}. \] Find $\lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{a_n}{n}$. \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)] \item $0$ \item $1$ \item $1/2$ \item None of the remaining three options \end{enumerate}
  2. 2 Let $S_3$ be the symmetric group on $3$ letters. Consider the real vector space \[ V = \{f: S_3 \to \mathbb{R} \mid f(g) = f(g^3), \, \forall g \in S_3\}. \] What is the dimension of this space? \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)] \item $1$ \item $2$ \item $3$ \item Greater than $3$ \end{enumerate}
  3. 3 Consider the set $S = \{(x, y) \in \mathbb{R}^2 \mid (x^3 + 1)^3 = (y^5 + 1)^5\}$. How many connected components does $S$ have? \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)] \item $1$ \item $3$ \item Greater than $3$, but finite \item Infinite \end{enumerate}
  4. 4 Define a set $S$ of real polynomials as follows: \[ S = \{p \in \mathbb{R}[x] \mid p \text{ is not constant, } |p(0)| < 1\}. \] For $p$ in $S$, define $U_p = \{x \in \mathbb{R} \mid |p(x)| < 1\}$. Which of the following statements is correct? \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)] \item There exists $p \in S$ such that $U_p$ is a union of infinitely many disjoint open intervals. \item For each $p \in S$, $U_p$ is a union of infinitely many disjoint open intervals. \item For each $p \in S$, $U_p$ is a union of finitely many open intervals. \item There exists $p \in S$ such that $U_p$ is an unbounded set. \end{enumerate}
  5. 5 What is the number of functions $f: \mathbb{R} \setminus \{0, 1\} \to \mathbb{R} \setminus \{0, 1\}$ such that $|f(x) - f(y)| = |x - y|$ for all $x, y \in \mathbb{R} \setminus \{0, 1\}$? \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)] \item $1$ \item $2$ \item Greater than $2$, but finite \item Infinite \end{enumerate}
  6. 6 Let $x_n = \left(1 - \frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\right)^n \exp(n^{1/4})$. Which of the following statements is correct? \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)] \item $\lim_{n \to \infty} x_n = 0$ and $\sum_{n=1}^\infty x_n$ converges. \item $\lim_{n \to \infty} x_n = 0$ but $\sum_{n=1}^\infty x_n$ diverges. \item $\lim_{n \to \infty} x_n$ exists but is non-zero. \item $\lim_{n \to \infty} x_n$ does not exist. \end{enumerate}
  7. 7 Let $f: [0,1] \to \mathbb{R}$ be such that \[ f(x) = \begin{cases} \frac{1}{2^n}, & x = \frac{p}{2^n} \text{ where } p \text{ is an odd integer, and} \\ 0, & \text{otherwise.} \end{cases} \] Which of the following statements is correct? \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)] \item $f$ is continuous at $x \in [0,1]$ if and only if $x$ is rational. \item $f$ is continuous at $x \in [0,1]$ if and only if $x$ is irrational. \item $f$ is not continuous at $x$, for any $x \in [0,1]$. \item None of the remaining three statements is true. \end{enumerate}
  8. 8 Let $\{q_n\}_{n=1}^\infty$ be an enumeration of the rationals. In other words, $\mathbb{Q} = \{q_n \mid n \in \mathbb{N} \setminus \{0\}\}$ and $q_m \ne q_n$ if $m \ne n$. Set \[ X = \bigcup_{n=1}^\infty \left(q_n - \frac{1}{n^2}, \, q_n + \frac{1}{n^2}\right). \] Which of the following statements is correct? \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)] \item $X$ is dense in $\mathbb{R}$, but not equal to $\mathbb{R}$. \item $X = \mathbb{R}$. \item $X$ contains an open interval of length $10$. \item None of the remaining three statements is correct. \end{enumerate}
  9. 9 Consider the linear map $T: \mathbb{R}[x] \to \mathbb{R}[x]$ defined by $T(p(x)) = \frac{d}{dx}(x p(x))$. Which of the following statements is correct? \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)] \item $T$ is injective but not surjective. \item $T$ is surjective but not injective. \item $T$ is bijective. \item $T$ is neither injective nor surjective. \end{enumerate}
  10. 10 Let \[ A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 & 1 \\ 2 & 1 & 2 \\ 1 & 2 & 1 \end{pmatrix}. \] Consider the following statements: \begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)] \item $A$ has a positive eigenvalue. \item $A$ has a negative eigenvalue. \end{enumerate} Which of the following statements is correct? \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)] \item (i) and (ii) are true. \item (i) and (ii) are false. \item (i) is true and (ii) is false. \item (ii) is true and (i) is false. \end{enumerate}
  11. 11 Let $A$ be a nonzero $n \times n$ matrix with real entries, where $n > 1$. Consider the following statements. \begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)] \item If $A^2 = 0$ then $\operatorname{rank}(A) \le \lfloor \frac{n}{2} \rfloor$. \item If $\operatorname{rank}(A) \le \lfloor \frac{n}{2} \rfloor$ then $A^2 = 0$. \end{enumerate} Which of the following statements is correct? \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)] \item (i) and (ii) are true. \item (i) and (ii) are false. \item (i) is true and (ii) is false. \item (ii) is true and (i) is false. \end{enumerate}
  12. 12 Consider the following subset of $\mathbb{R}^2$: \[ S = \left\{\left(x, \sin\frac{1}{x}\right) \;\middle|\; x > 0\right\} \cup \left\{\left(0, \pm\frac{1}{n}\right) \;\middle|\; n \ge 1\right\}. \] Which of the following statements is correct? \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)] \item $S$ is path connected. \item $S$ is connected, but not path connected. \item $S$ is not connected, but has finitely many connected components. \item $S$ has infinitely many connected components. \end{enumerate}
  13. 13 Consider the following statements: \begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)] \item Any linear map $\mathbb{R}^3 \to \mathbb{R}^3$ has a one-dimensional invariant subspace. \item Any linear map $\mathbb{R}^3 \to \mathbb{R}^3$ has a two-dimensional invariant subspace. \end{enumerate} Which of the following statements is correct? \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)] \item (i) and (ii) are both true. \item (i) and (ii) are both false. \item (i) is true and (ii) is false. \item (i) is false and (ii) is true. \end{enumerate}
  14. 14 Consider the following statements: \begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)] \item For any linear map $T: \mathbb{R}^3 \to \mathbb{R}^3$ with a two-dimensional kernel, the trace of $T$ is an eigenvalue of $T$. \item For any linear map $T: \mathbb{R}^3 \to \mathbb{R}^3$ with a two-dimensional kernel, $X^2$ divides the characteristic polynomial of $T$. \end{enumerate} Which of the following statements is correct? \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)] \item (i) and (ii) are both true. \item (i) and (ii) are both false. \item (i) is true and (ii) is false. \item (i) is false and (ii) is true. \end{enumerate}
  15. 15 For a positive integer $n$, define the function $f_n: \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ by $f_n(x) = \sin\left(\frac{x}{n}\right)$. Consider the sequences $\{f_n\}_{n=1}^\infty$ and $\{f_n'\}_{n=1}^\infty$ of functions from $\mathbb{R}$ to $\mathbb{R}$. Which of the following statements is correct? \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)] \item The sequences $\{f_n\}_{n=1}^\infty$ and $\{f_n'\}_{n=1}^\infty$ both converge uniformly on $\mathbb{R}$. \item Neither of the sequences $\{f_n\}_{n=1}^\infty$ and $\{f_n'\}_{n=1}^\infty$ converges uniformly on $\mathbb{R}$. \item The sequence $\{f_n\}_{n=1}^\infty$ converges uniformly on $\mathbb{R}$, but the sequence $\{f_n'\}_{n=1}^\infty$ does not. \item The sequence $\{f_n'\}_{n=1}^\infty$ converges uniformly on $\mathbb{R}$, but the sequence $\{f_n\}_{n=1}^\infty$ does not. \end{enumerate}
  16. 16 Let $n > 1$ be a positive integer. Let $A = [a_{ij}]_{1 \le i,j \le n}$ be an $n \times n$ matrix, such that $a_{i, i+1} = c_i$ for $i = 1, \dots, (n - 1)$ and $a_{n, 1} = c_n$ where $c_1, \dots, c_n$ are real numbers and all the other entries are $0$. Which of the following sentences is correct regardless of $n$? \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)] \item $\det(\operatorname{Id} - A^n) = 0$ if $\prod_{i=1}^n c_i = 1$. \item $\det(\operatorname{Id} - A^n) > 0$ if $\prod_{i=1}^n c_i > 0$. \item $\det(\operatorname{Id} - A^n) < 0$ if $\prod_{i=1}^n c_i > 1$. \item $\det(\operatorname{Id} - A^n) > 0$ if $\prod_{i=1}^n c_i > 1$. \end{enumerate}
  17. 17 Let $n \ge 1$. Consider non-zero $n \times n$ real matrices $A$ such that $\operatorname{trace}(AX) = 0$ for every real matrix $X$ with $\operatorname{trace}(X) = 0$. Consider the following assertions. \begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)] \item For every such matrix $A$, $\operatorname{trace}(A)^n = n^n \cdot \det(A)$. \item For every such matrix $A$, the rank of $A$ is $n$. \end{enumerate} Which of the following sentences is correct? \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)] \item (i) is correct and (ii) is incorrect. \item (ii) is correct and (i) is incorrect. \item (i) and (ii) are correct. \item (i) and (ii) are incorrect. \end{enumerate}
  18. 18 Let $S$ be the set of functions $f: (0,1) \to \mathbb{R}$ with the property that there is a sequence $\{f_n\}_{n=1}^\infty$ of functions that converges uniformly to $f$, where each $f_n: (0,1) \to \mathbb{R}$ is a twice continuously differentiable function (i.e., $f_n''$ exists and is continuous). Which of the following statements is correct? \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)] \item Any $f \in S$ is continuous, but there exists $f \in S$ such that $f$ is not differentiable. \item Any $f \in S$ is differentiable, but there exists $f \in S$ such that $f'$ is not continuous. \item Any $f \in S$ is continuously differentiable, but there exists $f \in S$ such that $f''$ does not exist. \item Any $f \in S$ is twice continuously differentiable. \end{enumerate}
  19. 19 For a real valued function $f: [0,1] \to \mathbb{R}$, let $\omega_f: [0,1] \to \mathbb{R}$ be the oscillation of $f$ defined by \[ \omega_f(t) := \sup_{|x - y| \le t} |f(x) - f(y)|. \] Which one of the following statements is correct for every $f: [0,1] \to \mathbb{R}$? \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)] \item $f$ is continuous if and only if $\lim_{t \to 0+} \omega_f(t) = 0$. \item If $f$ is continuous then $\lim_{t \to 0+} \omega_f(t) = 0$, but not conversely. \item If $\lim_{t \to 0+} \omega_f(t) = 0$ then $f$ is continuous, but not conversely. \item None of the remaining three statements is correct. \end{enumerate}
  20. 20 The number of real cubic polynomials of the form $x^3 + ax + b$, each of whose complex roots lies on the unit circle $S^1 = \{z \in \mathbb{C} \mid z \overline{z} = 1\}$, equals \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)] \item $0$ \item $2$ \item $\infty$ \item None of the remaining three options \end{enumerate}
  21. 1 There exists a linear polynomial $p(x) \in \mathbb{R}[x]$ for which there exist exactly two subsets $S \subset \mathbb{R}$ such that $p(S) = S$ and such that $\#S = 2$.
  22. 2 There are exactly $10$ abelian groups of order $2025$, up to isomorphism.
  23. 3 If $A \subset \mathbb{R}$ is an uncountable subset, then there exist uncountably many elements $x \in A$ such that $x$ is a limit point of $A \setminus \{x\}$.
  24. 4 There exists an open subset of $\mathbb{R}$ with uncountably many connected components.
  25. 5 Let $(X, d)$ be a nonempty complete metric space, and let $T: X \to X$ be a continuous map such that $T^2$ is a contraction, i.e., there exists $a < 1$ such that for all distinct $x, y \in X$, we have $d(T^2(x), T^2(y)) < a d(x, y)$. Then there exists a unique element $x \in X$ such that $T(x) = x$.
  26. 6 Let $S^2 = \{(x, y, z) \in \mathbb{R}^3 \mid x^2 + y^2 + z^2 = 1\}$, and let $d: S^2 \times S^2 \to \mathbb{R}$ be the restriction of the Euclidean metric on $\mathbb{R}^3$. If $f: S^2 \to S^2$ is a map such that $d(f(x), f(y)) = d(x, y)$ for all $x, y \in S^2$, then $f$ is surjective.
  27. 7 Let $C([0,1], \mathbb{R})$ be the set of continuous functions from $[0,1]$ to $\mathbb{R}$, equipped with the metric $d$ given by $d(f_1, f_2) = \sup_{x \in [0,1]} |f_1(x) - f_2(x)|$. Let $X \subset C([0,1], \mathbb{R})$ be defined by \[ X := \left\{f \in C([0,1], \mathbb{R}) \;\middle|\; \int_0^1 f(t)\,dt \ne 0\right\}. \] Then, with the induced metric, $X$ is connected.
  28. 8 Let $SO_4 = \{A \in M_4(\mathbb{R}) \mid AA^t = \operatorname{Id} \text{ and } \det(A) > 0\}$. Then every matrix in $SO_4$ has $1$ or $-1$ as an eigenvalue.
  29. 9 Let $T$ be an endomorphism of a finite dimensional real vector space $V$, and let $W$ denote the image of $T$. Let $T'$ denote the restriction of $T$ to $W$. Then $T$ and $T'$ have the same trace.
  30. 10 Every subgroup of $S^1 = \{z \in \mathbb{C} \mid z \overline{z} = 1\}$, viewed as a group under multiplication, is finite.
  31. 11 If $f: F \to F$ is a homomorphism of fields, then $f$ is surjective.
  32. 12 Given any linear transformation $T: \mathbb{R}^2 \to \mathbb{R}^2$, there exist linear transformations $T_1, T_2: \mathbb{R}^2 \to \mathbb{R}^2$ such that $(1, 0)$ belongs to the kernel of $T_1$, $(0, 1)$ belongs to the image of $T_2$, and $T = T_1 + T_2$.
  33. 13 For any positive integer $d$, the set $\{10^a - 10^b \mid a, b \text{ are distinct positive integers}\}$ contains a multiple of $d$.
  34. 14 There exists $\theta \in (0, \pi/2)$ such that $\sin\theta + \sec\theta + \cot\theta = 3$.
  35. 15 Let $m$ be a positive integer, and $S_m$ the symmetric group on $m$ letters. Let $n$ be a positive integer such that for every group $G$ of order $n$, there exists an injective group homomorphism $G \hookrightarrow S_m$. Then $m \ge n$.
  36. 16 Let $l_1$ be the line in $\mathbb{R}^2$ joining $(0,0)$ and $\left(\frac{1}{2}, \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right)$ and $l_2$ the line in $\mathbb{R}^2$ joining $(0,0)$ and $\left(\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}, \frac{1}{2}\right)$. Consider the group of bijections $\mathbb{R}^2 \to \mathbb{R}^2$ under composition, and its subgroup $G$ generated by the reflections about $l_1$ and $l_2$. Then $G$ has exactly $12$ elements.
  37. 17 There are only finitely many subsets $I \subset \mathbb{N}$ with the property that \[ \left\{f(x) = \sum_{i=0}^n a_i x^i \in \mathbb{R}[x] \;\middle|\; n \in \mathbb{N}, \, a_i \in \mathbb{Q} \text{ for all } i \in I\right\} \] is a subring of $\mathbb{R}[x]$.
  38. 18 There are uncountably many continuous functions $\mathbb{Q} \to \{0, 1\}$.
  39. 19 Let $f: \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ be a continuously differentiable function. Suppose there exists $C \in \mathbb{R}$ such that $f$ is injective on $(C, \infty)$. Then there exists $D \in \mathbb{R}$ such that $f'$ is injective on $(D, \infty)$.
  40. 20 If $f, g: [0,1] \to \mathbb{R}$ are continuous and $f \cdot g = 0$, then one of $f$ or $g$ is zero on an open subset of $[0,1]$.
