Paper

B.Sc. Mathematics · Semester V2019
  1. 1(a) Define moments and products of inertia of a rigid body. If $(A,B,C)$ are the moments and $(D,E,F)$ are the products of inertia of a rigid body with respect to the rectangular axes $OX,OY$ and $OZ$, then prove that the moment of inertia about a line through $O$ having direction cosines $\ell,m,n$ is given by $A\ell^2+Bm^2+Cn^2-2Dmn-2En\ell-2F\ell m.$ [1+1+4]
  2. 1(b) A heavy uniform rigid straight rod $OA$ of length $2a$ is free to turn about its end $O$. If the rod revolves uniformly with angular velocity $\omega$ about the vertical through $O$ making a constant angle $\pi/4$ with the vertical, then show that $\omega^2=\frac{3g}{2\sqrt2a}$. [4]
  3. 2(a) Show that the momental ellipsoid at the centre of an ellipsoid is $(b^2+c^2)x^2+(c^2+a^2)y^2+(a^2+b^2)z^2=\text{constant}.$ [5]
  4. 2(b) The lengths $AB$ and $AD$ of the sides of a rectangle $ABCD$ are $2a$ and $2b$. Show that the inclination to $AB$ of one of the principal axes at $A$ is $\frac12\tan^{-1}\frac{3ab}{2(a^2-b^2)}$. [5]
  5. 3(a) A uniform rod of mass $m$ and length $2a$ can turn freely about a fixed axis. Show that the least angular velocity with which it must be started from the lowest position so that it may just make complete revolution is $\sqrt{\frac{3g}a}$, and with this angular velocity the time to describe an angle $\theta$ is $\sqrt{\frac{4a}{3g}}\log\tan\left(\frac\pi4+\frac\theta4\right)$. [5]
  6. 3(b) A uniform rod of weight $W$, free to turn about a smooth pivot at one end is held horizontally and released. Prove that when in the subsequent motion, the rod makes an angle $\theta$ with the vertical, the pressure in the pivot is $\frac W4\sqrt{1+99\cos^2\theta}$. [5]
  7. 4(a) Define compound pendulum. Hence define its centre of suspension and centre of oscillation. Show that in a compound pendulum, the centre of suspension and centre of oscillation are interchangeable. [1+1+1+3]
  8. 4(b) Find the minimum time of oscillation of a given compound pendulum. [4]
  9. 5(a) An elliptic lamina can rotate about a horizontal axis passing through a focus and perpendicular to its plane. If the eccentricity of the ellipse be $\sqrt{\frac25}$, show that the centre of oscillation will be at the other focus. [5]
  10. 5(b) A solid homogeneous cone of height $h$ and semi-vertical angle $\alpha$ oscillates about a horizontal axis through its vertex. Show that the length of the simple equivalent pendulum is $\frac h5(4+\tan^2\alpha)$. [5]
  11. 6(a) A uniform rod is held at an inclination of $45^o$ to the vertical with one end in contact with a horizontal table where the coefficient of friction is $\mu$ It is then released. Show that it will commence to slide if $\mu<\frac35$. [5]
  12. 6(b) A uniform rod of length $2a$ is placed with one end in contact with a smooth horizontal table and then allowed to fall. If $\alpha$ be its initial inclination to the vertical, show that its angular velocity when it is inclined at an angle $\theta$ is $\left[\frac{6g}a\frac{\cos\alpha-\cos\theta}{1+3\sin^2\theta}\right]^{1/2}$. Find also the reaction of the table. [5]
  13. 1(a) Define pressure at a point in a fluid in equilibrium. Show that the pressure at a point in a fluid in equilibrium is the same in every direction. [1+4]
  14. 1(b) A closed tube in the form of an equilateral triangle contains equal volumes of three liquids which do not mix and is placed with its lowest side horizontal. Prove that if the densities of the liquids are in arithmetic progression (AP), their surfaces of separation will be at the points of trisection of the sides of the triangle. [5]
  15. 2(a) Define the pressure gradient force at a point in a fluid of uniform density $\rho$. Hence define the surface of equi-pressure. [2+1]
  16. 2(b) Show that for a fluid in equilibrium under the external force $\vec F=(X,Y,Z)$, the surfaces of equidensity, equi-pressure and equi-potential energy coincide. Find also the differential equations for the equi-pressure and equi-density surfaces. [4+3]
  17. 3(a) Define centre of pressure of a given area in a fluid in equilibrium. Find the centre of pressure of a plane area. Hence show that it approaches the horizontal through the centre of gravity with a velocity which varies directly as the velocity and inversely as the square of the depth of the centre of gravity. [1+4]
  18. 3(b) Show that the depth of the centre of pressure of a rhombus totally immersed with one diagonal vertical and its centre at a depth $h$ is $\left(\frac1{24}a^2+h^2\right)\Big/h$, where `$a$' is the length of the vertical diagonal. \fbox{\parbox{3.4cm}{\small\textbf{Use Separate Answer scripts for each Unit}}} [5]
