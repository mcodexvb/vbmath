MMO-31(A9)

Lie Group of Transformations Partial Differential Equations
M.Sc. Mathematics · Semester III2017
  1. 1 Show that $2^{\text{nd}}$ order linear ordinary differential equation (ODE) $y''+p(x)y'+q(x)y=r(x)$ ($p(x),q(x),r(x)$ are smooth functions) admits $x^*=x,\ y^*=y+\epsilon_1\varphi_1(x)+\epsilon_2\varphi_2(x)$ as its symmetry group of transformations provided $\varphi_i(x)\ (i=1,2)$ are solutions of some differential equation to be determined by you. Hence, recover the solution of given ODE obtained by using method of variation of parameters (Green's function method). \begin{center}\textbf{\textit{Or}}\end{center} Obtain infinitesimal generators (IGs) for Lie group of symmetry transformations admitted by the ODE $y''+y'^2=0$.
  2. 2 Define Lie group of transformations (LGTs). Verify whether the transformations $x'=x\cosh\epsilon+y\sinh\epsilon+a_1,\ y'=x\sinh\epsilon+y\cosh\epsilon+a_2$ is a LGTs. Find IGs for this LGTs, if yes. [1+4+4]
  3. 3 Prove that one parameter Lie group of trnasformations (OPLGTs) $\tilde x'=\hat X(\tilde x;\epsilon)$ generated by the IG $\hat X$ is equivalent to $\tilde x'=e^{\epsilon\hat X}\tilde x$. Hence, find the LGTs generated by $\hat x=y\partial_x+x\partial_y$. [5+4]
  4. 4(a) Find LGTs (in the extended space) for $y^{\prime\prime\prime}$ corresponding to LGTs $x'=x\cos\epsilon+y\sin\epsilon,\ y'=-x\sin\epsilon+y\cos\epsilon$. [3]
  5. 4(b) Define Lie algebra. When is it said to be solvable? Prove that every two dimensional Lie algebra is solvable. Verify whether Lie algebra of IGs for LGTs $x'=ax+b,\ y'=cy$ is solvable? [1+1+2+2]
  6. 5 Define invariant function and invariant family of functions admitted by a LGTs. State and prove necessary and sufficient conditions for a function to be an invariant or be a member in the invariant family.\par Find invariant family of functions for the LGTs\par \hspace*{2em}$x'=x\cos\epsilon-y\sin\epsilon,\ y'=x\sin\epsilon+y\cos\epsilon$.
  7. 6 Verify whether $y''+\dfrac1{y^2}y'-\dfrac1{xy}=0$ admits LGTs generated by $\hat x_1=x^2\partial_x+\dfrac12y\partial_y,\ \hat x_2=x^2\partial_x+xy\partial_y$, as its symmetry group. If yes, solve it by the method of canonical variables.
