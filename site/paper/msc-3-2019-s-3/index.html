MMC-33

Fluid Mechanics
M.Sc. Mathematics · Semester III2019
  1. 1(a) Derive the equation of continuity by both the Eulerian and Lagrangian methods. [2+2]
  2. 1(b) Show that if $\varphi=xf(r)$ be a possible form of the velocity potential of an incompressible liquid motion then find the velocity vector. Given that the liquid speed $q\to0$ as $r\to\infty$, deduce that the surfaces of constant speed are $\left(r^2+3x^2\right)r^{-8}=$constant. [4]
  3. 1(c) Define stream line and path line. Find their differential equations. [2]
  4. 2(a) Derive Euler's equation of motion and hence deduce Bernoulli's equation in general form. [2+2]
  5. 2(b) The velocity components, in cylindrical polar coordinates,\par \[u_r(r,\theta)=-U\left(1-\frac{a^2}{r^2}\right)\cos\theta,\ u_\theta(r,\theta)=U\left(1+\frac{a^2}{r^2}\right)\sin\theta\] satisfy the equations of motion for a two-dimensional inviscid incompressible flow. Find the pressure associated with this velocity field. $U$ and $a$ are constants. [6]
  6. 3(a) State and prove the theorem of Blasius. [1+4]
  7. 3(b) Within a circular boundary of radius `$a$' there is a two-dimensional liquid motion due to source producing liquid at the rate $m$, at a distance $f$ from the centre, and an equal sink at the centre. Find the velocity potential and show that the resultant pressure on the boundary is $\rho m^2f^3\big/\left[2a^2\left(a^2-f^2\right)\pi\right]$ where $\rho$ is the density. Deduce, as a limit, velocity potential due to a doublet at the centre. [5]
  8. 4(a) State and prove Kelvin's circulation theorem. Hence deduce permanence of irrotational motion. [1+2+1]
  9. 4(b) Prove that the mean value of $\varphi$ over any spherical surface, throughout whose interior $\nabla^2\varphi=0$, is equal to the value of $\varphi$ at the centre of the sphere. [3]
  10. 4(c) If $\Sigma$ be the boundary of a large spherical surface of radius $R$, containing fluid in motion and also enclosing one or more closed surfaces, then the mean value of $\varphi$ on $\Sigma$ is of the form $\bar\varphi=M/R+C$ where $M,C$ are constants, provided that the fluid extends to infinity and is at rest there. \par\hfill P.T.O.\ \unreadable{page 2 of this paper is not in the PDF} [3]
