MMC-41

New Syllabus
M.Sc. Mathematics · Semester IV2019
  1. 1 Define normal section of a smooth surface $M$ at a point $P\big((x^1(u,v),x^2(u,v),x^3(u,v))\big)$ in the direction $\vec w$. Hence find the normal section to the surface $M=\{(x^1,x^2,x^3):(x^1)^2+(x^2)^2=1,\ -2\le x^3\le2\}$ at the point $(0,1,0)$ in the direction $\vec w_1=(0,0,1)$, $\vec w_2=(0,1,0)$ and $\vec w_3=\left(\frac1{\sqrt2},0,\frac1{\sqrt2}\right)$. Find curvature and torsion to these curves. \par\centerline{\textbf{OR}} Define manifold $\mathcal M$ of dimension $m$ and a affine linear connection on $\mathcal M$. Define covariant derivative of a vector field in terms of connection. Derive expressions for $\nabla_Xf$, $\nabla_XY$ and $\nabla_X\omega$ for $f:\mathcal M\to\mathbb R$, $X=X^i(\tilde x)\frac{\partial}{\partial x^i}$, $Y=Y^i(\tilde x)\frac{\partial}{\partial x^i}$, $\omega=\omega_i(\tilde x)dx^i$. [10]
  2. 2 Define the first fundamental form of a smooth surface $M=\{(x^1(u,v),x^2(u,v),x^3(u,v)):(u,v)\in\Omega\subset\mathbb R^2\}$. Give geometrical interpretation of coefficients involved in the first fundamental form. Find the first fundamental form for the surface $M=\{\sin u\cos v,\sin u\sin v,\cos u\}$ $(0\le u\le\pi,\ 0\le v\le2\pi)$. \par\centerline{\textbf{OR}} Define parallel displacement of a vector on a curved space. If $K_\nu$ is the parallel displacement of $A_\nu$ at $P(\tilde x)$ to a neighbouring point $Q(\tilde x+d\tilde x)$, prove that $dA_\nu=K_\nu-A_\nu=A^\mu Y^n_\mu Y_{n,\nu,\sigma}\,dx^\sigma=A^\mu\Gamma_{\mu\nu\sigma}dx^\sigma$. Where $\Gamma_{\mu\nu\sigma}$ is Christoffel symbol of first kind. Derive the relation between Christoffel symbol of first, second kind and the metric tensor $g_{\mu\nu}$ of the curved space. [1+5+2+2]
  3. 3 Explain the symbols $g_{pq}$ and $\Gamma^p_{qr}$ involved in the derivatives of position vector of a point on a curve on the smooth surface with respect to the parameter involved. Define the geodesic of a surface. Use this definition to derive the equation for geodesic on a surface. Hence show that geodesics on a sphere are great circles on it. \par\centerline{\textbf{OR}} Define Lie group and Lie group of transformations. Verify whether $S=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}1&a\\0&1\end{pmatrix},a\in\mathbb R\right\}$ and $T_{(a,b)}:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R$ given by $x^*=T_{(a,b)}x=ax+b$ are Lie group and Lie group of transformations. \par\textbf{P.T.O.} [2+6+2]
  4. 4 Find $g_{pq}$ and $\Gamma^p_{qr}$ for the surfaces $M_1=\{(x^1,x^2,x^3):(x^1)^2+(x^3)^2=1,\ -\infty\le x^2\le\infty\}$ and $M_2=\{x_1^2+x_2^2+x_3^2=1,\ x_1,x_2,x_3\in\mathbb R\}$. \par\centerline{\textbf{OR}} What do you mean by a vector field $Y=Y^i(\tilde x)\frac{\partial}{\partial x^i}$ on a manifold $\mathcal M$ is parallel along a Curve $C$ on $\mathcal M$? Define geodesic on a manifold and derive its equation. Find geodesic on a 2-sphere. [2+3+5]
  5. 5 Define second fundamental form of a surface. Compute the second fundamental form of the surface of revolution $\vec X(u,v)=(f(u)\cos v,f(u)\sin v,g(u))$. \par\centerline{\textbf{OR}} [2+8]
  6. 5(a) Define $k$-form on a manifold $\mathcal M$ of dimension $m$. If $\mathcal M$ be a three dimensional manifold with a local coordinate $(x,y,z)$ around a point $P\in\mathcal M$, find the basis of $\Lambda^{(k)}$, $k=1,2,3,4$. Define wedge product and state fundamental properties of this operation. If $\omega\in\Lambda^{(m-1)}$ and $\bar\omega\in\Lambda^{(2)}$ are two forms on $\mathcal M$, find $\omega\wedge\bar\omega$. [1+2+2]
  7. 5(b) Define exterior derivative of a $k$-form on a manifold $\mathcal M$ of dimension $m\,(m\ge k)$. State Poincare lemma and prove it for 0-form $f:\mathcal M\to\mathbb R$. State its correspondence with operations gradient, divergence and curl, if any. [1+2+2]
  8. 6 State Gauss curvature involving first and second fundamental forms. Find the parameter dependence of Gauss curvature at a point $(u,v)$ on the surface $(u,v,f(u,v))$. \par\centerline{\textbf{OR}} What do you mean by commutator? State few important properties of this operation. Define tangent bundle and a distribution on a manifold. When a distribution is said to be integrable and in involution. Verify which of the following distributions $\mathcal D_1=\{(1,0,y),(0,1,-x)\}$ and $\mathcal D_2=\{(1,0,x),(0,1,y)\}$ are integrable? [1+2+2+2+3]
