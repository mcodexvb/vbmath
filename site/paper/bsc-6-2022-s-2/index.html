CCMA-14

Algebra IV
B.Sc. Mathematics · Semester VI2022
  1. 1(a) Suppose that $f:\mathbb Z_{50}\to\mathbb Z_{50}$ is an automorphism with $f(11)=13$. Determine a formula for $f(x)$. [3]
  2. 1(b) Let $f$ be a homomorphism of a group $G$ into a group $G^1$. Show that $\ker f$ is a normal subgroup of $G$. [2]
  3. 2(a) Show that any infinite cyclic group is isomorphic to $\mathbb Z$. [3]
  4. 2(b) Show that $(\mathbb Q,+)$ is not isomorphic to $(\mathbb Q^+,\cdot)$. [2]
  5. 3(a) Show that there is only one noncommutative group of order 6 up to isomorphism. [3]
  6. 3(b) How many subgroups are there in the group $\mathbb Z/21\mathbb Z$? [2]
  7. 4(a) Suppose that $\mathbb Z_{10}$ and $\mathbb Z_{15}$ are homomorphic images of a finite group $G$. What can be said about $|G|$? [2]
  8. 4(b) Let $H$ and $K$ be two subgroups of a group $G$, with $H$ normal in $G$. Then show that $K/(H\cap K)$ is isomorphic to $HK/H$. [3]
  9. 5(a) Let $\phi$ be an automorphism on a group $G$. Show that $H=\{a\in G:\phi(a)=a\}$ is a subgroup of $G$. [3]
  10. 5(b) Show that $|Inn(S_3)|=6$. [2]
  11. 6(a) Let $G$ be a group such that $Z(G)=\{e\}$. Show that $Z(Aut(G))=\{e\}$. [4]
  12. 6(b) State Cauchy's theorem on finite groups. [1]
  13. 1(a) State and prove the First Isomorphism Theorem for rings. [3]
  14. 1(b) Let $R$ be a commutative ring of characteristic 2. Then show that the mapping $a\to a^2$ is a ring homomorphism from $R$ to $R$. [2]
  15. 2(a) Show that the ring $2\mathbb Z$ is not isomorphic to the ring $3\mathbb Z$. [3]
  16. 2(b) Give an example of a simple ring which is not a field. Justify your answer. [2]
  17. 3(a) Show that every ideal of $\mathbb Z$ are of the form $m\mathbb Z$ for $m\in\mathbb Z$. [4]
  18. 3(b) Show that there does not exist any proper ideal $I$ in a a ring $R$ that contains a unit. [1]
  19. 4 Find the number of elements in the quotient ring $\mathbb Z[i]/{<3+i>}$. [5]
  20. 5(a) Let $R$ be a commutative ring with unity 1. If $R$ has a prime ideal $P$ such that $P$ contains no zero divisors, then show that $R$ is an integral domain. [3]
  21. 5(b) In the ring $\mathbb Z\times\mathbb Z$, give an example of a prime ideal which is not maximal. Justify your answer. [2]
  22. 6 Let $R$ be a commutative ring with unity 1. Then show that every proper ideal of $R$ is contained in a maximal ideal of $R$. [5]
  23. 1 Let $A$ be a $m\times n$ matrix over $\mathbb R$, Show that row rank of $A$ is equal to the column rank of $A$. [5]
  24. 2(a) Let $A\in M_{m\times n}(\mathbb R)$ and $B\in M_{n\times p}(\mathbb R)$. If rank of $AB=$ rank of $B$, then show that there exists $D\in M_{n\times m}(\mathbb R)$ such that $DAB=B$. [3]
  25. 2(b) Let $A\in M_{5\times3}(\mathbb R)$ and $B\in M_{3\times5}(\mathbb R)$. If rank of $A=$ rank of $B=3$, find rank of $AB$. [2]
  26. 3(a) Let $B\in M_n(R)$. Define $T:M_n(R)\to M_n(R)$ by $T(A)=AB-BA$. Show that $T$ is a linear transformation and find its kernal. [2+1]
  27. 3(b) Give an example of a linear transformation which is not one-one but onto. Justify your answer. [2]
  28. [Question number absent](a) Let $V$ be a vector space over the field $F$ and $\beta$ an $\gamma$ two finite bases of $V$. For a linear transformation $T:V\longrightarrow V$ show that $[T]_\beta$ and $[T]_\gamma$ are similar. [2]
  29. [Question number absent](b) Suppose $U,V$ and $W$ be three finite dimensional vector spaces over the field $F$. Let $T:U\longrightarrow V$ and $S:V\longrightarrow W$ be two linear transformations. Then show that $\mathit{rank}(ST)=\mathit{rank}(T)-\dim(Im\,T\cap\ker S)$. [3]
  30. 5(a) Let $V$ be a vector space over the field $F$ and $T:V\to V$ be a linear operator such that $T^2=cT$ for some $c\in F$. Prove that $V=\operatorname{Im}T\oplus\ker T$. [3]
  31. 5(b) Find a basis for $L(P_2(\mathbb R),\mathbb R^2)$. [2]
  32. 6(a) Let $T:P_2(\mathbb R)\to M_2(\mathbb R)$ be the linear transformation defined by, \[T(p(t))=\begin{pmatrix}f''(0)&3f(2)\\0&f'(1)\end{pmatrix}.\] Find the matrix representation of $T$ relative to the standard bases of the given vector spaces. [4]
  33. 6(b) Prove that the linear operator $T:\mathbb R^2\to\mathbb R^2$ defined by $T(x,y)=(2x,0)$ is not a projection oparetor. [1]
