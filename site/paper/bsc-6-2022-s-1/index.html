CCMA-13

Analysis VI
B.Sc. Mathematics · Semester VI2022
  1. 1(a) Show that a complex sequence $\{z_n\}$ converges to $z_0$ if and only if $\{Re(z_n)\}$ converges to $Re(z_0)$ and $\{Im(z_n)\}$ converges to $Im(z_0)$.
  2. 1(b) When is a complex series $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}z_n$ called conditionally convergent? Discuss the convergence of the series \[\sum_{n=1}^\infty n^2 2^n(1+i)^{-n}.\] [6+4]
  3. 2(a) Find the points in $\mathbb C$ at which the function $f$ defined by $f(z)=\begin{cases}\frac{z^2}{z}&\text{if }z\ne 0\\0&\text{if }z=0\end{cases}$ is differentiable.
  4. 2(b) Compute an analytic function $f(z)=u+iv$ such that $u-v=e^x(\cos y-\sin y)$. [5+5]
  5. 3(a) Write down Cauchy-Riemann equations in polar form and derive them.
  6. 3(b) Obtain a condition on $k$ such that the Möbius map $f(z)=\frac{z-3}{1-2z}$ maps the circle $|z-1|=k$ onto a straight line. Also find the equation of the straight line.
  7. 4(a) Find all Möbius transformations of the half-plane $Re(z)\le0$ onto the unit disc $|w|\le1$.
  8. 4(b) Evaluate $\displaystyle\oint_C\frac{e^{iz}}{z-\pi i}\,dz$, where $C$ is the (positively oriented) curve $|z-2|+|z+2|=6$. [6+4]
  9. 5(a) Let $f$ and $g$ be entire functions such that $|f(z)|\le|g(z)|$ and $g(z)\ne0\ \forall z\in\mathbb C$. Show that there is a constant $c$ such that $f(z)=cg(z)$.
  10. 5(b) Let $P(z)$ be a polynomial of degree $n$ and $a\in\mathbb C$. Then show that there are exactly $n$ points in $\mathbb C$ which are mapped by $P(z)$ to $a$. [5+5]
  11. 6(a) Define a metric space. Prove that for any three points $\alpha,\beta,\gamma$ in a metric space $(X,\rho)$, $|\rho(\alpha,\beta)-\rho(\beta,\gamma)|\le\rho(\gamma,\alpha)$. [2+2]
  12. 6(b) Let $\rho:R^2\times R^2\to R$ be defined by $\rho(x,y)=|x_1-y_1|$, where $x=(x_1,x_2),y=(y_1,y_2)\in R^2$. Examine if $\rho$ is a metric on $R^2$. [2]
  13. 6(c) Prove that $l_3$ is a metric space. [4]
  14. 7(a) Define an open sphere in a metric space $(X,\rho)$ and prove that it is an open set. [1+2]
  15. 7(b) Show that finite intersection of open sets in a metric space $(X,\rho)$ is open. [3]
  16. 7(c) Prove that a sequence $\{x_n\}$ converges to $x$ in the metric space $c[a,b]$ if and only if the corresponding sequence of real valued functions $\{x_n(t)\}$ converges uniformly to $x(t)$ on $[a,b]$. [4]
  17. 8(a) Define the closure $\bar A$ of a set $A\subset X$ in a metric space $(X,\rho)$. The closure of a set $A$, consisting of a single point is the set $A$ itself - justify it. [1+2]
  18. 8(b) Define a Cauchy sequence in a metric space $(X,\rho)$. Prove that if a sequence $\{x_n\}$ converges, then it is a Cauchy sequence. Show by an example the converse is not always true. [1+2+1]
  19. 8(c) Define a closed set in a metric space. Examine if arbitrary union of closed sets in a metric space is closed. [1+2]
  20. 9(a) Define a complete metric space. Prove that the space $m$, of all bounded sequences of real numbers with a suitable metric defined by you is complete. [1+4]
  21. 9(b) Let $X$ be the set of all polynomials $P(t)$, $0\le t\le1$ with real coefficients and the distance between $P(t),Q(t)$ in $X$ be defined by $\rho(P,Q)=Sup\{|P(t)-Q(t)|:0\le t\le1\}$. Show that $(X,\rho)$ is not a complete metric space. [3]
  22. 9(c) Let $X$ be a complete metric space and let $Y$ be a subset of $X$. Then prove that $Y$, considered as a metric subspace is complete if it is closed. [2]
  23. 10(a) Define a contraction mapping over a metric space. \par Let $X=(0,\frac14)$ be a metric space with the usual metric of R. Let $T:X\to X$ be given by $Tx=x^2$. Show that $T$ is a contraction maping having no fixed point in $X$. [1+3]
  24. 10(b) Let $(X,\rho)$ and $(Y,\rho_1)$ be two metric spaces and $f:X\to Y$ be a function. Then prove that $f(x)$ is continuous at $x\in X$ if and only if for every sequence $\{x_n\}$, $x_n\in X$ converging to $x\in X$ implies $f(x_n)\to f(x)$ as $n\to\infty$. [6]
