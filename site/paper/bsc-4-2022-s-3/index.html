CCMA-10

B.Sc. Mathematics · Semester IV2022
  1. 1 Explain the concept of statistical regularity. State clearly the frequency definition of probability of an event $A$ connected with a random experiment $E$. Explain the real strength of this definition. Hence define the conditional probability of the event $B$ on the hypothesis that event $A$ has already occurred. [4+2+2+2]
  2. 2(a) If $\{A_n\}$ is a monotonic sequence of events, then show that \[P(\lim A_n)=\lim P(A_n).\] [4]
  3. 2(b) A card is drawn at random from each of two well-shuffled packs of cards. What is the probability that at least one of them is a queen of spades? [3]
  4. 2(c) What is the probability that a bridge hand contains four aces? [3]
  5. 3(a) State Bayes' theorem. There are three urns containing respectively 1 white and 2 black balls, 2 white and 1 black balls, 2 white and 2 black balls. One ball is transferred from the first to the second urn; then one ball is transferred from the second to the third urn; finally, one ball is drawn from the third urn. Find the probability that the ball drawn is white. [2+4]
  6. 3(b) Consider three events $A,B,C$. If $A$ and $B$ are independent and $B$ and $C$ are independent, then does it follow that events $A$ and $C$ are independent? [4]
  7. 4(a) Define Poisson sequence of trials. Three marksmen can hit a target with probabilities $1/2,2/3,3/4$ respectively. They shoot simultaneously and two hits were registered. Compute the probability that each of the three marksmen misses the target. [2+3]
  8. 4(b) Define Markov chain. In a Markov chain of coin tossings, the first toss is given to be fair and the transition probabilities of $H\to H$, $H\to T$, $T\to H$, $T\to T$ are respectively $1/2,1/2,0,1$. Find the probability of head(H) in the third toss and tail(T) in the fourth toss. [1+4]
  9. 5(a) Define a random variable $X$. Define distribution function $F$ of a random variable $X$. For any fixed point $a$, compute $F(a-0)$ and $F(a+0)$. [1+2+1+1]
  10. 5(b) If $X$ is a normal $(m,\sigma)$ variate, then show that \[P(|X-m|>a\sigma)=2[1-\Phi(a)]\] where $\Phi(x)$ denotes the standard normal distribution function and $a$ is a fixed point. [5]
  11. 6(a) Show that the function $|x|$ in $(-1,1)$ is a possible probability density function and hence find the distribution function. [4]
  12. 6(b) If $X$ is uniformly distributed over the interval $(-2,2)$ then find the distribution of the random variable $\min\{X,1\}$. [3]
  13. 6(c) If $X$ is normal $(0,1)$ then find the distribution of $e^X$. [3]
  14. 7(a) Define $k$-th central moment $\mu_k$ of a random variable $X$. Compute the same for normal $(m,\sigma)$ variate. [1+2]
  15. 7(b) The probability density of a continuous distribution is given by \[f(x)=\frac34x(2-x);\quad 0<x<2.\] Compute the mean and variance. [2+2]
  16. 7(c) Define mode of a distribution. Find the mode of a Binomial $(4,0.25)$ variate. [1+2]
  17. 8(a) Show that if $X$ is a $\gamma(\frac12n)$ variate then $2X$ has a chi-square distribution with $n$ degrees of freedom. Compute the mean, variance and mode of $\chi^2(n)$ variate. [2+3]
  18. 8(b) For the $t$-distribution with $n$ degrees of freedom, prove that the variance exists only for $n>2$ and its value is $n/(n-2)$. [5]
  19. 9(a) Define joint distribution of a pair of random variables. Define their marginal distributions. Prove that if $X$ and $Y$ are independent then \[P(X=b,Y=d)=P(X=b)P(Y=d).\] [2+2+3]
  20. 9(b) $X$ and $Y$ are independent variates, each uniformly distributed over the interval $(0,1)$. Find the probability that the greater of $X,Y$ is less than a fixed number $k(0<k<1)$. [3]
  21. 10(a) Define convergence in probability. If $X_n$ is a Binomial$(n,p)$ variate, then show that \[\frac{X_n}{n}\to p\text{ in probability as }n\to\infty.\] [1+4]
  22. 10(b) If $X_1,X_2,\cdots X_n,\cdots$ be a sequence of independent and identically distributed random variables each with mean $m$ and variance $\sigma^2<\infty$ for $i=1,2,\cdots$ then show that \[\lim_{n\to\infty}P(|\bar S_n-m|\ge\epsilon)=0\] for every $\epsilon>0$. \renewcommand{\paperfull}{25} \renewcommand{\paperplural}{s} [5]
