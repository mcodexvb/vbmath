MMC-13

Algebra-I
M.Sc. Mathematics · Semester I2016
  1. 1(a) Prove that $\mathbb{Z}_m\times\mathbb{Z}_n$ is a cyclic group if and only if $\gcd(m,n)=1$. [4]
  2. 1(b) Define internal direct product of normal subgroups of a group. Show that $(\mathbb{Q},+)$ can not be expressed as an internal direct product of subgroups. [3]
  3. 1(c) State and prove the conjugacy class equation of a finite group. [3]
  4. 2(a) State and prove the Cauchy's theorem for a finite non-abelian group. [4]
  5. 2(b) Let $G$ be a non cyclic group of order 21. Find the number of elements of order 3 in $G$. [3]
  6. 2(c) Let $G$ be a non-trivial $p$-group and $S$ be a finite $G$-set. Denote $S_0=\{s\in S\mid gs=s \text{ for all } g\in G\}$. If $p\nmid|S|$, then show that $S_0\neq\varphi$. [3]
  7. 3(a) Show that every group of order 35 is a cyclic group. [3]
  8. 3(b) Let $G$ be a finite group of order $p^nm$, where $m,n\in\mathbb{N}$, $p$ is a prime and $p\nmid m$. Then show that a subgroup $H$ is a Sylow $p$-subgroup of $G$ if and only if $|H|=p^n$. [4]
  9. 3(c) Let $n\geq5$. If $H$ is a subgroup of $S_n$ that contains all 3-cycles, then show that $H'$ contains all 3-cycles of $S_{n}$. Hence or otherwise show that $S_5$ is not solvable. [3]
  10. 4(a) Show that a linear transformation $T:U\to V$ is onto if and only it it maps a generating subset of $U$ onto a generating subset of $V$. [3]
  11. 4(b) Let $T:V\to V$ be a linear transformation. If $B=\{e_1,e_2,\ldots,e_n\}$ is a basis of $V$, then show that $[T(v)]_B=[T]_B[v]_B$ for every $v\in V$. [4]
  12. 4(c) Define an idempotent linear operator. Show that $T:V\to V$ is idempotent if and only if $T^2=T$. [3]
  13. 5(a) Find eigen values of the linear transformation $T:\mathbb{R}^3\to\mathbb{R}^3$ defined by $T(x,y,z)=(2x+y,\,y-z,\,2y+4z)$. Also find the eigen space $E_2$. [3]
  14. 5(b) State and prove the Cayley-Hamilton theorem for linear operators. [4]
  15. 5(c) Let $T:\mathbb{R}^2\to\mathbb{R}^2$ be a linear transformation. Show that either $\mathbb{R}^2$ is a $T$-cyclic space or $T=cI$ for some $c\in\mathbb{R}$. [3]
  16. 6(a) Find the dual basis of the basis $\{(1,-1,3),(0,1,-1),(0,3,-2)\}$ of $\mathbb{R}^3$. [3]
  17. 6(b) Let $W$ be a subspace of $V$. Then show that $\dim V=\dim W+\dim W^{o}$. [4]
  18. 6(c) Let $T:U\to V$ be a linear transformation. Then show that $\operatorname{Ker}T^{t}=(\operatorname{Im}T)^{o}$. [3]
