MMC-13

Linear Algebra
M.Sc. Mathematics · Semester I2023
  1. 1(a) Let $A\in M_2(\mathbb R)$ be an orthogonal matrix with $\det A=-1$. Find the eigen values of $A$. Also find the general form of such matrices $A$. [2+2]
  2. 1(b) Let $T$ be a linear operator on $V$ with an eigen value $\lambda$. Show that the geometric multiplicity of $\lambda$ can not exceed its algebraic multiplicity. [3]
  3. 1(c) State and prove the Cayley-Hamilton theorem for linear operators. [3]
  4. 2(a) Let $T$ be a linear operator on $V$. Show that there is the unique monic polynomial $m(t)\in F[t]$ of least degree such that $m(T)=T_0$. [3]
  5. 2(b) Give an example of two non similar matrices which have the same minimal and characteristic polynomials. [2]
  6. 2(c) Let $A\in M_n(F)$ be a nilpotent matrix. Then show that $A^n=O$. If index of nilpotence of $A$ is $n$, then show that there can not be a matrix $B\in M_n(F)$ such that $B^2=A$. [2]
  7. 2(d) Let $A,B\in M_n(F)$ and $f(t)=\det(tI_n-B)$. Show that $f(A)$ is invertible if and only if $A$ and $B$ have no common eigen values. [3]
  8. 3(a) Show that a linear operator $T$ is diagonalizable if and only if the minimal polynomial of $T$ is a product of distinct linear factors. [4]
  9. 3(b) Let $J=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&1\\0&1&0\\1&0&0\end{pmatrix}$. Show that $J$ is diagonalizable over $\mathbb R$. Find a basis of $\mathbb R^3$ of eigen vectors of $J$. [3]
  10. 3(c) Let $M\in M_n(\mathbb R)$ and $T:M_n(\mathbb R)\longrightarrow M_n(\mathbb R)$ be defined by $T(A)=MA$. If $M$ is diagonalizable then show that $T$ is diagonalizable. Does the converse hold. [3]
  11. 4(a) Show that a linear operator $T:V\longrightarrow V$ is triangulizable if and only if the characteristic polynomial of $T$ splits into linear factors. [4]
  12. 4(b) Let $\lambda$ be an eigen value of an invertible linear operator $T$. Show that $G_\lambda(T)=G_{\lambda^{-1}}(T^{-1})$. [3]
  13. 4(c) Let $A$ be a $4\times4$ nilpotent matrix. Find all possible Jordan canonical forms of $A$, and the index of nilpotence and geometric multiplicity of 0 corresponding to each form. [3]
  14. 5(a) State and prove the Riesz representation theorem. [3]
  15. 5(b) Let $T$ be a linear operator on $\mathbb R^2$ that represents the counterclockwise rotation at an angle $\theta$. Show that $T_\theta^*=T_{-\theta}$. [2]
  16. 5(c) If $f:P_2(\mathbb R)\longrightarrow\mathbb R$ is defined by $f(p)=p(1)+p'(2)$, find a vector $q\in P_2(\mathbb R)$ such that $f(p)=\langle p,q\rangle$ for all $p\in P_2(\mathbb R)$, where $\langle p,q\rangle=\int_0^1p(x)q(x)dx$. [3]
  17. 5(d) Let $T:\mathbb R^3\longrightarrow \mathbb R^3$ be a linear operator. Show that $\ker T^*=(\mathit{Im}\,T)^\perp$. [2]
  18. 6(a) Let $U$ be a complex inner product space and $T:U\longrightarrow U$ be a linear operator. Prove that $T$ is a normal operator if and only if it is unitarily diagonalizable. [4]
  19. 6(b) Let $V$ be an inner product space and $P:V\longrightarrow V$ be an idempotent linear operator. Show that $P$ is self adjoint if and only if $PP^*=P^*P$. [3]
  20. 6(c) Prove that a linear operator $T$ on an inner product space $V$ is unitary if and only if $\|T(v)\|=\|v\|$ for all $v\in V$. \endgroup [3]
