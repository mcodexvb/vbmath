Internal test

M.Sc. Mathematics · Semester III2023
  1. Q1(a) What is meant by an argument? When is an argument said to be valid? Examine the validity of the argument \[\begin{array}{ll} P_1:&(A\to B)\land(C\to D)\\ P_2:&(A\to\sim D)\land(C\to\sim B)\\ P_3:&A\lor C\\ \hline \therefore G:& B\leftrightarrow\sim D \end{array}\]
  2. Q1(b) Construct a formal proof of validity of the argument \[\begin{array}{ll} P_1:&(A\lor B)\to(C\land D)\\ P_2:&(C\lor E)\to(\sim F\land G)\\ P_3:&(F\lor H)\to(A\land I)\\ \hline \therefore G:&\sim F \end{array}\]
  3. Q1(c) Examine the validity of the argument \[\begin{array}{ll} P_1:&X\leftrightarrow(Y\to Z)\\ P_2:&Y\leftrightarrow(\sim X\land\sim Z)\\ P_3:&Z\leftrightarrow(X\lor\sim Y)\\ P_4:&Y\\ \hline \therefore G:&X\lor Z \end{array}\]
  4. Q1(d) Symbolize\par If I work, then I earn money, but if I am idle, then I enjoy myself. Either I work or I am idle. However, if I work, then I do not enjoy myself, while if I am idle, then I do not earn money. Therefore, I enjoy myself if and only if I do not earn money.
  5. Q2(a) Show that a connected graph $T$ is a tree if and only if it is maximally acyclic.
  6. Q2(b) Show that every graph $G$ with at least one edge has a subgraph $H$ with $\delta(H)>\epsilon(H)\ge\epsilon(G)$.
  7. Q2(c) Show that every graph $G$ contain a path of length at lea\textit{[cropped]} $\delta(G)$ and a cycle of length at least $\delta(G)+1$ ($\delta(G)\ge2$).
  8. Q2(d) Design an algorithm for finding a spanning tree for a graph $G$. \par\emph{[Individual question marks are absent from the scan.]}
  9. 1 Count the number of paths of length $n$ in the $xy$-plane starting from $(0,0)$ with steps \[\begin{aligned} R &: (x,y)\longmapsto(x+1,y);\\ L &: (x,y)\longmapsto(x-1,y)\text{ and}\\ U &: (x,y)\longmapsto(x,y+1). \end{aligned}\] It is given that a step $R$ is not to be followed by a step $L$ and vice-versa. [[7]]
  10. 2 Count the number of integer solutions of the system \[\begin{aligned} x+y+z&=16\\ \text{Subject to}\qquad 1&\le x\le5\\ 2&\le y\le6\\ -1&\le z\le7. \end{aligned}\] [[7]]
  11. 3 Numbers 1 to 10 are arranged in random order around a circle. Show that there are three consecutive numbers whose sum is atleast 17. [[6]]
  12. 1 Derive equation of continuity by Eulerian and Lagrangian methods for general fluid motion.
  13. 2 Find the condition that the surface $f(x,y,z,t)=c$, a constant, may be a boundary surface of a fluid motion. Show that $\frac{x^2}{a^2}\cos^2 t+\frac{y^2}{b^2}\sec^2 t=1$ is a possible form of the boundary surface of a fluid flow.
  14. 3 Establish the relation between the angular velocity vector and vorticity vector. Find the stream lines and path lines of the particles when $u=2x/(1+t)$, $v=-y/(1+t)$, $w=-z/(1+t)$.
  15. 4 State and prove the Milne-Thomson circle theorem. Also determine image system for a source outside a circle of radius $a$ with the help of the circle theorem.
  16. 5 State and prove the theorem of Blasius.
  17. 6 Two sources, each of strength $m$ are placed at the points $(-a,0)$, $(a,0)$ and a sink of strength $2m$ at the origin. Show that the stream lines are the curves $(x^2+y^2)^2=a^2(x^2-y^2+\lambda xy)$ where $\lambda$ is a variable parameter. Show also that the fluid speed at any point is $2ma^2/(r_1r_2r_3)$ where $r_1,r_2,r_3$ are the distances of the points from the sources and the sink.
