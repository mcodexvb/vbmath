CCMA-13

Analysis VI
B.Sc. Mathematics · Semester VI2023
  1. 1(a) If $f:\mathbb C\longrightarrow\mathbb C$ is a function such that $\lim_{z\to z_0}f(z)=\bar w$, where $w\in\mathbb C$, then show that $\lim_{z\to z_0}\overline{f(z)}=w$. [3]
  2. 1(b) If a continuous function $f:D\longrightarrow\mathbb C$, defined on a domain $D$, takes only the real integral values, show that $f$ is constant. [4]
  3. 1(c) Derive Cauchy-Riemann equations in complex form. [3]
  4. 2(a) Examine if $u(x,y)=y+e^x\cos y$ is a harmonic function in $\mathbb C$. If possible, find its harmonic conjugate and then construct an analytic function with $u(x,y)$ as its real part. [4]
  5. 2(b) Discuss the convergence of the series $\displaystyle\sum_1^\infty n(1+i)(2i)^{-n}$. [3]
  6. 2(c) Let $f:\mathbb C\longrightarrow L$ be analytic, where $L$ is the line $x+2y=1$. Prove or disprove: $f$ is bounded in $\mathbb C$. [3]
  7. 3(a) Find all the Möbius transformations of the unit disc $|z|\le1$ onto the right half-plane $Re(w)\ge0$. [5]
  8. 3(b) Evaluate $\displaystyle\int_\Gamma(\bar z\,dz+z\,d\bar z)$ along the curve $\Gamma$ defined by $z^2-2z\bar z+(\bar z)^2=(1+i)z+(1-i)\bar z$ from the point $z=-1+i$ to the point $z=-6+2i$. [5]
  9. 4(a) State and prove Cauchy's integral formula. [4]
  10. 4(b) Describe the orientation principle related to Möbius transformations. Hence find the image of the exterior of the circle $|z+2|=2$ under the map $w(z)=\frac{z}{2z+8}$. [1+3]
  11. 4(c) What do you mean by the cross ratio of four complex numbers. Explain how it is related to Möbius transformations. [1+1]
  12. 5(a) Using the techniques of Complex Analysis, establish the fundamental theorem of Algebra. [4]
  13. 5(b) If $f$ is an entire function satisfying $f(z)=f(z+z_1)=f(z+z_2)$, $\forall z\in\mathbb C$, where $z_1$ and $z_2$ are two non-zero complex numbers such that $\frac{z_1}{z_2}\notin\mathbb R$, show that $f$ is constant. [4]
  14. 5(c) Evaluate $\displaystyle\int_\Gamma\bar z\,dz$, where $\Gamma$ is the right-half of the circle $|z|=1$ from $z=-i$ to $z=i$. [2]
  15. 6(a) Define a metric space. Give an example of it with proper justifications. [2+1+2]
  16. 6(b) Let $X$ be a non empty set and $\rho$ be a real valued function of ordered pairs of elements of $X$ which satisfies the following two conditions: \par $\rho(x,y)=0$ if and only if $x=y$ and $\rho(x,y)\le\rho(x,z)+\rho(y,z)$. Show that $\rho(x,y)$ is a metric on $X$. [3]
  17. 6(c) Let $R$ be the set of all real numbers and for $x,y$ in $R$, let $\rho(x,y)=|x^4-y^4|$. Show that $(X,\rho)$ is not a metric space. [2]
  18. 7(a) Define a convergent sequence in a metric space $(X,\rho)$. Show that in a metric space $(X,\rho)$, if $x_n\to x_0$ and $y_n\to y_0$ as $n\to\infty$ then $\rho(x_n,y_n)\to\rho(x_0,y_0)$. [1+2]
  19. 7(b) Show that necessary and sufficient condition that a sequence $\{x_n\}$ of points of $X$ converges to $x\in X$ is that every neighbourhood $S(x,a)$ of $x$ contains all the points of the sequence except perhaps a finitely many. [2+2]
  20. 7(c) Is the arbitrary intersection of open sets open in a metric space? Answer with reason. [1+2]
  21. 8(a) Define the closure $\bar A$ of a set $A\subset X$ in a metric space $(X,\rho)$. Prove that a point $p\in\bar A$ if and only if $S\cap A\ne\emptyset$ for every neighbourhood $S$ of $p$. [1+2+2]
  22. 8(b) If $A,B$ are subsets of $X$ in a metric space $(X,\rho)$, then show that \par (i) $A\subset\bar A$, (ii) $A\subset B$ implies $\bar A\subset\bar B$, (iii) $\overline{A\cup B}=\bar A\cup\bar B$. [1+2+2]
  23. 9(a) Give an example with proper justification of a Cauchy sequence in a metric space. [2]
  24. 9(b) Prove that a Cauchy sequence, which has a convergent subsequence is itself convergent. [3]
  25. 9(c) Define a complete metric space. Prove that $C[a,b]$ is a complete metric space. [1+4]
  26. 10(a) Let $X$ be a metric space and $Y$ be a subset of $X$. Then prove that $Y$, considered as a metric space is closed, if it is complete. [1+3]
  27. 10(b) State and prove Banach's contraction mapping principle. [6]
