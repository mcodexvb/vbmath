GATE Mathematics · Mathematics (MA), 2012

  1. 1 The straight lines $L_1 : x = 0$, $L_2 : y = 0$, and $L_3 : x + y = 1$ are mapped by the transformation $w = z^2$ into the curves $C_1, C_2$, and $C_3$ respectively. The angle of intersection between the curves at $w = 0$ is \begin{enumerate}[label=(\Alph*)] \item $0$ \item $\pi/4$ \item $\pi/2$ \item $\pi$ \end{enumerate}
  2. 2 In a topological space, which of the following statements is NOT always true: \begin{enumerate}[label=(\Alph*)] \item Union of any finite family of compact sets is compact. \item Union of any family of closed sets is closed. \item Union of any family of connected sets having a non-empty intersection is connected. \item Union of any family of dense subsets is dense. \end{enumerate}
  3. 3 Consider the following statements:\\ $P$: The family of subsets $\left\{A_n = \left(-\dfrac{1}{n}, \dfrac{1}{n}\right),\ n = 1, 2, \dots\right\}$ satisfies the finite intersection property.\\ $Q$: On an infinite set $X$, a metric $d : X \times X \to \mathbb{R}$ is defined as $d(x, y) = \begin{cases} 0, & x = y, \\ 1, & x \ne y. \end{cases}$ The metric space $(X, d)$ is compact.\\ $R$: In a Fr\'echet ($T_1$) topological space, every finite set is closed.\\ $S$: If $f : \mathbb{R} \to X$ is continuous, where $\mathbb{R}$ is given the usual topology and $(X, \tau)$ is a Hausdorff ($T_2$) space, then $f$ is a one-one function.\\ Which of the above statements are correct? \begin{enumerate}[label=(\Alph*)] \item $P$ and $R$ \item $P$ and $S$ \item $R$ and $S$ \item $Q$ and $S$ \end{enumerate}
  4. 4 Let $H$ be a Hilbert space and $S^\perp$ denote the orthogonal complement of a set $S \subseteq H$. Which of the following is INCORRECT? \begin{enumerate}[label=(\Alph*)] \item For $S_1, S_2 \subseteq H$; $S_1 \subseteq S_2 \Rightarrow S_1^\perp \subseteq S_2^\perp$ \item $S \subseteq (S^\perp)^\perp$ \item $\{0\}^\perp = H$ \item $S^\perp$ is always closed \end{enumerate}
  5. 5 Let $H$ be a complex Hilbert space, $T : H \to H$ be a bounded linear operator, and let $T^*$ denote the adjoint of $T$. Which of the following statements are always TRUE?\\ $P$: $\forall x, y \in H,\ \langle Tx, y \rangle = \langle x, T^* y \rangle$\\ $Q$: $\forall x, y \in H,\ \langle x, Ty \rangle = \langle T^* x, y \rangle$\\ $R$: $\forall x, y \in H,\ \langle x, Ty \rangle = \langle x, T^* y \rangle$\\ $S$: $\forall x, y \in H,\ \langle Tx, Ty \rangle = \langle T^* x, T^* y \rangle$ \begin{enumerate}[label=(\Alph*)] \item $P$ and $Q$ \item $P$ and $R$ \item $Q$ and $S$ \item $P$ and $S$ \end{enumerate}
  6. 6 Let $X = \{a, b, c\}$ and let $\mathcal{T} = \{\phi, \{a\}, \{b\}, \{a, b\}, X\}$ be a topology defined on $X$. Then which of the following statements are TRUE?\\ $P$: $(X, \mathcal{T})$ is a Hausdorff space. \qquad $Q$: $(X, \mathcal{T})$ is a regular space.\\ $R$: $(X, \mathcal{T})$ is a normal space. \qquad $S$: $(X, \mathcal{T})$ is a connected space. \begin{enumerate}[label=(\Alph*)] \item $P$ and $Q$ \item $Q$ and $R$ \item $R$ and $S$ \item $P$ and $S$ \end{enumerate}
  7. 7 Consider the statements:\\ $P$: If $X$ is a normed linear space and $M \subseteq X$ is a subspace, then the closure $\overline{M}$ is also a subspace of $X$.\\ $Q$: If $X$ is a Banach space and $\sum x_n$ is an absolutely convergent series in $X$, then $\sum x_n$ is convergent.\\ $R$: Let $M_1$ and $M_2$ be subspaces of an inner product space such that $M_1 \cap M_2 = \{0\}$. Then $\forall m_1 \in M_1,\ m_2 \in M_2;\ \|m_1 + m_2\|^2 = \|m_1\|^2 + \|m_2\|^2$.\\ $S$: Let $f : X \to Y$ be a linear transformation from the Banach space $X$ into the Banach space $Y$. If $f$ is continuous, then the graph of $f$ is always compact.\\ The correct statements amongst the above are: \begin{enumerate}[label=(\Alph*)] \item $P$ and $R$ only \item $Q$ and $R$ only \item $P$ and $Q$ only \item $R$ and $S$ only \end{enumerate}
  8. 8 A continuous random variable $X$ has the probability density function \[ f(x) = \begin{cases} \dfrac{3}{5}e^{-\frac{3}{5}x}, & x > 0, \\ 0, & x \le 0. \end{cases} \] The probability density function of $Y = 3X + 2$ is \begin{enumerate}[label=(\Alph*)] \item $f(y) = \begin{cases} \dfrac{1}{5}e^{-\frac{1}{5}(y-2)}, & y > 2 \\ 0, & y \le 2 \end{cases}$ \item $f(y) = \begin{cases} \dfrac{2}{5}e^{-\frac{2}{5}(y-2)}, & y > 2 \\ 0, & y \le 2 \end{cases}$ \item $f(y) = \begin{cases} \dfrac{3}{5}e^{-\frac{3}{5}(y-2)}, & y > 2 \\ 0, & y \le 2 \end{cases}$ \item $f(y) = \begin{cases} \dfrac{4}{5}e^{-\frac{4}{5}(y-2)}, & y > 2 \\ 0, & y \le 2 \end{cases}$ \end{enumerate}
  9. 9 A simple random sample of size $10$ from $N(\mu, \sigma^2)$ gives $98\%$ confidence interval $(20.49, 23.51)$.   10. 10 For the linear programming problem: \begin{align*} \text{Maximize } & z = x_1 + 2x_2 + 3x_3 - 4x_4 \\ \text{Subject to } & 2x_1 + 3x_2 - x_3 - x_4 = 15, \\ & 6x_1 + x_2 + x_3 - 3x_4 = 21, \\ & 8x_1 + 2x_2 + 3x_3 - 4x_4 = 30, \\ & x_1, x_2, x_3, x_4 \ge 0, \end{align*} $x_1 = 4$, $x_2 = 3$, $x_3 = 0$, $x_4 = 2$ is \begin{enumerate}[label=(\Alph*)] \item an optimal solution \item a degenerate basic feasible solution \item a non-degenerate basic feasible solution \item a non-basic feasible solution \end{enumerate}
